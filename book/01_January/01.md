January 1st - Control and Focus

“The chief task in life is simply this: to identify and separate matters so that I can say clearly to myself which are externals not under my control, and which have to do with the choices I actually control. Where do I look for good and evil? Not to uncontrollable externals, but within myself to the choices that are my own...” 

—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.5.4-5

In the ever-shifting landscape of software development, the Stoic practice of distinguishing between what we can change and what we can't is crucial. Unlike the physical world, where we are often powerless against natural forces, the digital realm offers a different kind of challenge – one that tests our internal resolve and focus.

Consider this: you're faced with a legacy codebase, riddled with undocumented changes and complexities. It's easy to feel frustrated and blame external factors – the developers who wrote it, the lack of documentation, or the time constraints. But Stoicism teaches us to redirect our energy from frustration to action. While we can't change the past or the existing code, we can control how we approach the problem – with patience, systematic thinking, and a willingness to learn and adapt.

Each time you encounter a setback – be it a technical glitch, a team dynamic issue, or a personal hurdle – remember Epictetus's words. Ask yourself, "What aspect of this situation is within my control?" By focusing on this, you conserve your energy for what truly matters and what you can influence. This practice not only makes you a more effective developer but also a more resilient and adaptable individual.
