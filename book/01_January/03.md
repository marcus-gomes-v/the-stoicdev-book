## January 3rd - The Art of Saying 'No'

***“How many people have without your knowledge stolen parts of your life from you, how much you have lost, how large a part has been taken up by useless grief, foolish gladness, greedy desire, or polite conversation; how little of yourself is left to you: you will then perceive that you will die prematurely.”***
—SENECA, ON THE BREVITY OF LIFE, 3.3

Open last week’s calendar. Count the meetings where you said nothing and nobody needed you there. Add the “quick question” that ate an afternoon, the thread where you argued for an hour about a lint rule, and the evening you spent replaying one code review comment in your head.

Seneca’s list is two thousand years old and it still fits. Grief over things that can’t be changed, small wins celebrated too long, wanting what the next team has, polite talk that goes nowhere. None of it is wicked. It just isn’t yours, and it adds up to a surprising share of your life.

Saying no is how you take some of it back. Decline the meeting that could have been a message. Mute the thread you’re only reading to stay annoyed. Tell the colleague you’ll look at their problem at four, after you finish what you sat down to do. Most people respect that more than you expect, and the hours you keep are the same ones you were about to complain about losing.
