## January 4th - THE BIG THREE

***“Thy present opinion founded on understanding, and thy present conduct directed to social good, and thy present disposition of contentment with everything which happens—that is enough.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 9.6

Marcus keeps coming back to three things, and here he fits them in one sentence: how you see what’s in front of you, what you do about it, and how you take whatever happens that you can’t change. Modern writers on Stoicism often call them perception, action and will. He also repeats a word that’s easy to skip. Present. Each of the three is about now, not the sprint after next.

Take an ordinary bad morning. The build is red, a reviewer left twenty comments on your pull request, and product just moved the deadline up a week. Start with perception. The red build is a failing test, not proof that you’re bad at your job. The twenty comments are twenty signs that someone read your code carefully. See the thing as it is before you decide how to feel about it.

Then action. Fix the test. Accept the comments you agree with and push back, calmly, on the two you don’t. Spend the day on work that helps the team, which is not always the work that makes you look busy.

Then will. The deadline moved. You can say clearly what fits in the new date and what doesn’t, and after that the decision belongs to someone else. Accept it and get back to work. Marcus says that is enough, and on most days it really is.
