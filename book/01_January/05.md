## January 5th - UNDERSTAND YOUR GOALS

***“Let all your work, therefore, have some purpose, and keep some object in view: these restless people are not made restless by labour, but are driven out of their minds by mistaken ideas.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 12.5

Seneca is describing people who rush around Rome all day. They leave the house without knowing where they’re going, follow whoever they bump into, and come home exhausted, unable to say where they’ve been. The next morning they do it again. The work, he says, isn’t what wears them out. What wears them out is never knowing what it’s for.

Most teams have had a sprint like that. Tickets move across the board, standups are full of updates, the burndown chart looks healthy. Two weeks later someone asks what the release actually changed for users, and nobody has a clear answer.

It usually goes wrong at the start, when a task arrives and you begin building before you know what done looks like. The stakeholder asked for “a dashboard.” Did they mean a page their boss glances at once a month, or a tool the support team will live in eight hours a day? Those are different projects with different deadlines. Before you write any code, ask what problem this solves and how everyone will know it worked.

Then write the answer down somewhere you’ll keep seeing it, at the top of the ticket or the design doc. Later in the week you’ll catch yourself polishing a component nobody asked for, or starting a refactor that can wait. That sentence at the top is what pulls you back to the actual goal.
