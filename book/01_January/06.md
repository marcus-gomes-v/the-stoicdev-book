## January 6th - Discovering Your True North in Tech

***“He who does not know what the world is, does not know where he is. And he who does not know for what purpose the world exists, does not know who he is, nor what the world is. But he who has failed in any one of these things could not even say for what purpose he exists himself. What then dost thou think of him who [avoids or] seeks the praise of those who applaud, of men who know not either where they are or who they are?”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.52

There’s a point in many careers, often somewhere around year five or six, when the next step stops being obvious. You could go for staff engineer, or move into management because that’s what people seem to do at this stage. A friend keeps texting you about a startup. Everyone has advice, and most of it is about what will look good from the outside. My own path went from writing code to leading technical teams, and then to a sabbatical I took to work this out.

Marcus asks a plainer question first. Do you know where you are, and what you’re for? If you don’t, he says, you can’t really say who you are either. And then it makes little sense to steer by the applause of people who don’t know those things about themselves.

A lot of career noise is exactly that applause. The recruiter who hasn’t read your profile but is sure this role is perfect for you. The colleague who sizes everyone up by their title. Neither of them can tell you what kind of work makes you lose track of time.

You can find that out, though not in the middle of a release week. Take a quiet hour and look back over the last year. Which days did you end tired but glad? Maybe it was the week you untangled a nasty production bug, or the afternoon you sat with a customer until you finally understood what they needed. Write those days down. The pattern in them is your true north, and it’s a far better guide for your next step than anyone’s applause.
