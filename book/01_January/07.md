## January 7th - Mastering the Mind: A Developer's Guide

***“And the functions of a soul are the exercise of choice, of refusal, of desire, of aversion, of preparation, of purpose, and of assent. What, then, can that be which makes the soul dirty and unclean in these functions? Nothing but its erroneous decisions.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.11.6-7

These lines come from a talk Epictetus gave about cleanliness. He spends a good part of it on bodies and baths, and then turns the subject around. The dirt that matters most is in the mind, and it only gets there one way: through bad decisions.

First he lists what the mind does all day. It chooses and refuses. It wants and avoids. It prepares, it sets a purpose, and it agrees that something is true. Seven jobs, and all of them are running while you work.

Picture an ordinary afternoon. You choose to take the ticket nobody wants, or you refuse it. You want the clean rewrite and avoid the awkward conversation about scope. You prepare for tomorrow’s demo, or tell yourself you’ll wing it. You decide what this week is really for. And many times an hour you assent to something without noticing: this error must be the network, this reviewer has it in for me, this estimate is probably fine.

That last job does the most damage. A codebase rarely rots because of one big mistake. It rots through a hundred small decisions that seemed reasonable at the time and were never checked. The mind works the same way. Nothing outside you can make it dirty. Only a judgment you accepted without looking at it closely can do that. So look closely. Before you agree with a thought, especially an angry or a convenient one, ask whether it’s actually true.
