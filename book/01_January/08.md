January 8th - Breaking Free from Habitual Vices in Development

“We must give up many things to which we are addicted, considering them to be good. Otherwise, courage will vanish, which should continually test itself. Greatness of soul will be lost, which can’t stand out unless it disdains as petty what the mob regards as most desirable.”

—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 74.12b-13

In the realm of software development, certain habitual vices, though seemingly innocuous, can subtly erode our effectiveness and enthusiasm. These habits often begin as minor grievances or routine shortcuts but can quickly solidify into patterns that limit our potential.

Consider the ease with which we slip into cycles of criticism over code quality, or how effortlessly we let the absence of perfect documentation become a barrier to progress. Think about the times we've allowed the comfort of brainstorming and ideation to overshadow the hard work of execution, or how we sometimes settle into the mediocrity of doing just enough, eschewing the challenges that spur growth.

These behaviors, while offering short-term comfort or escape, ultimately cloud our clarity and self-control. They represent a loss of freedom – the freedom to engage creatively and proactively with our work, the freedom to grow beyond our current capabilities.

The journey of a developer is as much about coding as it is about overcoming these internal challenges. It's about recognizing these addictive patterns and consciously stepping away from them. It involves embracing the discomfort of growth, the uncertainty of uncharted territories in technology, and the vulnerability of exposing our ideas to scrutiny and collaboration.

In shedding these habitual vices, we open ourselves to a world of creativity and excellence. We move beyond the average, embracing a journey of continuous learning and improvement. This is the path to not just being a developer, but being a developer with self control, and a constantly evolving skill set.
