## January 8th - Breaking Free from Habitual Vices in Development

***“Let go of this view, and neither devotion nor loyalty will hold, since whoever wants to show them must put up with much of what is called evil and give up much of what we indulge in as if it were good. Courage dies, which ought to keep putting itself to the test. Greatness of soul dies, which cannot stand out unless it has scorned as trifles everything the crowd longs for as the greatest.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 74.12-13

The view Seneca means is the Stoic one he has just been defending: that the only real good is acting well, and that everything else (comfort, money, approval) can be useful but isn’t good in itself. Drop that idea, he warns, and you’ll find you can’t keep your loyalty or your nerve, because both of them cost things you’ve learned to treat as precious.

Developers have their own list of those things. Being liked in planning meetings. Being the person who always says yes. The comfort of the module you know by heart, and the quiet relief of never opening the one that scares you. A spotless record of no incidents on your watch, kept spotless by never shipping anything risky.

None of these is terrible on its own, which is exactly how they turn into habits. Watch what happens once they’re treated as goods. You let a bad estimate stand because correcting it would make the meeting awkward. You approve a pull request you didn’t really read, because the author is senior and you’d like to stay on their good side. You stay on the old stack for one more year, then another, because learning something new would mean looking slow for a while.

Each time, a little courage goes, the kind Seneca says has to keep testing itself to stay alive. His greatness of soul is mostly about caring less for what everyone around you is chasing, so you’re free to do the right thing when it costs you. Pick one of these comforts and give it up for a week. Read the whole diff, or say the honest number out loud in planning. It will cost you a little, and you’ll get some of your nerve back in exchange.
