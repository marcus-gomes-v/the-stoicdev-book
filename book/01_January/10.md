January 10th - If You Want to Be Steady

"The essence of good is a certain kind of reasoned choice; just as the essence of evil is another kind. What about externals, then? They are only the raw material for our reasoned choice, which finds its own good or evil in working with them. How will it find the good? Not by marveling at the material! For if judgments about the material are straight that makes our choices good, but if those judgments are twisted, our choices turn bad."
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 1.29.1-3

In the whirlwind of technology debates and trends, it's essential for a StoicDev to focus on reasoned choices rather than external noise. The ongoing discussions about which programming language or framework is superior are mere distractions. True steadiness in software development comes from clear understanding and deliberate action, not from following the crowd.

Epictetus emphasizes the importance of internal clarity over external validation. As a developer, your real challenge isn't in choosing the 'right' tool, but in making decisions that align with your goals and values. The key to stability in your profession lies in filtering out the noise, focusing on what brings your projects from conception to completion, and understanding the steps involved in this journey.

Your success as a developer is defined by the quality of your judgments and decisions, not by the opinions of others. By maintaining clarity about your objectives and the reasoning behind your choices, you find steadiness amid the chaos. This approach is not about isolating yourself from external influences, but rather about discerning which voices and trends to heed and which to set aside. In the realm of coding and technology, a clear mind and a steadfast approach are invaluable assets in navigating a path marked by constant change and varied opinions.
