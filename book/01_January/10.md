## January 10th - If You Want to Be Steady

***“The essence of the good is a certain kind of moral purpose, and that of the evil is a certain kind of moral purpose. What, then, are the external things? They are materials for the moral purpose, in dealing with which it will find its own proper good or evil. How will it find the good? If it does not admire the materials. For the judgements about the materials, if they be correct, make the moral purpose good, but if they be crooked and awry, they make it evil.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 1.29.1-3

The chapter this comes from is called “Of steadfastness,” and Epictetus opens it by putting good and bad somewhere unexpected: in how we decide about things, rather than in the things themselves. Everything outside us is material, the way wood is material for a carpenter.

Developers argue about materials all the time. Rust or Go. Monolith or microservices. The new frontend framework that one conference talk made look like the end of all our problems. It’s easy to believe that good software comes from picking the right tool, and to feel shaken every time a benchmark or a blog post suggests you picked wrong.

Epictetus would say you’ve started admiring the materials. A language is wood. A framework is wood. Plenty of teams have built something solid on a boring stack, and plenty have built a mess on the fashionable one. What made the difference was judgment: whether people understood the problem, kept things simple where they could, and were honest about the trade-offs they were making.

That’s where steadiness comes from. Once you’ve thought a decision through properly, a hype cycle can’t move you very far. You can still change your mind when there’s a good reason. You just won’t change it because the internet got excited on a Tuesday.
