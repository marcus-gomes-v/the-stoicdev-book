## January 11th - If You Want to Be Unsteady

***“For if a man should transfer his caution to the sphere of the moral purpose and the deeds of the moral purpose, then along with the desire to be cautious he will also at once have under his control the will to avoid; whereas, if he should transfer his caution to those matters which are not under our control and lie outside the province of the moral purpose, inasmuch as he is applying his will to avoid towards those things which are under the control of others, he will necessarily be subject to fear, instability, and perturbation.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.1.12

Some developers are very careful about the wrong things. They worry about which stack will look best on a résumé in two years, whether the next reorg will put them on the exciting team, whether anyone noticed their name in the release notes. All of that sits in other people’s hands. So they’re anxious most of the time, and they keep jumping from one shiny greenfield project to the next.

Epictetus says to move the caution. Be careful about your own decisions instead: whether you understood the bug before you patched it, whether you cut a corner you’ll regret next quarter. That kind of caution actually works, because what you’re guarding is yours.

The developer who has kept a ten-year-old billing system running without drama usually knows this already. Nobody writes posts about their work. They sleep fine.
