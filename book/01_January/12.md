## January 12th - The One Path to Serenity

***“There is but one way to serenity (keep this thought ready for use at dawn, and by day, and at night), and that is to yield up all claim to the things that lie outside the sphere of the moral purpose, to regard nothing as your own possession; to surrender everything to the Deity, to Fortune...”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.4.39

Epictetus tells you when to use this thought: at dawn, during the day, and at night. So try it on a whole day. In the morning, before you open Slack, think through what’s ahead and notice which parts aren’t yours. The flaky CI runner. The client who changes their mind every Wednesday. Whether the release ships on time depends on a dozen people besides you.

Around lunch, check what you do own: your attention, your judgment, the quality of whatever you hand over. It’s a short list, and nobody can take it from you. In the afternoon something will go sideways. A dependency breaks, or a meeting runs an hour long. Let it. You were never holding it in the first place.

At night, close the laptop and hand the rest over to what Epictetus called the Deity and Fortune. Sleep is good practice for that. You stop steering for eight hours and the world keeps turning without you. Then you do it again tomorrow.
