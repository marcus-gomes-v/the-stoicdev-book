## January 13th - Circle of Control

***“Under our control are moral purpose and all the acts of moral purpose; but not under our control are the body, the parts of the body, possessions, parents, brothers, children, country—in a word, all that with which we associate.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 1.22.10

Read that list slowly. The body. Possessions. Parents, brothers, children, country. Epictetus isn’t saying these don’t matter. He’s saying they don’t obey you. You can love your kids completely and still have no say in whether they sleep tonight.

Work has its own version of the list. Your company’s roadmap. The cloud region that went down this morning. Your team’s headcount, the new VP who wants everything rewritten, the open-source library whose maintainer just archived the repo. You’re tied to all of them, and none of them takes orders from you.

What does take orders from you is small: what you decide, and what you do because of it. You decide whether to answer the rewrite proposal with a calm written argument or a week of sulking. You decide whether the outage turns into a blameless postmortem or a search for someone to blame. You decide whether to fork the dead library or migrate off it.

That circle is small, but it’s complete. Nothing outside it can reach in and make the choice for you. When the week gets loud, step back inside it and work from there.
