## January 14th - Mastering the Mind Amid Digital Tempests

***“Perceive at last that thou hast in thee something better and more divine than the things which cause the various affects, and as it were pull thee by the strings. What is there now in my mind—is it fear, or suspicion, or desire, or anything of the kind?”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 12.19

Puppets show up again and again in the Meditations. Marcus pictures a person jerked around on strings, and the strings are their own passing feelings. Here he asks the question that shows you where the strings are attached: what is in my mind right now?

Ask it the next time you notice your hand reaching for your phone in the middle of a function. There’s usually an answer. Fear: the problem is hard, and checking Slack is a way not to feel stuck. Suspicion: someone commented on your pull request and you want to know whether they’re criticizing you. Desire: a new tool launched this morning and you’d like to be the first on the team to try it.

None of these feelings makes you a bad developer. They’re just strings, and notifications and feeds are built by very smart people to find them and pull. If you don’t notice what you’re feeling, the pull wins and twenty minutes disappear.

Marcus says there’s something in you better than all of that: the part that can notice the feeling, give it a name and decide what to do with it. You don’t have to fight every distraction. You only need to catch the one happening now, say “that’s fear” or “that’s envy,” and choose whether to follow it. Most of the time you won’t. Close the tab and go back to the function.
