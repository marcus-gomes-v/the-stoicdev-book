## January 15th - The Developer's Path to Clarity and Conviction

***“Tranquility comes only to those who have reached a judgment that is fixed and certain. The rest keep slipping and being set back on their feet, wavering by turns between what they have let go and what they go after. Why all this tossing about? Because nothing is clear to people who steer by the most unreliable guide of all: what others say.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 95.57-58

You can watch this happen to a team. In January the plan is to move everything to a new framework, because a well-known company wrote a blog post about it. By March half the code is migrated, and someone shares a thread saying that framework is already dead. In June there’s a proposal to go back. Every change felt urgent at the time. None of them came from a decision anyone had actually reasoned through.

Seneca calls this being tossed about. You drop something, pick it up again, drop it again, and you never get any peace, because what’s steering you is whatever people are saying this month. That is the least reliable guide there is. It changes direction every time someone with a big audience has a bad week.

What helps is doing the slow work once, early. When you choose a tool, write down why: the problem it solves for you, the trade-offs you accepted, and what would have to change for you to reconsider. A short decision record in the repo is enough.

Then, when the next wave of opinion arrives, you have something to check it against. If the conditions you wrote down have changed, change course, calmly and for a reason you can explain. If they haven’t, you can read the thread with interest, close it, and go back to work. That is the settled judgment Seneca is talking about, and it’s what lets you stay calm while the opinions keep changing around you.
