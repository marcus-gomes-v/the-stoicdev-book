## January 16th - Rethinking Routine: The Developer's Guide to Intentional Action

***“Much the same happens in many other matters: we deal with things not according to right beliefs, but by following bad habit. Since, then, I say this is how things stand, the person in training must try to get the better of himself, so as not to welcome pleasure or run from hardship, not to be in love with living or afraid of death, and, where money is concerned, not to value taking over giving.”***
—MUSONIUS RUFUS, LECTURES, 6

Musonius Rufus was Epictetus’ teacher, and this comes from his lecture on training. Just before it he describes how most of us grew up treating effort as bad and pleasure as good, giving money away as a loss and receiving it as a win. Then he says the uncomfortable part. We keep living by those habits without ever checking whether they’re right.

Every engineering team has a few of these. The deploys that only happen on Thursdays because someone got burned on a Friday years ago. The forty-step release checklist nobody has read all the way through since it was written. Reaching for the same library for every problem, or writing the code first and the tests “later.” Ask why, and the answer is some version of “that’s how we’ve always done it.”

Training, for Musonius, means getting the better of yourself on exactly these points. You stop running from the hard part of the work and stop grabbing the easy part just because it’s easy. You give your time to a colleague when it would be more comfortable to keep it for yourself.

Pick one habit this week and look at it honestly. Ask what it’s for and what it would cost to stop. Sometimes the old way survives the question, and that’s fine. At least now you know why you’re doing it.
