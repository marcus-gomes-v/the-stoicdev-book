## January 17th - Initiating Action: Beyond Fear of Failure

***“And so now I am your teacher, and you are being taught in my school. And my purpose is this—to make of you a perfect work, secure against restraint, compulsion, and hindrance, free, prosperous, happy, looking to God in everything both small and great; and you are here with the purpose of learning and practising all this. Why, then, do you not complete the work, if it is true that you on your part have the right kind of purpose and I on my part, in addition to the purpose, have the right kind of preparation? What is it that is lacking? ... The thing itself is possible and is the only thing that is under our control... Let us let bygones be bygones. Only let us begin, and, take my word for it, you shall see.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.19.29-34

You know the feeling. The side project has had a README and an empty src folder for eight months. The conference talk proposal is half written. The migration everyone agrees is necessary keeps sliding to next quarter.

Epictetus’ students had the same problem. They had a teacher who knew the way and every reason to start, and still they hung back. So he asks them, a little impatiently, what exactly is missing. Nothing, it turns out. The work is possible, and it’s the one thing that is actually in their hands.

His advice is short. Let bygones be bygones. The eight months you didn’t touch the project don’t have to be paid back before you’re allowed to start. Open the editor and write one function. Write the next paragraph of the proposal, even a rough one. Only begin, he says, and you’ll see.
