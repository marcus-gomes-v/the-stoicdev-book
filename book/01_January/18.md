## January 18th - Perceiving Grace in the Mundane

***“Pass then through this little space of time conformably to nature, and end thy journey in content, as an olive falls off when it is ripe, blessing nature who produced it, and thanking the tree on which it grew.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 4.48

Just before this sentence, Marcus lists the dead. Physicians who frowned over the sick, astrologers who predicted other people’s deaths, philosophers who talked endlessly about immortality, whole cities buried. It’s a grim list, and then he ends it with an olive.

The olive doesn’t fight the fall. It ripens and drops, and Marcus imagines it grateful to the tree that grew it. It’s one of the gentlest images in the whole book.

Most of what we build has a short life too. The service you spent a year on gets replaced in three. The startup is acquired and its product switched off. Even good code is eventually deleted by someone who never knew your name. You can find that depressing, or you can look at it the way Marcus looks at the olive.

Something nourished you along the way: a first job that let you break things, a reviewer who was patient with your early pull requests, an old codebase that taught you what not to do. When a project ends, or a job does, try to leave the way the olive does. Ripe, and grateful.
