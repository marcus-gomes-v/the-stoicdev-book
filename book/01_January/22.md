## January 22nd - Reflecting to Forge Ahead

***“I will watch myself closely from now on and, most useful of all, go back over my day. What makes us so bad is that no one looks back over his own life. We think about what we are going to do, and rarely even that; what we have done, we don’t think about. And yet good counsel about the future comes from the past.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 83.2

In another book, On Anger, Seneca describes his evening habit. Once the lamp is out and his wife, who knows the routine, has gone quiet, he goes back over the whole day: what he did, what he said, where he was too harsh. Here, in a letter to his friend Lucilius, he explains why it matters. We spend our time planning and almost none looking back, and the past is where the lessons are.

Teams already know this, at least in theory. That’s what a retrospective is for, or a good postmortem. The trouble is that most of us skip the personal version. We’ll write a five-page incident report about a database failover and never spend five minutes on why we snapped at a colleague in standup.

Try it tonight. Open a plain text file and write down what went well, what you’d do differently, and what you avoided. Keep it short and honest. In a month you’ll have something no sprint report can give you: a record of how you actually work.
