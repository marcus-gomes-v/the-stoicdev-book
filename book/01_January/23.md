## January 23rd - Unveiling the Illusion of Wealth

***“Let us pass to the rich. How many occasions there are on which they are just like poor men! When they are on a journey their baggage is cut down, whenever they are obliged to travel fast their train of attendants is dismissed. When they are serving in the army, how small a part of their property can they have with them, since camp discipline forbids superfluities!”***
—SENECA, ON CONSOLATION TO HELVIA, 12.1-2

Seneca wrote this from exile on Corsica, to comfort his mother. He came from a wealthy family and would later be one of the richest men in Rome, so he knew the subject from the inside. Even the rich, he points out, live like the poor whenever they have to travel light, and nobody seems to suffer much for it.

Tech has its own version of wealth. The salary band, the stock options, the laptop with the most memory, the title with “principal” in it. Some of that is genuinely nice. But think about your best working days. You were deep in a problem with a cheap keyboard and a terminal, and you didn’t miss any of the rest.

The things that make the work good travel light. Curiosity. Colleagues you trust. The quiet satisfaction of a bug you finally understand. None of that depends on the next raise, which is worth knowing before you trade a job you love for one that only pays more.
