## January 25th - Valuing the Essential

***“What’s left to be prized? This, I think—to limit our action or inaction to only what’s in keeping with the needs of our own preparation. . . it’s what the exertions of education and teaching are all about—here is the thing to be prized! If you hold this firmly, you’ll stop trying to get yourself all the other things. . . . If you don’t, you won’t be free, self-sufficient, or liberated from passion, but necessarily full of envy, jealousy, and suspicion for any who have the power to take them, and you’ll plot against those who do have what you prize. . . . But by having some self-respect for your own mind and prizing it, you will please yourself and be in better harmony with your fellow human beings, and more in tune with the gods—praising everything they have set in order and allotted you.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.16.2b-4a

In the tech industry, where success is often measured by the latest achievements or possessions, Marcus Aurelius's wisdom reminds us to prize what truly matters: our capacity for self-improvement, autonomy, and the cultivation of our intellect. Just like Warren Buffett, John Urschel, and Kawhi Leonard, who prioritize their true interests over material gain, developers can find greater freedom and satisfaction in focusing on personal and professional growth rather than external accolades or possessions.

This approach not only simplifies our lives but also enriches them, freeing us from the endless cycle of desire and dissatisfaction. By valuing our own preparation and mental well-being above all, we align ourselves more closely with our true purpose and find peace in the pursuit of knowledge and skill, rather than in fleeting external rewards. Today, let us reflect on what we truly value and how we can align our actions with those principles, fostering a sense of fulfillment and freedom in our daily lives.
