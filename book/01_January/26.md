## January 26th - Harnessing Your Inner Guide

***“Wipe out thy imaginations by often saying to thyself: Now it is in my power to let no badness be in this soul, nor desire, nor any perturbation at all; but looking at all things I see what is their nature, and I use each according to its value.—Remember this power which thou hast from nature.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.29

Marcus gives himself a sentence to repeat, and he means repeat: “often saying to thyself.” It works like a mantra, a phrase you say until it’s there before you need it.

You’ll need it on the days your head fills up with stories. The product manager who rewrote your ticket is trying to make you look bad. The silence after your proposal means everyone thought it was stupid. The senior engineer’s one-word review, “no,” means you’re about to be pushed out. Marcus calls these imaginations. They feel like facts, and they arrive faster than you can check them.

His sentence is a way to stop and check. It is in my power to let none of this in. I can look at what this thing really is and give it the weight it deserves, no more. Usually the ticket was rewritten for a boring reason. The silence was people reading. The “no” came with a link you hadn’t clicked yet.

Find your own short version of his line and say it often, on days when nothing is wrong. It will be ready on the day something is.
