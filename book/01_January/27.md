January 27th - Essential Training for the Stoic Developer

“There are three areas in which the person who would be wise and good must be trained. The first has to do with desires and aversions—that a person may never miss the mark in desires nor fall into what repels them. The second has to do with impulses to act and not to act—and more broadly, with duty—that a person may act deliberately for good reasons and not carelessly. The third has to do with freedom from deception and composure and the whole area of judgment, the assent our mind gives to its perceptions. Of these areas, the chief and most urgent is the first which has to do with the passions, for strong emotions arise only when we fail in our desires and aversions.” 
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.2.1-3a

Epictetus outlines three critical training areas for cultivating wisdom and virtue. For developers, these principles can be transformative, guiding both personal growth and professional excellence.

First, scrutinize your desires and aversions. In the tech industry, it's easy to be swayed by trends or fear of missing out. Focus on valuing what truly benefits your growth and rejecting what detracts from it.

Second, consider your motivations for action. Before diving into a new project or technology, ask yourself if you're driven by genuine interest and the potential for impact, or merely by external pressures and expectations.

Lastly, refine your judgment. Use reason to cut through the noise of the digital age, discerning the true value of information and opportunities that come your way.

By actively engaging in these areas of training, you'll not only enhance your capabilities as a developer but also navigate your career and life with greater purpose and clarity.
