## January 29th - The Art of Focused Simplicity

***“Every moment think steadily as a Roman and a man to do what thou hast in hand with perfect and simple dignity, and feeling of affection, and freedom, and justice, and to give thyself relief from all other thoughts. And thou wilt give thyself relief if thou doest every act of thy life as if it were the last, laying aside all carelessness and passionate aversion from the commands of reason, and all hypocrisy, and self-love, and discontent with the portion which has been given to thee. Thou seest how few the things are, the which if a man lays hold of, he is able to live a life which flows in quiet, and is like the existence of the gods; for the gods on their part will require nothing more from him who observes these things.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 2.5

Marcus asks for something simple and hard: do what’s in front of you, properly, and give yourself a rest from everything else.

Picture the opposite. You’re fixing a bug while half-listening to a call, with a draft message to your manager open in another window and part of your brain still arguing with yesterday’s review comment. The fix takes three hours and introduces a new bug.

Now picture doing only the fix. One window. Notifications off for forty minutes. You read the code, reproduce the problem, write the test, make the change. There’s nothing glamorous about it, and it’s the closest thing to calm most workdays offer.

Doing each task as if it were your last sounds dramatic. In practice it just means you stop saving your attention for something more important later, because this is the important thing right now. Marcus says the list of what really matters is short. Doing the task in your hands well, without fuss or complaint, is near the top of it.
