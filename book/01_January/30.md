## January 30th - Embracing Selective Ignorance

***“If you wish to make progress, then be content to appear senseless and foolish in externals, do not make it your wish to give the appearance of knowing anything; and if some people think you to be an important personage, distrust yourself.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 13

At lunch someone asks what you think about the latest layoffs at a big tech company, the new AI model everyone is posting about, and the drama in a popular framework’s issue tracker. You haven’t followed any of it. There’s a small sting in saying so, as if a developer who doesn’t keep up is already falling behind.

Epictetus tells his students to accept looking foolish about externals. Don’t fake knowledge, and don’t chase a reputation for knowing everything. His reason comes in the very next line: you can’t give full attention to your own character and to keeping up with the world at the same time. One of them will get neglected.

For developers the trade-off is concrete. Deep work on a hard problem needs hours of unbroken attention, and every feed you follow and every hot take you feel you must have an opinion on takes a slice of it.

So say “I haven’t been following it” without apologizing. It’s a complete sentence. And if people start treating you as the one who knows everything, be a little suspicious of yourself. It usually means you’ve been spending your attention on looking informed.
