## January 31st - Nurturing the Mind: Stoicism as Therapy

***“Do not return to philosophy as if she were a master, but act like those who have sore eyes and apply a bit of sponge and egg, or as another applies a plaster, or drenching with water. For thus thou wilt not fail to obey reason, and thou wilt repose in it.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 5.9

A month into a book like this, it’s easy to turn Stoicism into one more thing you’re failing at. You skipped three mornings. You snapped at someone in a code review on the 14th. Now the daily page feels like a ticket assigned to you that you keep not closing.

Marcus knew that feeling. Earlier in the same note he tells himself not to be disgusted or discouraged when he fails to live by his principles, and simply to come back. He says to come back to philosophy the way you’d go back to a remedy for sore eyes, and not to a strict boss with a list of everything you did wrong.

Think of a cold compress after a day of staring at a screen. That’s the tone. Open this book on a bad day because it helps, not because you owe it a streak. Read a page after a rough incident review, or before a hard one-on-one. If it eases something, it has done its job, and you’ll find reason much easier to follow when it isn’t shouting at you.
