February 1st - Cultivating Calm in the Code

“Keep this thought handy when you feel a fit of rage coming on—it isn’t manly to be enraged. Rather, gentleness and civility are more human, and therefore manlier. A real man doesn’t give way to anger and discontent, and such a person has strength, courage, and endurance—unlike the angry and complaining. The nearer a man comes to a calm mind, the closer he is to strength.” 
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 11.18.5b

In the competitive and often stressful world of software development, it's easy to let anger and frustration take hold. Just as athletes use trash talk to unbalance their opponents, the challenges and pressures we face can provoke reactions that distract and undermine our performance. Remember, displaying anger is not a sign of strength; it signifies a loss of control—a vulnerability.

Take inspiration from Joe Louis, the "Ring Robot," whose power lay in his unshakeable calm, not visible fury. True strength for a developer lies in maintaining composure, mastering our responses, and not allowing the heat of the moment to disrupt our focus or dictate our actions. Embrace the strength of a calm mind; it is your greatest asset in navigating the complexities of technology and teamwork.
