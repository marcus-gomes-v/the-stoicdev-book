## February 1st - Cultivating Calm in the Code

***“Let this truth be present to thee in the excitement of anger, that to be moved by passion is not manly, but that mildness and gentleness, as they are more agreeable to human nature, so also are they more manly; and he who possesses these qualities possesses strength, nerves, and courage, and not the man who is subject to fits of passion and discontent. For in the same degree in which a man’s mind is nearer to freedom from all passion, in the same degree also is it nearer to strength.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 11.18

There’s a particular kind of message that shows up at six on a Friday. Someone reverted your change without asking. A stakeholder replies to everyone that the feature is “basically broken.” Your hands are already typing the answer.

Stop there. Read what you wrote out loud. Would you say it to their face, in a room, with the whole team listening?

We like to think the sharp reply is the strong one. Marcus ran an empire through a plague and years of war, and he believed the opposite: the calm person is the strong one, and the angry one is the one who got hurt. In an outage, in a code review, in a tense planning meeting, the person everyone ends up trusting is the one whose voice didn’t go up.

Send the calm version. Or send nothing until tomorrow morning.
