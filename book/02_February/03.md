## February 3rd - Unraveling the Roots of Anxiety

***“When I see a man in anxiety, I say to myself, What can it be that this fellow wants? For if he did not want something that was outside of his control, how could he still remain in anxiety?”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.13.1

The night before a big demo, you’re lying awake running through it. You know the feature works. You’ve tested it a dozen times. So what exactly is keeping you up?

Epictetus would ask what you want. Probably not just a working demo, which is mostly in your hands. You want the VP to be impressed. You want nobody to ask the one question you can’t answer. You want the staging environment, which belongs to another team, to behave for twenty minutes.

None of that is yours. In the same chapter he describes a lyre player who sings beautifully when he’s alone and starts shaking the moment he walks into the theatre, because now he wants applause too, and applause belongs to the audience.

This turns a vague dread into a question you can actually answer. Write down what you’re anxious about and mark next to each item whether you control it. Prepare the parts that are yours, properly. Hold the rest more loosely, since no amount of lying awake was ever going to change it.
