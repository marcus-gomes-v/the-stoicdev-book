## February 4th - Cultivating Emotional Resilience

***“Who, then, is the invincible man? He whom nothing that is outside the sphere of his moral purpose can dismay.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 1.18.21

Watch a good engineer during a bad incident review. The VP wants to know who approved the change. Someone from sales says customers are furious. A director asks, twice, why nobody caught this earlier. And the engineer answers each question plainly, without getting defensive, without shrinking, and moves on to the next one.

People call that kind of calm a personality trait. Usually it comes from knowing which part of the room is theirs. The outage happened. The anger in the room is real. Neither is under their control. What they say next is.

Epictetus calls this person invincible, and then he tests the claim the way a trainer tests an athlete, round after round. Put money in his way. Put a pretty girl in his way. Try him in the dark. Throw abuse at him. Is he still standing?

Your rounds look different. A deadline moved up a week. A client who changes the spec every Friday. A bug that only shows up in production. You won’t fix all of them today. What you can do is keep each one outside, where it belongs, deal with it, and say, quietly, next.
