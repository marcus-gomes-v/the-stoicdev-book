## February 8th - Questioning Emotional Relief

***“‘The pain I feel is severe.’ What then? Do you stop feeling it if you bear it like a weakling?”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 78.17

The pager goes off for the third time this week. The fix you shipped yesterday broke something else. You slam the laptop shut, swear at nobody in particular, and write a message in the team channel that you’ll regret by lunch.

It feels like relief for a minute. Then you open the laptop and the bug is still there, and now there’s a message to apologize for.

Seneca wrote this to a friend who was ill, and he knew that illness himself; as a young man it had worn him down to skin and bone. His question is blunt: does bearing the pain badly make you feel it any less? It doesn’t. It usually adds a second problem to the first.

So the next time you’re about to blow up at a deadline or a flaky test, ask whether the outburst will help. If the honest answer is no, walk around the block and come back to the same bug with a cooler head. It will still be hard. It just won’t be harder than it needs to be.
