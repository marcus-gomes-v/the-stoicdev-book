## February 10th - The Toxicity of Anger

***“There is no more stupefying thing than anger, nothing more bent on its own strength. If successful, none more arrogant, if foiled, none more insane—since it’s not driven back by weariness even in defeat, when fortune removes its adversary it turns its teeth on itself.”***
—SENECA, ON ANGER, 3.1.5

While tales of anger fueling success and revenge sound compelling, they overlook the significant costs. Anger, as Seneca warns, blinds and consumes, leaving destruction in its wake even when it seems to drive us forward. It's a volatile and unsustainable source of motivation, producing as much harm to the bearer as to the target of their wrath.

In the world of development, where challenges and setbacks are par for the course, resorting to anger as a motivator is particularly perilous. It not only clouds judgment but also strains relationships and hampers creative problem-solving. Remember Martin Luther King Jr.'s caution against bearing the burden of hate and anger. Instead, seek motivation from healthier sources: passion for your work, the satisfaction of overcoming obstacles, and the joy of innovation. These fuels sustain and enrich, propelling you forward without the corrosive side effects of anger.
