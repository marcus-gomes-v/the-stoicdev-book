## February 11th - Sovereign of Self: Hero or Nero?

***“Our mind is now a king, now a tyrant. It is a king when it looks to what is honorable: it takes care of the health of the body entrusted to it and orders it to do nothing shameful, nothing low. But when it is out of control, greedy and pampered, it takes on that hateful and dreadful name and becomes a tyrant.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 114.24

Give a developer a little power and you find out who they are. The new tech lead who suddenly rejects every pull request that isn’t written their way. The architect who stops listening because nobody can overrule them anymore. Usually they were perfectly decent people a month earlier.

Seneca saw this up close. He tutored and advised Nero, and he knew how a ruler slides from king to tyrant. But in this letter he is talking about something smaller and closer: your own mind. When it looks to what is honorable, it takes care of you and asks nothing shameful of you. When it gets greedy and pampered, it becomes a tyrant over the very life it was supposed to protect.

The same goes for whatever small kingdom you run, a codebase, a team, an on-call rotation. You can rule it like a king or like a tyrant, and it usually starts with how you rule yourself: the shortcuts you allow, the moods you indulge, the late nights you call dedication.
