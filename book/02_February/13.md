## February 13th - The Illusion of Indulgence

***“Whenever you get an impression of some pleasure, as with any impression, guard yourself from being carried away by it, let it await your action, give yourself a pause. After that, bring to mind both times, first when you have enjoyed the pleasure and later when you will regret it and hate yourself. Then compare to those the joy and satisfaction you’d feel for abstaining altogether. However, if a seemingly appropriate time arises to act on it, don’t be overcome by its comfort, pleasantness, and allure—but against all of this, how much better the consciousness of conquering it.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 34

The notion of "cheat days" in dieting offers a paradoxical insight into human psychology: what seems like a reward often turns into a lesson in overindulgence and subsequent regret. This principle extends beyond dietary habits to all forms of pleasure that promise satisfaction at the expense of long-term well-being.

In the realm of software development, the temptation to cut corners, overindulge in procrastination, or chase after the latest but unnecessary technology can seem like a pleasure in the moment. Yet, the aftermath often reveals the true cost of such decisions. Epictetus urges us to pause and consider the consequences of our choices, encouraging a deeper reflection on the true nature of pleasure.

By exercising self-control and weighing the fleeting satisfaction against the lasting fulfillment of discipline and integrity, we find that true contentment lies not in yielding to temptation but in mastering it. This mastery, this choice of long-term satisfaction over momentary delight, is where genuine pleasure resides.
