## February 14th - Harnessing Rational Thought

***“This one thing is wisdom, to understand thought, as that which guides all the world everywhere.”***
—HERACLITUS, QUOTED IN DIOGENES LAERTIUS, LIVES OF THE EMINENT PHILOSOPHERS, 9.1

“Why did I do that?” Every developer has asked it, staring at a force-push to the main branch, or a reply-all sent in a bad mood, or a migration run against production instead of staging. You knew better. You had all the information. You just didn’t think.

Heraclitus wasn’t impressed by people who knew a lot. In the same passage, Diogenes Laertius quotes him saying that much learning does not teach understanding. Wisdom, for him, is one thing: understanding the reason that steers everything.

You carry a piece of that reason with you. It’s the part that reads the command before pressing enter, that asks what this change will break, that waits for the anger to pass before answering. Most of our regrets come from moments when something else was steering: hurry, pride, the wish to be done. Before you act, especially when the stakes are high, hand the wheel back to your thinking. That is most of the job.
