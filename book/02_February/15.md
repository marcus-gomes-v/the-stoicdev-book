## February 15th - Dissolving Nightmares of the Mind

***“Return to thy sober senses and call thyself back; and when thou hast roused thyself from sleep and hast perceived that they were only dreams which troubled thee, now in thy waking hours look at these [the things about thee] as thou didst look at those [the dreams].”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.31

At three in the morning the mind is a terrible engineer. The migration you’re running tomorrow will definitely corrupt the database. The odd tone in your manager’s message means you’re about to be let go. The code review you haven’t answered has turned the whole team against you. It all feels completely real.

Then morning comes. The migration runs fine, or fails in some boring, fixable way. Your manager was just typing fast. The reviewer forgot about it too.

Marcus describes waking from a bad dream and realizing it was only a dream, and asks you to look at your waking worries the same way. A lot of them are made of the same stuff.

So when the dread starts, treat it like a bug report with no steps to reproduce. Ask what exactly the problem is and what evidence you have, and check whatever can be checked right now. Some worries survive that and turn into real tasks, which is good, because real tasks can be worked on. The rest fade the way dreams do once you look at them in daylight.
