## February 16th - Simplifying the Path to Virtue

***“If any man should propose to thee the question, how the name Antoninus is written, wouldst thou with a straining of the voice utter each letter? What then if they grow angry, wilt thou be angry too? Wilt thou not go on with composure and number every letter? Just so then in this life also remember that every duty is made up of certain parts. These it is thy duty to observe, and without being disturbed or showing anger towards those who are angry with thee, to go on thy way and finish that which is set before thee.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.26

A product manager asks, for the third time, how the permissions system works. You’ve explained it in the doc, in standup, in a thread last week. This time they sound annoyed, as if the confusion were your fault. The temptation is obvious: answer with an edge, paste the link to the doc with no comment, let them feel how tired you are of this.

Marcus has an odd little example for this moment. If someone asked you how to spell your name, would you shout each letter at them? And if they got angry, would you get angry back? Of course not. You’d spell it again, calmly, letter by letter, until they had it.

He says every duty is made of parts like that. Explaining the permissions system is one of yours. So is reviewing the pull request from the colleague who never reads the guidelines, or answering the same support ticket for the tenth time. Each one is a small task you can finish. The feelings around it (their impatience, your irritation) are extra weight you don’t have to pick up.

None of this makes you a pushover. If the docs are bad, fix the docs. If the same question keeps coming back, that tells you something about the system. But in the moment itself the job is simple: spell out the letters. Go on your way and finish what is in front of you, without being thrown by the people who are angry with you, and without becoming one of them.
