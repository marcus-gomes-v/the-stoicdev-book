## February 17th - Contentment in the Present

***“But it is impossible that happiness, and yearning for what is not present, should ever be united. For happiness must already possess everything that it wants; it must resemble a replete person: he cannot feel thirst or hunger.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.24.17

“I’ll relax once this release is out.” Then the release goes out, and there’s the next one. “I’ll be happy when I make senior.” Then you make senior, and the question becomes staff. “Once we pay down this tech debt, things will calm down.” The tech debt never gets paid down, because new code becomes old code every day.

Epictetus says plainly that happiness and longing for what isn’t here can’t live together. Happiness, as he describes it, is like someone who has just eaten a good meal. They aren’t hungry. They aren’t thirsty. They aren’t sitting at the table already thinking about the next meal.

That doesn’t mean you stop wanting things. Ambition is fine. A promotion is a reasonable goal and a clean codebase is a good thing to work toward. The trouble comes when your peace of mind has to wait for them, because the goalposts move every time you get close.

So try to notice what’s already here. The problem you’re stuck on today is interesting, or you wouldn’t have chosen this work. The colleague you paired with this morning taught you something. The test suite you finally fixed is running green. None of that needs a milestone to count.

You can still aim for the next thing. Just don’t hold your contentment hostage until you get there. The day you’re living in is the only one where you can actually be satisfied.
