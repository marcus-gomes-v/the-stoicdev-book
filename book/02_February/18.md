## February 18th - Mastery Over Impressions: Weathering Life's Storms

***“The man who exercises himself against such external impressions is the true athlete in training. Hold, unhappy man; be not swept along with your impressions! Great is the struggle, divine the task; the prize is a kingdom, freedom, serenity...”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.18.27-28

Epictetus uses the image of an athlete here, and a few lines later, a storm. Sailors in his day called on the gods when the weather turned. What storm, he asks, is worse than a powerful impression that knocks your reason off its seat?

You know these storms. A message from the CEO that just says “call me.” A dashboard full of red ten minutes before a launch. A review comment that reads, at first glance, like an insult. In the first seconds the impression does all the work. Your stomach drops, the story of what’s happening is already written, and you’re halfway into a reaction.

The athlete is the answer. Nobody shows up to a competition untrained and expects to hold. You train for the big moments on the small days. You catch yourself assuming the worst about a terse email, you stop, you tell the impression to wait while you take a look at it, and you check what is actually there. Do that often enough on small things and you’ll have something to stand on when the real storm comes. He thought the prize was worth the effort: freedom, and a calm that doesn’t depend on the weather.
