## February 19th - Savoring Life's Offerings with Stoic Grace

***“Remember that you ought to behave in life as you would at a banquet. As something is being passed around it comes to you; stretch out your hand and take a portion of it politely. It passes on; do not detain it. Or it has not come to you yet; do not project your desire to meet it, but wait until it comes in front of you. So act toward children, so toward a wife, so toward office, so toward wealth; and then some day you will be worthy of the banquets of the gods.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 15

Picture a long dinner table where the dishes go around. When a plate reaches you, you take a reasonable helping and pass it on. When it moves on to someone else, you don’t grab it back. When it hasn’t reached you yet, you don’t stand up and lean across the table. You wait.

Epictetus says to live like that, and a career is a good place to practice. The interesting project goes to someone else this quarter. Don’t sulk about it, and don’t spend the quarter trying to pull it toward you. The promotion hasn’t come yet. Don’t let wanting it take over every one-on-one. And when something good does land in front of you, a chance to lead or a team you love working with, take it gladly and in moderation, without clutching.

He applies this to the biggest things too: children, a spouse, office, wealth. All of them are passed to us for a while, and none of them is ours to hold forever.

There’s nothing passive about this. You still show up to the table. You still eat. You just stop treating every dish that passes as a personal loss, and you become the kind of colleague people are glad to sit next to.
