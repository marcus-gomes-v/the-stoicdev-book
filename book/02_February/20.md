## February 20th - Reflecting on the Allure of Excess

***“How many pleasures have been enjoyed by robbers, patricides, tyrants.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.34

Marcus’s line is short and a little grim. Robbers, men who killed their own fathers, tyrants: they all had plenty of pleasures. His point is that pleasure, by itself, proves nothing. Some of the worst people who ever lived had good dinners and comfortable beds.

That’s worth keeping in mind when you measure your own life by how good it feels. The startup that grows fast on dark patterns has great quarters. The engineer who takes credit for other people’s work enjoys the promotion. The team that skips every review moves quickly and has a lot of fun, right up until the incident. Feeling good and doing well are two different measurements.

None of this makes pleasure bad. A clean release, a long lunch with the team, a quiet weekend after a hard launch: enjoy all of it. But when you choose where to work, what to build and how to get ahead, ask something other than whether it will feel good. Ask whether you’d be comfortable if the people you respect saw exactly how you got there.

That’s the test the robbers and tyrants fail, however much they enjoy themselves. And it’s one you can pass on an ordinary Tuesday, in a code review, when nobody is watching.
