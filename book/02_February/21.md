## February 21st - The Freedom in Wanting Less

***“Remember that it is not merely desire for office and wealth which makes men abject and subservient to others, but desire also for peace, and leisure, and travel, and scholarship. For it makes no difference what the external object be, the value you set upon it makes you subservient to another... where our heart is set, there also our impediment lies.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.4.1-2; 15

We usually picture greed as wanting money or a big title. Epictetus adds a list that’s less comfortable: wanting peace and quiet, wanting leisure, wanting to travel, wanting time to study. Those sound like healthy desires. He says they can make you a servant just as easily.

Think of the developer who has decided they can only be happy once they’re fully remote. Or the one who needs a sabbatical, or a team with no meetings, or finally enough time to learn Rust properly. None of those wants is bad. But once your contentment depends on getting one of them, whoever controls it controls you: the manager who approves remote work, the budget that would fund the sabbatical, the calendar you can’t clear.

That’s what he means when he says that where your heart is set, there your impediment lies. The thing you’ve attached yourself to becomes the exact place where you can be blocked.

So hold even good wants a little more loosely. Work toward them, ask for them, plan for them. Just keep a part of you that is fine either way, because that part is the one nobody can take from you.
