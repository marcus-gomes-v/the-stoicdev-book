February 21st - The Freedom in Wanting Less

"Remember that it’s not only the desire for wealth and position that debases and subjugates us, but also the desire for peace, leisure, travel, and learning. It doesn’t matter what the external thing is, the value we place on it subjugates us to another . . . where our heart is set, there our impediment lies." 
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.4.1-2; 15

Epictetus's insight is not a condemnation of desiring peace, leisure, travel, or learning, but a caution against the relentless pursuit of any external desire. Our vulnerability lies not in what we desire but in the intensity and dependency of our desires. When our happiness becomes contingent upon achieving something external, we risk disappointment and loss of self-control, as fate is unpredictable.

The wisdom here is in aspiring to want less. This is not about diminishing ambition or suppressing curiosity; rather, it's about cultivating a state of contentment with what we have and finding freedom in the absence of unrelenting desires. This approach empowers us to focus on what truly matters, reduces our susceptibility to external fluctuations, and enhances our resilience.

In the context of personal and professional development, this means setting goals that are within our control, such as personal improvement and skill acquisition, rather than fixating on outcomes that depend on external factors. By valuing the process over the outcome, we gain autonomy over our happiness and success, embodying the stoic ideal of wanting little and thus approaching the invincibility of the gods.
