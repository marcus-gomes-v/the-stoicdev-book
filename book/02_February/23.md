February 23rd - Rising Above Reactive Emotions

“You shouldn’t give circumstances the power to rouse anger, for they don’t care at all.” 
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 7.38

Drawing wisdom from the ancient playwright Euripides, Marcus Aurelius reminds us of a timeless lesson: external circumstances are indifferent to our emotions. In the unpredictable world of software development, where unforeseen challenges and changes are par for the course, this Stoic principle is particularly relevant. It teaches us not to personalize the impersonal or to expect the inanimate to sympathize with our frustrations.

This perspective is liberating for the Stoic developer. It encourages us to focus on our responses rather than futilely attempting to influence indifferent external events. By recognizing that our anger or distress has no bearing on the situation itself, we can choose a more measured, constructive approach to problem-solving and collaboration.

Circumstances, much like the indifferent gods of Euripides's play, are not swayed by our emotional turmoil. This realization empowers us to detach from reactive emotions and to approach our work with calm rationality. Remember, it's not the external that shapes us, but our internal stance—our reasoned choice in the face of what lies beyond our control.
