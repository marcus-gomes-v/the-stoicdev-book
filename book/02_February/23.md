## February 23rd - Rising Above Reactive Emotions

***“It is not right to vex ourselves at things, For they care nought about it.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 7.38

The build server doesn’t care that it’s your birthday. The flaky test doesn’t know you have a demo in an hour. The cloud provider’s outage was not aimed at you. And yet we talk to these things as if they could hear us. We swear at the CI pipeline. We take the failed deploy personally.

Marcus copied out a line from the playwright Euripides to remind himself: there’s no point being angry at things, because they don’t care. It’s almost funny how obvious that is, and how often we forget it.

Anger at circumstances is wasted twice. It changes nothing about the circumstance, and it drains energy from the one thing that can change it, which is your next move. Read the error message. Check what changed. Ask the person who knows the system. The machine will not meet you halfway, so all the work of staying calm is yours, and so is all the benefit.

People are different, of course. They do care, and that’s a subject for another day. But a lot of what ruins our mornings is things, and things are indifferent.
