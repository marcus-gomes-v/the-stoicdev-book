## February 24th - Mastering Your Response

***“Bear in mind that it is not the man who reviles or strikes you that insults you, but it is your judgement that these men are insulting you. Therefore, when someone irritates you, be assured that it is your own opinion which has irritated you. And so make it your first endeavour not to be carried away by the external impression; for if once you gain time and delay, you will more easily become master of yourself.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 20

A senior engineer leaves a comment on your pull request: “Did you even run this?” Your face goes hot. You start drafting a reply about how you’ve been here four years and you know how to run a test, thanks.

Stop at the hot face. Epictetus would say the comment hasn’t harmed you yet. What harms you is the judgment you add to it: that this is an insult, that they think you’re incompetent, that it has to be answered. That judgment is yours. You made it in about half a second, which is why it feels like a fact.

His advice is practical. Don’t let the first impression carry you away. Buy yourself time. Go get some water. Read the comment again in twenty minutes. Very often you’ll see something else. Maybe they were rushed, maybe they’re bad at writing comments, maybe you really didn’t run it. Then you can answer the useful part of the comment and let the tone go.

People are sometimes rude, and this doesn’t pretend otherwise. But whether their rudeness becomes your bad afternoon is still up to you, and the longer the pause, the easier it is to stay the one in charge.
