## February 24th - Mastering Your Response

***“Keep in mind that it isn’t the one who has it in for you and takes a swipe that harms you, but rather the harm comes from your own belief about the abuse. So when someone arouses your anger, know that it’s really your own opinion fueling it. Instead, make it your first response not to be carried away by such impressions, for with time and distance self-mastery is more easily achieved.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 20

In the realm of software development, as in life, the perception of events as harmful or benign lies within our control. Epictetus's wisdom urges us to distinguish between external actions and our internal responses. The sting of criticism, the frustration of a bug, or the tension in team dynamics do not inherently harm us; rather, it is our interpretation and reaction that can cause distress.

This principle encourages developers to approach challenges with a focus on their own agency. When faced with criticism, rather than instinctively reacting with defensiveness or anger, we have the power to analyze the feedback constructively. By choosing not to label events as 'good' or 'bad' and instead viewing them as opportunities for growth, learning, or simply as neutral occurrences, we can maintain our composure and effectiveness.

Epictetus's teaching is a call to practice self-mastery in our professional lives. It prompts us to question our automatic interpretations and to remember that our true power lies in our response. This approach not only leads to personal growth but also fosters a more harmonious and productive work environment. By embracing this stoic mindset, we empower ourselves to navigate the complexities of software development with resilience and equanimity.
