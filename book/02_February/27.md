## February 27th - Balancing Passion and Indifference in the Tech World

***“Of things some are good, others bad, and yet others indifferent. Now the virtues and everything that shares in them are good, while vices and everything that shares in vice are evil, and what falls in between these, namely, wealth, health, life, death, pleasures, pain, are indifferent.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.19.12-13

This is one of the oldest Stoic sorting rules, and Epictetus quotes it the way a student recites a lesson, right before warning that reciting it is the easy part. Some things are good, some bad, some indifferent. The good is virtue. The bad is vice. Everything else, including wealth, health, life, death, pleasure and pain, sits in the middle.

That list can sound cold. Health doesn’t matter? Of course it matters, and the Stoics thought it was better to have it than not. Their point was that none of these things makes you a good or bad person. They are the material you work with.

For a developer this is a useful lens on burnout. We pour ourselves into a project as if its success were the measure of our worth. The launch slips, priorities shift, a reorg cancels six months of work, and it feels like a verdict on us. By this rule, it isn’t one. A cancelled project belongs in the middle column. What you did while working on it belongs in the first: whether you were honest about the estimates, whether you helped the new hire, whether your work was careful.

You can still care about the project. Care a lot. Just keep it in the right column, so that when it goes wrong, you don’t go down with it.
