## February 28th - Regaining Equilibrium Amidst Turmoil

***“The soul is something like a bowl of water, and the external impressions something like the ray of light that falls upon the water. Now when the water is disturbed, it looks as though the ray of light is disturbed too, but it is not disturbed. And so, therefore, when a man has an attack of vertigo, it is not the arts and the virtues that are thrown into confusion, but the spirit in which they exist; and when this grows steady again, so do they too.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.3.20-22

Some days you’re just not yourself. You read the same function four times and it still doesn’t make sense. You snap at a colleague over nothing. A bug that would normally take ten minutes eats the whole afternoon, and by five you’re wondering whether you were ever any good at this.

Epictetus has a lovely image for those days. The soul is like a bowl of water, and what comes in from outside is like a ray of light falling on it. When the water is shaken, the light looks shaken too. But the light hasn’t changed. Only the water has.

Your skills didn’t disappear on Tuesday. The instinct for a bad design and the patience you usually have with juniors are still there. What got stirred up is the state you’re in: the bad night’s sleep, the worry about the reorg, the argument at breakfast.

So don’t pass big judgments on yourself on a choppy day. Let the water settle. Go for a walk, close a few easy tickets, get some sleep. When it calms down, so will everything you thought you’d lost.
