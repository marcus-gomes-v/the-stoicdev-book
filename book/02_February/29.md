## February 29th - The Art of Focused Mastery

***“Children who put their hand down into a narrow-necked jar and try to take out figs and nuts: if they get their hand full, they can’t get it out, and then they cry. Drop a few and you will get it out. And so do you too drop your desire; do not set your heart upon many things and you will obtain.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.9.22

Anyone who has watched a small child at a cookie jar knows this scene. The hand goes in, grabs too much, and won’t come back out. Epictetus’s fix is almost comically simple: drop a few.

Look at your own hand. You’re learning a new language, following three frameworks, building a side project, studying for a certification, mentoring two juniors, and trying to keep up with every new AI tool that launches on a Tuesday. Your fist is full, and nothing is coming out of the jar. Each of these is a perfectly good thing to want. Together, they keep you stuck.

You don’t have to give up curiosity. You do have to choose. Pick the one or two things that matter most this season and let the rest go for now. They’ll still be in the jar later. Go deep enough on one subject that you actually get something out of it, and then reach back in.

He ends with a promise: set your heart on fewer things, and you’ll actually get them. For a developer that usually means fewer tabs open and more work finished.
