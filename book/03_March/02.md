## March 2nd - The Balance of Self-Perception

***“It is above all things necessary to form a true estimate of oneself, because as a rule we think that we can do more than we are able.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 6.2

Sprint planning, Monday morning. Someone asks who can take the payment migration, and your hand is already up. You also have the search bug and an on-call week starting Wednesday. Somewhere in your head a voice says it’ll be fine.

Seneca would call that a bad estimate, and not of the ticket. Of yourself. As a rule, he says, we think we can do more than we can, and he goes through the ways: one man trusts his eloquence too far, another asks more of his land than it can produce, another loads heavy work onto a weak body. Every team has its versions. The person who talks their way into an architecture they can’t build. The person who keeps saying yes until the burnout shows up.

An honest estimate cuts the other way too. Plenty of good developers stay quiet in design reviews because they decided, without ever checking, that their opinion doesn’t count. That’s the same mistake pointed downward.

So look at your own record the way you’d look at a velocity chart. What did you really ship last quarter, and how long did it really take? Then raise your hand for the work that fits, and turn down the rest before it starts to crush you.
