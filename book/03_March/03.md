## March 3rd - Choosing Unity Over Fragmentation

***“These things do not go together. You must be one person, either good or bad; you must labour to improve either your own governing principle or externals; you must work hard either on the inner man, or on things outside; that is, play the rôle of a philosopher, or else that of a layman.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.15.13

Open the side-projects folder on almost any developer’s laptop. A half-finished game engine. A trading bot from one long winter. A machine learning course abandoned at lesson four. A note that just says “start a newsletter.” Every few months something new catches the eye, and whatever you were doing before starts to feel boring.

Epictetus saw the same pattern in his students. They heard one good lecture and wanted to be philosophers, then later they wanted to be tax collectors, then officials at court. These things don’t go together, he tells them. You have to be one person.

He isn’t saying you can never change stacks or careers. He’s asking where your effort goes. You can work on the inside: your judgment, or the way you take being wrong in front of the team. Or you can work on the outside: the title, the follower count, the look of being on top of every new release. Both eat hours. Only one of them stays with you.

Trying to do both usually means doing neither well. Pick the inner work first, and let the rest follow if it wants to.
