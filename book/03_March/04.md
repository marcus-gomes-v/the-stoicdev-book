## March 4th - Cultivating Authentic Freedom

***“He is free who lives as he wills, who is subject neither to compulsion, nor hindrance, nor force, whose choices are unhampered, whose desires attain their end, whose aversions do not fall into what they would avoid. Who, then, wishes to live in error?—No one.—Who wishes to live deceived, impetuous, unjust, unrestrained, peevish, abject?—No one.—Therefore, there is no bad man who lives as he wills, and accordingly no bad man is free.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.1.1-3

On paper you might look free. Remote job, flexible hours, a senior title, nobody checking when you log in. Then look at an ordinary Tuesday. You refresh the dashboard every ten minutes after a deploy. You reread a teammate’s message three times hunting for the insult. You bring last cycle’s promotion grudge into every one-on-one.

Epictetus uses the word in a stricter way. Free is the person whose desires don’t keep missing and whose fears don’t keep coming true, who isn’t shoved around by compulsion from inside or outside. Then he asks the obvious question: who would choose to live deceived, impetuous, peevish? Nobody. And yet plenty of us live exactly like that, which means we aren’t living as we wish at all.

That’s the uncomfortable part. Make an honest list of what keeps you from living the way you want. The job and the manager will be on it. So will your own reactions, the snap reply in a review thread and the envy you won’t admit to anyone.

Pick one of those reactions this week and watch where it starts. You won’t quit it by deciding to, but you can learn to see it coming a little earlier each time. Every time you catch it, you get a small piece of your own life back.
