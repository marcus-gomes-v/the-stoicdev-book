## March 5th - The Hidden Price of Excess

***“So with the things we go after, the things we work so hard to reach, we should look closely and see that either they hold no real benefit or they hold more trouble than benefit. Some are unnecessary; some are not worth what they ask. But we do not see this through, and things that cost us a great deal look to us as if they were free.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 42.6

The library was free. That’s what everyone said in the planning meeting. Nobody counted the afternoon spent wiring it up, the breaking change in the next major version, the security advisory that pinged the whole team on a Sunday, or the new hire who now has to learn its odd conventions before touching the checkout flow.

Seneca noticed that we only call something expensive when we pay for it with money. When we pay with ourselves, with time and worry and attention, we act as if it cost nothing. That’s how so many of the things we chase hardest end up giving us nothing, or giving us more trouble than use.

It goes well beyond dependencies. The fourth monitor. The certification you’re collecting mostly for the badge. The extra committee you joined because saying no felt rude. The title you’ve been chasing for two years, which comes with a calendar you would never accept if someone showed it to you in advance.

Before you add the next thing to your stack or your week, ask what it costs in hours and attention as well as money. Some of it will still be worth it. A surprising amount won’t.
