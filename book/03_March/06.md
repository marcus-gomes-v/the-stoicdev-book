## March 6th - Resisting the Lure of Personal Narratives

***“In your conversation avoid making mention at great length and excessively of your own deeds or dangers, because it is not as pleasant for others to hear about your adventures, as it is for you to call to mind your own dangers.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 33.14

Every engineering team has the story. The night the primary database ran out of disk. The migration that went sideways in the middle of a product launch. Whoever was there tells it at lunch, at the offsite, to every new hire, and each telling gets a little longer and the stakes a little higher.

The first time, people lean in. By the fifth, the juniors are nodding politely and checking their phones under the table. Epictetus says it plainly: your own adventures are far more fun for you to remember than for anyone else to hear.

The cost goes beyond boredom. When the most senior person in the room keeps steering back to what they survived, the conversation stops being about the problem the team has today. New people learn that the way to be taken seriously is to have a war story of your own, and the ones who don’t have one yet stay quiet in the retro.

Tell the story when it actually helps, when someone is about to make the same mistake you made. Keep it short. Then ask about theirs, and listen to the whole answer.
