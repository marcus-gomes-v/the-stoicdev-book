## March 7th - Beyond Sensory Illusions

***“Self-conceit he used to call a falling sickness (epilepsy) and eyesight a lying sense.”***
—DIOGENES LAERTIUS, LIVES OF THE EMINENT PHILOSOPHERS, 9.7

You glance at the dashboard and everything is green. You skim the diff and it looks fine. You read the stack trace, recognize the error, and you’re sure of the fix before you reach the end of the line. Three hours later you learn the dashboard was watching the wrong service, and the error came from a place you never looked.

Heraclitus called eyesight a lying sense. He wasn’t telling anyone to stop using their eyes. What we see arrives already interpreted, and the interpretation is often ours rather than the world’s. Then he went further and called self-conceit a falling sickness, the old name for epilepsy. It takes you without warning, and you go down in front of everyone.

The two sayings belong together. The faster you are sure, the less you look. That’s how an engineer with fifteen years behind them can lose a whole morning to a “cache bug” that turns out to be a typo in an environment variable.

So slow your certainty down a little. Reproduce before you fix. Read the whole log line. When you hear yourself say “it’s obviously the database,” treat it as a guess, and go check.
