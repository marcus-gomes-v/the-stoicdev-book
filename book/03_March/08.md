## March 8th - Guarding the Fortress of Your Mind

***“If someone handed over your body to any person who met you, you would be vexed; but that you hand over your mind to any person that comes along, so that, if he reviles you, it is disturbed and troubled—are you not ashamed of that?”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 28

Someone you’ve never met opens an issue on your open-source project. The title is in capitals. The body calls your work garbage and asks if you’ve ever heard of testing. You close the laptop, but the issue stays open in your head. At dinner you’re drafting replies. At midnight you’re still drafting them.

Epictetus puts his finger right on it. If a stranger took hold of your body and led it off somewhere, you’d be furious. Yet you let any passerby take hold of your mind and shake it with whatever they feel like saying, and somehow that doesn’t bother you at all.

It happens in smaller ways all day long. A one-line comment in a review thread. A product manager’s “per my last message.” And then the notification you didn’t need to open, from a channel you never needed to join. Each of these is someone else deciding what you’ll think about for the next hour.

You can’t stop people from saying things. You can decide how much of your attention they get. Answer the issue tomorrow, briefly and politely, or don’t answer it. Mute the channel. Your mind is the one tool you can’t replace, so be more careful about who you lend it to.
