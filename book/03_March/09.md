## March 9th - Curating Your Circle for Growth

***“To this topic you ought to devote yourself before every other, how, namely, you may avoid ever being so intimately associated with some one of your acquaintances or friends as to descend to the same level with him; otherwise you will ruin yourself... Choose, therefore, which you prefer; either to be loved just as much as you used to be by the same persons, remaining like your former self, or else, by being superior to your former self, to lose the same affection... Otherwise this facing both ways will bring about a double result: You will neither make progress as you ought, nor will you get what you used to get before.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.2.1; 4-5

Most companies have a lunch table where the same four people complain about everything. Management is clueless, the product is doomed, tests are a waste of time, and anyone who writes documentation is angling for a promotion. It’s funny and it’s comfortable, and for a while you were one of the loudest voices there.

Then something shifts. You start writing tests. You stop laughing at the new hire who asks a lot of questions. The table notices. Someone calls you a company person, and the invitations come a little less often.

Epictetus is blunt about this. Nothing is done without paying for it, he says, and you can’t stay the same person if you stop doing the same things. You can be liked by the same people exactly as before, or you can get better and lose some of that affection. Try to keep a foot in both and you end up with neither. You don’t make real progress, and the old warmth doesn’t come back either.

You don’t have to drop friends the moment they disagree with you. But notice which rooms pull you down to their level, and decide honestly how much of your week you want to spend in them.
