## March 10th - The Guiding Light of Exemplary Figures

***“A great part of our wrongdoing is taken away if a witness stands beside us when we are about to do wrong. The mind should have someone it holds in reverence, someone whose authority makes even its most private corners more holy. How fortunate is the one who sets others right not only by being there but even by being thought of!”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 11.9

It’s seven on a Friday. The fix works on your machine, the test suite is slow, and nobody is going to review a two-line change this late. You could push straight to main. Then you picture one particular person reading that commit on Monday: the lead who taught you to write a rollback plan, or the maintainer whose code you studied when you were starting out. Suddenly you’re opening a pull request like a normal person.

Seneca, passing on advice from Epicurus, says most wrongdoing falls away when there’s a witness. The witness he has in mind is someone you respect so much that just imagining them makes you behave better, even with nobody around. In the next line he suggests Cato, and if Cato feels too stern, someone gentler.

Pick yours. They don’t need to be famous. A former manager, a teammate, the author of a library you admire for its care. Ask how they’d read your commit message, or your reply to the angry customer.

Then look at the other half of the quote. Somewhere a junior developer may already be picturing you before they cut a corner. Try to be someone worth picturing.
