## March 11th - The True Currency of Freedom

***“The unhampered man, who finds things ready to hand as he wants them, is free. But the man who can be hampered, or subjected to compulsion, or hindered, or thrown into something against his will, is a slave.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.1.128-129

People have a phrase for stock grants: golden handcuffs. The shares vest over four years, so you stay, even after the team you loved has been reorganized away and the work has turned into something you would never have signed up for. Nobody forces you. You just can’t afford to leave.

Epictetus had been a real slave, and he uses the word on purpose. Anyone who can be hampered, compelled or pushed into something against their will is not free, however good the title looks. The handle they get pushed by is almost always something they’ve set their heart on that isn’t theirs to control. The equity. The reputation. The approval of one particular VP.

You see it in small decisions too. The engineer who ships a dark pattern they find embarrassing because the promotion cycle is close. The lead who keeps quiet about an impossible date because the director has a temper.

You don’t have to quit tomorrow. Just know where your handles are, and try to have fewer of them. Spend less than you earn. Keep a few friends outside the company. The fewer things someone can pull you by, the more of your choices stay your own.
