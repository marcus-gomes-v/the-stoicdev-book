## March 12th - Empathy in Understanding

***“When a man has done thee any wrong, immediately consider with what opinion about good or evil he has done wrong. For when thou hast seen this, thou wilt pity him, and wilt neither wonder nor be angry. For either thou thyself thinkest the same thing to be good that he does, or another thing of the same kind. It is thy duty then to pardon him. But if thou dost not think such things to be good or evil, thou wilt more readily be well disposed to him who is in error.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 7.26

Someone rewrote your module over the weekend. No ticket, no heads-up, just a nine-hundred-line pull request merged on Sunday night with the message “cleanup.” On Monday morning your first instinct is to find out who approved it and say something sharp in the channel.

Marcus suggests a different first question: what did they think was good? Maybe they believed the old code put the launch at risk and nobody would listen. Maybe their manager told them that this month, speed mattered more than process. You don’t have to agree. You only have to see the belief that made the action look right to them.

Then he makes a quieter point. Often you hold the same belief yourself. You have also skipped a review to hit a date. You have also “just fixed it” because asking felt slower. If so, you owe them the pardon you would want. And if you really don’t share their view, it’s even easier to be patient with someone who is simply mistaken.

You still need to talk about how changes get merged. But you can start that conversation curious instead of angry, and people hear the difference within the first sentence.
