## March 13th - Trusting the Larger Design

***“Whenever you find fault with Providence, only consider and you will recognize that what happens is in accordance with reason.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.17.1

The promotion list comes out and your name isn’t on it. His is. The one who presents other people’s work at the all-hands, who sits in every meeting with the VPs, who has never fixed a flaky test in his life. It feels like the universe shipped with a bug.

Epictetus heard this complaint all the time. “But the wicked man is better off.” Better off in what? he asks. In money, perhaps, because he flatters, he’s shameless, he lies awake at night scheming. That was his price, and he got what he paid for. Now look at where you are ahead of him, in being faithful and considerate, and you’ll find you are the one who is better off there.

That’s what sits behind this opening line. When you catch yourself blaming the way the world works, look again, and you’ll usually see that what happened follows from reasons. Not always fair ones by your standards, but reasons. The person who spends two years managing up gets the title. The person who spends two years making the system reliable gets a reliable system, and the quiet respect of everyone on the on-call rotation.

You can still want the title, and learn to tell your own story better. But you stop treating the result like an injustice from the sky. It was a trade, and you get to decide whether you’d have made his.
