## March 14th - The Barrier of Ego

***“He would say that if we want to master the sciences there is nothing so fatal as conceit.”***
—DIOGENES LAERTIUS, LIVES OF THE EMINENT PHILOSOPHERS, 7.23

The junior on your team suggests an index. You’ve been writing SQL for twelve years, so you wave it off. Two weeks later the query is still slow, and someone from another team adds the index in a five-minute pull request.

Zeno, who founded the Stoic school, thought nothing was more fatal to learning than conceit, and he lived like he meant it. Diogenes Laertius tells us that even after Zeno had made real progress he kept slipping into another teacher’s lectures, and that when a logician showed him seven forms of an argument and asked a hundred drachmas for them, he paid two hundred.

Conceit is sneaky in our work because experience is real. You do know a lot. That knowledge is exactly what makes it hard to hear the one thing you don’t know yet, especially from someone with a smaller title. And the more senior you get, the fewer people will correct you to your face, so the habit has to come from you.

Treat “I already know this” as a warning light. Read the docs for the tool you think you’ve mastered. Let the junior finish the sentence.
