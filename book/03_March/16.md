## March 16th - Cherishing Your Inner Compass

***“Reverence the faculty which produces opinion. On this faculty it entirely depends whether there shall exist in thy ruling part any opinion inconsistent with nature and the constitution of the rational animal. And this faculty promises freedom from hasty judgment, and friendship towards men, and obedience to the gods.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 3.9

Before you have a feeling about anything at work, you have already formed an opinion. The CTO’s vague email means layoffs are coming. The new hire’s question means they didn’t read the docs. That framework is overhyped. These judgments arrive so fast they feel like facts, and everything you do next follows from them.

Marcus tells us to revere the faculty that makes them. He means something narrower than intelligence: the part of you that decides what things mean. Whether anything unreasonable takes root in your mind depends entirely on it. When it works well, he says, it brings freedom from hasty judgment, goodwill toward people, and obedience to the gods, which for a Stoic meant being at peace with how the world runs.

Developers put a lot of effort into protecting their focus. Fewer protect their judgment. It gets worn down by bad sleep and by a feed full of other people’s hot takes, and you usually notice only after a bad call.

Treat it like production. Don’t deploy untested opinions to it. When a judgment shows up fully formed, ask where it came from before you act on it.
