March 17th - Essence of True Beauty

"You are not your body and hairstyle, but your capacity for choosing well. If your choices are beautiful, so too will you be." 
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.1.39b-40a

It’s that line in the movie Fight Club: 'You are not your job, you’re not how much money you have in the bank. You are not the car you drive. You’re not the contents of your wallet.' Obviously, our friend Epictetus never saw that movie or read the book—but apparently, the consumerism of the 1990s existed in ancient Rome too.

In the digital age, our distractions have evolved. Endless social media scrolling, the latest trends in politics, wokeness, and a plethora of catchphrases vie for our attention, drawing us away from what truly matters. These are the modern-day equivalents of ancient Rome's consumerism, challenging our sense of self and skewing our perceptions of value and worth.

Yet, as Epictetus reminds us, our true beauty and identity lie not in these external validations but in the choices we make. The decision to engage meaningfully rather than passively consume; to act with integrity in a world that often rewards superficiality; to find worth in our actions rather than our possessions—these choices define us.

Let's remember, amid the noise and distractions of contemporary life, that our most significant asset is our ability to choose wisely. By focusing on the virtues of our decisions, we cultivate an inner beauty that transcends time, trends, and the fleeting judgments of society.
