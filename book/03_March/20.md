## March 20th - Poised for the Unpredictable

***"I may wish to be free from torture, but if the time comes for me to endure it, I'll wish to bear it courageously with bravery and honor. Wouldn't I prefer not to fall into war? But if war does befall me, I'll wish to carry nobly the wounds, starvation, and other necessities of war. Neither am I so crazy as to desire illness, but if I must suffer illness, I’ll wish to do nothing rash or dishonorable. The point is not to wish for these adversities, but for the virtue that makes adversities bearable."***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 67.4

In the realm of technology and software development, we don't wish for downtime, data breaches, or critical bugs. Yet, just like President James Garfield, who stood ready amidst the turmoil of a divided country and a short-lived presidency, we must also prepare ourselves for the inevitable adversities of our field. The way to ensure we're ready when systems fail or when unforeseen vulnerabilities surface isn't by hoping they never will, but by rigorously preparing through diligent work, comprehensive testing, and anticipation of potential issues.

Let's approach our work with the mindset of always being on guard, not out of fear, but from a place of strength and preparedness. By adopting a posture of readiness, by being "at home" when challenges come knocking, we not only manage to navigate them more effectively but also grow in our resilience and capability. Today, let's commit to the virtues that turn adversities into opportunities for growth, ensuring that when the inevitable occurs, we meet it with courage, honor, and unwavering professionalism.
