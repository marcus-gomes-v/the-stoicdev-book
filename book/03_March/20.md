## March 20th - Poised for the Unpredictable

***“I would rather that torture stay far from me; but if it has to be endured, what I will pray for is to carry myself through it with courage, with honor, with spirit. Naturally I would prefer not to be caught up in a war; but if war comes, I will pray to bear its wounds, its hunger and everything else that war’s necessity brings, with a noble heart. I am not so mad as to want to be sick; but if sickness must come, I will pray to do nothing without self-control and nothing feeble. So it is not the hardships that are worth wishing for, but the virtue by which hardships are borne.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 67.4

Nobody wants the security incident. Nobody hopes the database gets corrupted during the holiday freeze, or that layoffs are announced the week after they sign a mortgage. If you could choose, you’d skip all of it, and Seneca wouldn’t think less of you for that.

He wrote this letter as an old man who spent most of his days in bed, and he is careful about what he says we should wish for. Not the hardship. Nobody sane prays to get sick. What he wishes for is the way he will carry himself if the hardship comes anyway: with courage, with self-control, without doing anything he’d be ashamed of later.

You can work on that kind of wish in advance. An incident that goes well is rarely luck. It’s usually a team that ran a game day last quarter, wrote the runbook while things were calm, and agreed beforehand that the first message to customers would be honest even if it was embarrassing. The person who stays steady on the bridge call at 3 a.m. has usually practiced being steady on smaller, duller days.

So stop hoping the bad week never comes. It will. Put the energy into the version of yourself who shows up for it.
