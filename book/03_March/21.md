## March 21st - Inner Haven: Your Personal Retreat

***“Men seek retreats for themselves, houses in the country, sea-shores, and mountains; and thou too art wont to desire such things very much. But this is altogether a mark of the most common sort of men, for it is in thy power whenever thou shalt choose to retire into thyself. For nowhere either with more quiet or more freedom from trouble does a man retire than into his own soul, particularly when he has within him such thoughts that by looking into them he is immediately in perfect tranquillity; and I affirm that tranquillity is nothing else than the good ordering of the mind. Constantly then give to thyself this retreat, and renew thyself.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 4.3

Every developer has the fantasy. A cabin with no Wi-Fi. A year off to build furniture. A small town by the sea where nobody has heard of Kubernetes. On the worst weeks you browse rentals while the build runs and feel better for about ten minutes.

Marcus had the same longing, and he admits it to himself here. He also had less chance than most of us to act on it. He spent years on campaign along the Danube, running an empire from military camps. So he went looking for a retreat he could reach without leaving, and found it in his own mind.

The catch is in the middle of the passage. The retreat only works if the place is in order. Tranquillity, he says, is nothing else than the good ordering of the mind. If your head is a tangle of grudges and half-made decisions, closing your eyes for a minute just leaves you alone with the mess.

So keep a few clear principles you can return to quickly, the way you keep a clean main branch to reset to. Then use the small gaps you already have, the walk to the coffee machine or the minute while tests run, to go back there and come out a little steadier.
