## March 22nd - The Essence of True Education

***“What, then, does it mean to be getting an education? It means to be learning how to apply the natural preconceptions to particular cases, each to the other in conformity with nature, and, further, to make the distinction, that some things are under our control while others are not under our control.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 1.22.9-10

Ask any team whether code should be simple and readable, and everyone says yes. Ask whether this particular pull request, with its three new layers of abstraction, is simple and readable, and you get a forty-comment thread.

Epictetus saw the same thing. Everyone agrees that the good should be pursued. The fights start when we apply that idea to a specific case. His example is Agamemnon and Achilles, who agreed completely on principle and then fell out over one decision, at great cost to the whole Greek army.

So for him, being educated has little to do with diplomas. It means learning to apply the ideas everyone already holds to the case in front of you, and to apply them well. It also means sorting what is under your control from what isn’t.

You can see both skills in a good senior engineer. They don’t argue about whether testing matters. They know which few tests this change needs. When the release date moves because of a contract they never saw, they put their energy into the part of the plan they can still shape. You only learn that by practicing on real cases, one at a time.
