## March 23rd - The Straitjacketed Soul

***“Diseases of the mind are vices that have grown old and hard, such as greed and ambition. They have wound themselves far too tightly around the mind and have begun to be its lasting evils. To define it briefly, such a disease is a judgment that clings stubbornly to what is wrong, treating things that deserve to be wanted a little as if they deserved to be wanted very much.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 75.11

It starts small. You want the promotion, which is reasonable. So you pick the project with the most visibility instead of the one that most needs doing. You push for a rewrite partly because it will look good in the packet. None of it feels like greed or ambition at the time. It feels like being smart about your career.

Seneca draws a line between a passing passion and a disease of the mind. A passion is a sudden movement, like a single cold. Let it come back often enough without dealing with it and it turns chronic, the way a cough that never leaves becomes something much worse. By then the vice has wound itself around your judgment and quietly shapes how you see everything.

His definition is worth reading twice: wanting very badly what deserves to be wanted only a little. A title. A bigger team. A launch date six weeks earlier than anyone believes in.

You won’t fix that with one good decision. You fix it by catching it early, while it’s still a cold. Once a quarter, look at what you’ve been chasing hardest and ask honestly how much it deserves.
