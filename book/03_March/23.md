March 23rd - The Straitjacketed Soul

“The diseases of the rational soul are long-standing and hardened vices, such as greed and ambition—they have put the soul in a straitjacket and have begun to be permanent evils inside it. To put it briefly, this sickness is an unrelenting distortion of judgment, so things that are only mildly desirable are vigorously sought after.”
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 75.11

In the financial crash of 2008, highly intelligent individuals lost trillions in wealth. This debacle illustrates how even the sharpest minds can falter under the influence of greed. These experts, deeply familiar with market mechanisms and responsible for vast sums, were blinded by the desire for quick profits, leading to complex, poorly understood financial instruments and risky trades.

This historical reflection isn't just an economic lesson but a personal one. As developers, or in any profession, it’s crucial to examine how our own vices, like greed or ambition, might distort our judgment. Are there areas in your professional life where excessive ambition might be clouding your decisions? Perhaps in the pursuit of cutting-edge technologies or pushing a product to market prematurely?

Use today to reflect on these biases and consider how a more Stoic, rational approach could lead to better decision-making. This not only prevents personal and professional mishaps but also fosters a healthier, more ethical work environment.
