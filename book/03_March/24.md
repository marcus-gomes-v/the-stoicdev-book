## March 24th - Living Philosophy Daily

***“Eat as a man, drink as a man, adorn yourself, marry, get children, be active as a citizen; endure revilings, bear with an unreasonable brother, father, son, neighbour, fellow-traveller. Show us that you can do these things, for us to see that in all truth you have learned something of the philosophers.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.21.5-6

Epictetus had little patience for students who learned a principle on Monday and wanted to lecture on it by Tuesday. A builder, he said, doesn’t offer a talk on the art of building. He takes the contract, builds the house, and the house is the proof.

Our field has plenty of talks about building. Threads on clean architecture, slides on servant leadership. Some of them are good. None of them prove anything about the person who made them.

The proof turns up in ordinary places. How you treat the support engineer who escalates at five on a Friday. Whether you stay patient with the colleague who reopens the same argument in every planning meeting, the way Epictetus asks his students to bear with an unreasonable brother.

Read the philosophers, by all means. Then show what you learned somewhere nobody is clapping.
