## March 25th - The Simplicity of Wealth and Freedom

***“Freedom is not acquired by satisfying yourself with what you desire, but by destroying your desire.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.1.175

You told yourself that once you made senior you’d relax. Then you made senior, and for about three weeks it felt great. Now you’re reading the staff engineer rubric at midnight, and the feeling is exactly the one you had before.

Epictetus describes this precisely. People who haven’t yet reached the thing everyone admires imagine it will bring every good with it. Then it arrives, and they find the same fever, the same tossing about at sea, the same longing for whatever they still don’t have. So he turns the usual approach around. You don’t get free by feeding the desire until it’s full. You get free by taking the desire apart.

You can still want to grow. But it helps to notice which wants are really yours and which came bundled with the career ladder, and to let a few of them go before you spend another two years climbing toward the next rung.
