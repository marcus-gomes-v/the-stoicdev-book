## March 27th - Evaluating True Worth

***“Very valuable things, said he, were bartered for things of no value, and vice versa.”***
—DIOGENES LAERTIUS, LIVES OF THE EMINENT PHILOSOPHERS, 6.2.35

Diogenes liked to point out something odd about the market. A statue sold for three thousand drachmas, while a quart of barley flour, which kept people alive, went for two copper coins. We pay a fortune for what is ornamental and almost nothing for what we need.

Look at your team’s budget and you’ll find the same pricing. A six-figure contract for a platform half the engineers never log into. Meanwhile the fifteen-minute call with a real user, the README for the service everyone depends on, the test that would have caught last month’s outage: all of it cheap, and all of it postponed.

The trade runs the other way too. We hand over things of great value for almost nothing. A night’s sleep for one more feature nobody asked for. A good working relationship for the pleasure of winning an argument in a review thread.

Check the price tags again. Diogenes would bet they’re stuck on the wrong items.
