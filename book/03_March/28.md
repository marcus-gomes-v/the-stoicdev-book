## March 28th - Planning Against Panic

***“A life with no purpose drifts. And if a purpose has to be set, then firm principles start to become necessary. You will grant, I think, that nothing is more disgraceful than a person who hesitates, who is unsure, who steps back in fear. That is what will happen to us in everything unless we pull out the things that catch hold of our minds, hold them back, and stop them from going forward and trying with all they have.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 95.46

You can spot a team without a direction from the outside. The roadmap changes every time a big customer sends an email. Every planning meeting reopens the same question, and the loudest person usually wins.

Seneca says a life without a purpose drifts, and a career is no different. A sentence earlier he compares it to sailing: you need a star to steer by. Once you have one, you need principles too, decisions made in advance, so you aren’t working out under pressure what you believe.

That’s what keeps you from what he calls most disgraceful: hesitating, wavering, backing off in fear. A team that has agreed to fix flaky tests before adding features doesn’t need a fresh argument every time the build goes red. A developer who has decided never to ship code they haven’t run doesn’t negotiate with themselves at six in the evening.

Pick the star first. Then write down the few rules that follow from it, and let them carry you when you would otherwise flinch.
