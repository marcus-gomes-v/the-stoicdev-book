## March 29th - Chasing Approval: A Fool's Errand

***“If it should ever happen to you that you turn to externals with a view to pleasing someone, rest assured that you have lost your plan of life. Be content, therefore, in everything to be a philosopher, and if you wish also to be taken for one, show to yourself that you are one, and you will be able to accomplish it.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 23

There’s a name for it: résumé-driven development. The internal tool with twelve users that somehow needs Kubernetes, an event bus and three microservices. The rewrite in whatever language everyone is talking about this year. Nobody says the real reason in the design review, but everyone knows it. The project is partly for the next job interview, or the conference talk, or the senior engineer whose approval you still want.

Epictetus is direct about where that leads. The moment you turn to outside things to please someone, you have lost your plan. You’re no longer building for the problem in front of you. You’re building for an audience, and audiences change their minds every year.

His fix is almost stubborn. If you want to be taken for a good engineer, be one first in front of yourself. Choose the boring database when the boring database fits. Write the clear code nobody will post about. You’ll know why you made the choice, and that is enough. The people whose opinion is worth having usually notice anyway.
