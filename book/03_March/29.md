March 29th - Chasing Approval: A Fool's Errand

“If you should ever turn your will to things outside your control in order to impress someone, be sure that you have wrecked your whole purpose in life. Be content, then, to be a philosopher in all that you do, and if you wish also to be seen as one, show yourself first that you are and you will succeed.” 
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 23

It's all too common in the tech industry to chase validation from peers or the market. We may adopt the latest tech trends or buzzwords not because they are necessarily the best tools for the job, but because we want to be seen at the cutting edge, to impress. This can lead us away from what truly matters—solving real problems effectively.

Epictetus reminds us that our core purpose should guide our actions, not the fleeting approval of others. When you focus on being true to your values and your professional ethics, you naturally align with the philosophies that drive meaningful progress. Before you adopt that new framework or gadget, ask yourself: Am I doing this for the project's benefit, or for my ego? This introspection can save you from needless complexity and keep your projects streamlined and purposeful.
