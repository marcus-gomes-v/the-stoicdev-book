## March 30th - The Guiding Force of Reason

***“Hasten [to examine] thy own ruling faculty and that of the universe and that of thy neighbor: thy own, that thou mayst make it just: and that of the universe, that thou mayst remember of what thou art a part; and that of thy neighbor, that thou mayst know whether he has acted ignorantly or with knowledge, and thou mayst also consider that his ruling faculty is akin to thine.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 9.22

A message arrives from another team: they’re deprecating the API your service depends on, in six weeks. Your first draft of a reply is not one you’d want read out at your review.

Marcus offers three places to look before you send anything. First at your own mind, to make sure what you’re about to do is fair. Then at the whole, to remember what you belong to: one company, one product, users who don’t care which team owns which endpoint. Then at the other person’s mind, to see whether they acted out of ignorance or with full knowledge. Maybe nobody told them you depended on it. Maybe they knew, and you need to ask why.

He adds one more thing. Their mind is related to yours, and it works under the same kind of deadlines.

Run those checks and the reply usually changes. It gets calmer, and it’s far more likely to end in a meeting than an escalation.
