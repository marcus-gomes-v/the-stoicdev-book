## April 4th - HUMILITY AMIDST SUCCESS

***“Take care that thou art not made into a Caesar, that thou art not dyed with this dye; for such things happen. Keep thyself then simple, good, pure, serious, free from affectation, a friend of justice, a worshipper of the gods, kind, affectionate, strenuous in all proper acts. Strive to continue to be such as philosophy wished to make thee. Reverence the gods, and help men. Short is life. There is only one fruit of this terrene life—a pious disposition and social acts.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.30

The promotion comes through. Staff engineer, or head of engineering at a startup that just raised. Suddenly your opinion in the architecture review ends the discussion, even when it shouldn’t.

This is the dye Marcus was afraid of. He was the actual Caesar, the most powerful man of his world, and he still warned himself not to become one on the inside. Stay simple, stay kind, keep doing the proper work.

For you that might mean still reviewing a junior developer’s code with care, still taking your turn on call, still saying “I don’t know” in meetings. The title lasts a few years. The only fruit Marcus counted was how you treated people and what you did for them.
