## April 7th - EMBRACING ADAPTABILITY IN TECH LEADERSHIP

***“Here are two things of which one must rid men, conceit and diffidence. Now conceit is to fancy that one needs nothing further. And diffidence is to assume that one cannot enjoy a life of serenity under so many adverse circumstances.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.14.8

Most teams have two developers like this, and sometimes they’re the same person on different days. The first has seen it all. Their design doc doesn’t need review and their stack doesn’t need updating. The second has given up. The codebase is too far gone, the deadlines are insane, nothing will ever be calm here, so why try.

Epictetus wanted both rooted out. Conceit stops you from learning anything new. Diffidence stops you from believing things can get better.

Both hide well. Notice the next time you dismiss a suggestion without reading it, or the next time you hear yourself say “that’s just how it is here.” Either one is a good moment to stop and look again.
