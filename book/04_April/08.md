## April 8th - VALIDATING TRUTHS IN TECHNOLOGY LEADERSHIP

***“When it comes to money, where we feel our clear interest, we have an entire art where the tester uses many means to discover the worth. . . just as we give great attention to judging things that might steer us badly. But when it comes to our own ruling principle, we yawn and doze off, accepting any appearance that flashes by without counting the cost.”***
—EPICTETUS, Discourses, 1.20.8; 11

In tech leadership, just as we meticulously verify the security and efficiency of our code or software products, we should also critically evaluate the principles and assumptions guiding our decisions. We diligently test new technologies before deployment to ensure their value and safety, yet we often accept prevailing managerial or strategic beliefs without scrutiny. Are these beliefs genuinely benefiting our teams and projects, or are they just widely accepted myths? As leaders, cultivating a habit of questioning and validating these underlying assumptions can lead to more innovative and effective leadership strategies. This critical scrutiny is not just about doubting but ensuring that our actions and beliefs are genuinely aligned with our goals and values in the tech industry.
