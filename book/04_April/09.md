## April 9th - CALIBRATING JUDGMENTS IN SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT

***“Make it, therefore, your study at the very outset to say to every harsh external impression, ‘You are an external impression and not at all what you appear to be.’ After that examine it and test it by these rules which you have, the first and most important of which is this: Whether the impression has to do with the things which are under our control, or with those which are not under our control; and, if it has to do with some one of the things not under our control, have ready to hand the answer, ‘It is nothing to me.’”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 1.5

A message from your manager at 4:55 p.m.: “Can we talk tomorrow morning?” No context. By nine that night you’ve rehearsed the layoff conversation twice and started updating your CV.

That’s a harsh impression, and Epictetus would have you say to it first: you are an impression, not the thing itself. Then run the test. Is this under my control? What your manager wants to say tomorrow is not yours to decide. Whether you sleep tonight, and how you walk into that meeting, are yours.

The same goes for the new framework that will supposedly make your whole stack obsolete, or the security headline that turns out not to touch your version. Look first. Sort it. Whatever lands outside your control gets the answer Epictetus keeps ready: it is nothing to me.
