## April 9th - CALIBRATING JUDGMENTS IN SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT

***“From the very beginning, make it your practice to say to every harsh impression, ‘you are an impression and not at all what you appear to be.’ Next, examine and test it by the rules you possess, the first and greatest of which is this—whether it belongs to the things in our control or not in our control, and if the latter, be prepared to respond, ‘It is nothing to me.’”***
—EPICTETUS, Enchiridion, 1.5

In the meticulous world of software development, every new library, tool, or architectural pattern can seem like a must-have until it isn't. Just like a seasoned developer tests new code, we must test our impressions and assumptions. An urgent bug might seem catastrophic, or a new technology might appear as the ultimate solution. However, a disciplined approach requires stepping back to evaluate: Is this really within my control? Is it as critical as it seems? By systematically questioning our immediate reactions to technological trends and project pressures, we cultivate a more focused, effective approach to our work. This rigorous scrutiny ensures we don't react hastily but respond thoughtfully, optimizing our resources and focus for what truly aligns with our project goals and professional values.
