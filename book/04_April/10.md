## April 10th - DISPELLING ILLUSIONS IN TECH LEADERSHIP

***“It is not the things themselves that disturb men, but their judgements about these things.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 5

The database goes down at 2 a.m. That’s the event. Everything after it is judgement: this is a disaster, the CEO will hear about it, I’ll be blamed, we’re going to lose the big customer.

Listen to whoever is running the incident and you can tell which kind of sentence they’re using. “The primary is down, the replica is ten minutes behind, we’re failing over” is a description. “This is going to ruin us” is a judgement, and it spreads through a team faster than the outage does.

Epictetus isn’t asking you to pretend nothing happened. The server is down. Say that plainly and work on it. The panic is something you add on top, and you can choose not to add it.
