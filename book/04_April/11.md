## April 11th - CULTIVATING HUMILITY IN TECH LEADERSHIP

***“What is the first business of one who practises philosophy? To get rid of thinking that one knows; for it is impossible to get a man to begin to learn that which he thinks he knows.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.17.1

Ten years in, you know how things work. You know which ORM is slow, why microservices are a trap, how a good standup should run. Most of the time you’re right, and that’s the problem.

A new hire suggests a tool you dismissed in 2019. You don’t even open the link, because you already know. Six months later another team adopts it and their build times drop by half. The tool changed. Your opinion of it didn’t.

Epictetus made this the first job of anyone trying to get wiser: let go of the feeling that you already know. Nobody can teach you what you think you’ve already learned. The senior developers worth working with keep asking “what am I missing?” long after anyone would expect them to. It looks like modesty. Mostly it’s how they keep getting better.
