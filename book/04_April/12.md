## April 12th - BEYOND THE GLITTER: TECH LEADERS' TEMPTATIONS

***“Atreus: Who puts aside inflowing fortune’s gifts? Thyestes: Whoso has found how easily they ebb.”***
—SENECA, THYESTES, 536

A recruiter writes with an offer that seems too good: twice your salary, a staff title, equity in a company that “is about to explode.” Or your own company offers you the big rewrite and a new team, as long as it ships by Q3.

In Seneca’s play, Atreus offers his exiled brother Thyestes a share of the throne. Thyestes has held power before and lost it, and he answers with the line above. The one who turns down fortune’s gifts is the one who has seen how fast they drain away. He is right to be suspicious. The offer is a trap. He takes it anyway.

You don’t have to refuse every opportunity. But if you’ve lived through a startup that ran out of runway, or a reorg that dissolved your team overnight, you already know how easily these things ebb. Use what you know. Ask what the offer depends on and what’s left if it disappears. Sometimes the strong answer is no, and no is easier to say before you’ve spent the money in your head.
