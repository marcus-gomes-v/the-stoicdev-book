## April 13th - THE ELEGANCE OF SIMPLICITY IN SOFTWARE DESIGN

***“Labor not unwillingly, nor without regard to the common interest, nor without due consideration, nor with distraction; nor let studied ornament set off thy thoughts, and be not either a man of many words, or busy about too many things... Be cheerful also, and seek not external help nor the tranquillity which others give. A man then must stand erect, not be kept erect by others.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 3.5

You open a pull request where a simple config change arrives wrapped in a plugin system, three layers of abstraction and a factory for the factory. Somebody worked hard on it. Nobody can review it.

Marcus wrote this about his own habits: don’t dress up your thoughts, don’t talk too much, don’t be busy with too many things at once. It carries over well. Write the function that does one thing. Write the README a tired person can follow at midnight. In meetings, say what you mean in two sentences and then stop.

The last line matters too. Stand up straight on your own instead of being held up. Code that needs its author in the room to explain it is being held up. So is a design that only survives because one senior engineer defends it in every review. Build things that keep standing when you’re not there, and work in a way that doesn’t depend on someone else to keep you calm.
