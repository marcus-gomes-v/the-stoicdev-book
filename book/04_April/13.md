## April 13th - THE ELEGANCE OF SIMPLICITY IN SOFTWARE DESIGN

***“Don’t act grudgingly, selfishly, without due diligence, or to be a contrarian. Don’t overdress your thoughts in fine language. Don’t be a person of too many words and too many deeds... . Be cheerful, not wanting outside help or the relief others might bring. A person needs to stand on their own, not be propped up.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, Meditations, 3.5

In the realm of software development and tech leadership, the principle of "Less is more" is particularly potent. Just as literary greats master the art of brevity, the most effective software engineers and tech leaders focus on simplicity and clarity. They resist the temptation to over-engineer solutions or to use complex technologies when simpler ones will do. Marcus Aurelius, in his admonitions, could be speaking directly to the modern tech professional: don't overcomplicate your projects, don't be overly verbose in your documentation, and don't undertake unnecessary tasks that add no real value. This approach not only makes your work more elegant and efficient but also ensures that when you do speak or act, it carries more weight. Strive for designs that stand on their own, needing no excessive support or adornment a sign of true craftsmanship in the tech world.
