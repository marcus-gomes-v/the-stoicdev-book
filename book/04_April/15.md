## April 15th - THE INEVITABLE COSTS OF LIFE

***“Nothing will ever happen to me that I will meet with a sad heart or a sour face. I will pay no tax unwillingly. And all the things we groan about, all the things we dread, are the taxes of living. From these, Lucilius, my friend, you should neither hope for an exemption nor ask for one.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 96.2

Every job in software comes with a bill. The on-call week that lands on your birthday. The migration nobody wants but somebody has to do. The dependency that breaks on a Friday because a maintainer you’ve never met renamed a function. The meeting that could have been an email, every single week.

You can pay these grudgingly. Plenty of people do, and you can hear it in standup. Or you can pay them the way Seneca says he does, without the sour face, because they were always part of the price.

He isn’t pretending these things are pleasant. He calls them what we groan about and dread. His point is that nobody gets an exemption. There is no career without the pager and the legacy code. Hoping for one is like hoping for a salary with no income tax.

Once you stop expecting to be let off, something odd happens. The cost is still there, but the resentment on top of it, which was most of the weight, starts to lift.
