## April 16th - DISCERNING PATTERNS IN THOUGHT AND ACTION

***“In discourse thou must attend to what is said, and in every movement thou must observe what is doing. And in the one thou shouldst see immediately to what end it refers, but in the other watch carefully what is the thing signified.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 7.4

In a planning meeting the product manager says, “It would be nice to have this by the end of the month.” The junior developer hears “nice to have.” The senior hears that the CEO has already promised it to a customer.

Marcus asks for two kinds of attention. When people talk, listen for what the words actually point to, underneath how they sound. When something is done, look at where it’s heading. A teammate who stops commenting on pull requests is telling you something. So is a sudden “quick sync” invite from your manager with no agenda, and so is a commit that deletes half the tests in one go.

Most of us half-listen. We’re composing our answer while the other person is still talking, or reading Slack during the demo. Then we’re surprised by things that were said right in front of us.

Try listening the way you’d read a stack trace. Every line means something, and the important one is rarely the loudest.
