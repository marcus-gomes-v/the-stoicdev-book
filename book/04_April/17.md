## April 17th - NO HARM, NO FOUL

***“Take away thy opinion, and then there is taken away the complaint, ‘I have been harmed.’ Take away the complaint, ‘I have been harmed,’ and the harm is taken away.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 4.7

A reviewer leaves one line on your pull request: “Why would we do it this way?” You read it at the end of a long day and it lands like a slap. You start drafting a defense. You think about who else will see the thread.

Read it again in the morning. It might be contempt. It might also be a real question from someone who doesn’t know the constraint you were working around, or from someone who writes short comments because English is their third language, or who was typing on a phone between meetings.

Marcus lays out the order things happen in. First the opinion: this person is attacking me. Then the complaint: I’ve been harmed. Then the harm itself, the bad night, the cold standup, the grudge you carry into the next review. Take away the first step and the rest has nothing to stand on.

That doesn’t mean every comment is kind. Some reviewers are careless and a few are cruel. But the comment is text on a screen, and whether it becomes an injury depends on what you decide it means. You can answer the question that was asked without picking up an insult that may not be there.
