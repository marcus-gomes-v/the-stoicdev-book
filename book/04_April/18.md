## April 18th - OPINIONS AND PEACE OF MIND

***“What is misfortune? A judgement. What are strife, disagreement, fault-finding, accusing, impiety, foolishness? They are all judgements, and that, too, judgements about things that lie outside the province of moral purpose, assumed to be good or evil. Let a man but transfer his judgements to matters that lie within the province of the moral purpose, and I guarantee that he will be steadfast, whatever be the state of things about him.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.3.18-19

For one day, keep a tally of the verdicts you hand down on things that aren’t yours. The standup ran long, so it was a waste. The sprint got cut, so the quarter is ruined. The architect picked the other database, so the project is doomed. The client wrote in all caps, so the client is impossible.

None of those are facts. They are judgements stacked on top of facts, and most of them are about things you don’t control: other people’s choices, the budget, a vendor’s roadmap. Epictetus says this is where nearly all our strife and blame comes from. We stick the labels good and evil on things outside our own choices, and then we suffer from the labels.

Just before this line he says these judgements have to be rooted out. He isn’t asking you to stop judging altogether. He wants you to move your judgement to where it belongs, which is your own work and the way you treat people.

When the sprint gets cut, the fact is that you have fewer days. What you do with those days is the only part worth calling good or bad. Keep your judgement there and, as Epictetus promises, you’ll stay steady whatever the rest of the company is doing.
