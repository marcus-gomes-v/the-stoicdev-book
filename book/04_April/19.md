## April 19th - OUR SPHERE OF IMPULSES

***“Epictetus also said, a man must discover an art [or rules] with respect to giving his assent; and in respect to his movements he must be careful that they be made with regard to circumstances, that they be consistent with social interests, that they have regard to the value of the object...”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 11.37

Here is the emperor of Rome, writing in his private notebook, quoting a man who was born a slave. As far as we know, Marcus never met Epictetus. In the first book of the Meditations he thanks his teacher Rusticus for lending him Epictetus’s discourses from his own collection, and he clearly read them closely.

What he copied out is practical. Learn the art of assent, of deciding what you actually agree with. Then watch your impulses. Before you act, check that the action fits the circumstances, that it serves the people around you, and that the thing you’re chasing is worth what you’re paying for it.

You know what an unchecked impulse looks like in this job. The 6 p.m. hotfix pushed straight to main because the bug was embarrassing. The angry reply-all. The rewrite you start on a Monday because the old code offended you, with no idea how long it will take or who it helps.

Notice also who Marcus was willing to learn from. People with power often find it hard to take advice from anyone below them. The emperor took his from a former slave and wrote it down so he wouldn’t forget. If the most useful comment on your design comes from an intern, take it the same way. Good judgement doesn’t check job titles.
