## April 21st - GUARD YOUR ATTENTION

***“When you relax your attention for a little while, do not imagine that whenever you choose you will recover it, but bear this in mind, that because of the mistake which you have made to-day, your condition must necessarily be worse as regards everything else... What then? Is it possible to be free from fault altogether? No, that cannot be achieved, but it is possible ever to be intent upon avoiding faults. For we must be satisfied, if we succeed in escaping at least a few faults by never relaxing our attention.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.12.1; 19

You open your editor to fix one bug. A notification pops up, you check it, it links to a thread, the thread links to an article. Forty minutes later you’re back in the editor and you can’t remember which file you had open.

Epictetus noticed something we usually miss. Attention doesn’t come back just because you want it to. One lapse makes the next one easier, and soon not paying attention is a habit, and putting things off is another. The forty minutes are the cheap part. The habit is the expensive one.

He doesn’t ask for perfection either. Nobody writes code without bugs, and nobody stays focused all day. What he asks is that you keep trying not to slip. Close the chat app while you write the tricky part. Turn off the badge on your phone. You’ll still make mistakes. You’ll make fewer, and Epictetus says that is enough.
