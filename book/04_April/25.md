## April 25th EMBRACING CORRECTION

***“If any man is able to convince me and show me that I do not think or act right, I will gladly change; for I seek the truth, by which no man was ever injured. But he is injured who abides in his error and ignorance.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.21

You spent two days on a caching layer. In review, a colleague points out that the database already caches those queries, and shows you the numbers. Your first instinct is to defend the work, because it was work.

Marcus is clear about who gets hurt here: the one who holds on to the mistake. If you merge the cache to save face, you get extra complexity, a new class of bugs and a reviewer who will think twice before telling you anything again.

Being corrected stings for a few minutes. Thank the person, close the branch and keep what you learned about the database. It’s the cheapest lesson you’ll get all week.
