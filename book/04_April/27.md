## April 27th EXAMINE FROM EVERY ANGLE

***“Turn it [the body] inside out, and see what kind of thing it is; and when it has grown old, what kind of thing it becomes, and when it is diseased. Short lived are both the praiser and the praised, and the rememberer and the remembered: and all this in a nook of this part of the world; and not even here do all agree, no, not any one with himself: and the whole earth too is a point.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.21

Think of the thing you want to be known for. The open-source library with ten thousand stars. The conference talk that got passed around for a week. Now turn it over the way Marcus turns over the body. In five years the library is unmaintained and the talk is about a framework nobody uses. The people who praised it have moved on, and so will you.

This is less gloomy than it sounds. It takes the pressure off. If recognition is this small and this short-lived, it can’t be worth losing sleep over, and failing in front of people can’t be as final as it feels the night before a demo.

Do the work because it’s worth doing. The praise, when it comes, is a nice extra that fades on its own schedule.
