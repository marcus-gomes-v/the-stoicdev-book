## April 30th ALIGN YOURSELF WITH YOUR TRUE CHARACTER

***“Now it so happens that the rational and the irrational are different for different persons, precisely as good and evil, and the profitable and the unprofitable, are different for different persons. It is for this reason especially that we need education, so as to learn how, in conformity with nature, to adapt to specific instances our preconceived idea of what is rational and what is irrational. But for determining the rational and the irrational, we employ not only our estimates of the value of external things, but also the criterion of that which is in keeping with one’s own character.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 1.2.5-7

Two engineers get the same offer: double the salary to build ad tracking that quietly follows users from site to site. One takes it and sleeps fine. The other can’t stop thinking about it. Epictetus wouldn’t be surprised. What is reasonable for one person is unbearable for another, and the difference is character.

Right after this passage he gives a blunt example. One man will hold a chamber pot for another, because the alternative is a beating and no food. Another can’t bear to do it at all. The price is the same for both. What it costs them isn’t.

This is why he says we need education. He means something slower than a course or a certification: learning what you actually stand for. Without it you follow whatever script the company or the industry hands you. With it, you can look at an offer, a feature or a deadline and ask a question no spreadsheet can answer. Is this in keeping with who I am?
