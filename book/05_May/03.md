## May 3rd SHOW YOUR KNOWLEDGE THROUGH ACTION

***“Those who have learned the principles and nothing else are eager to throw them up immediately, just as persons with a weak stomach throw up their food. First digest your principles, and then you will surely not throw them up this way. Otherwise they are mere vomit, foul stuff and unfit to eat. But after you have digested these principles, show us some change in your governing principle that is due to them; as the athletes show their shoulders as the results of their exercising and eating, and as those who have mastered the arts can show the results of their learning.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.21.1-3

Most of us have been this person at least once. You read one article about event sourcing on Sunday, and by Monday’s design review you want to rebuild billing around it.

Epictetus has an unkind image for this: an idea that comes straight back up before it has been digested. Nothing has happened to it yet. It went in and came out again.

Digestion is slower and much quieter. You try the idea on one small service and find out where it hurts. Months later it shows up in your work without any announcement, in the way you split a module or in the question you ask before anyone writes a line of code. That is the change he wants to see, the way an athlete’s training shows in the shoulders and not in what the athlete says about training.
