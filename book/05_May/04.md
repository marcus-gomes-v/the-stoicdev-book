May 4th TRUE IMPRESSIVENESS

“How much better is it to be known for doing well by many than for living extravagantly? How much more worthy than spending on sticks and stones is it to spend on people?” 
—MUSONIUS RUFUS, LECTURES, 19.91.26-28

Think of all you know about the lifestyles of the rich and famous. The same type of gossip and notoriety was popular in Roman times. Certain Romans were known for their lavish spending on koi ponds, orgiastic parties, and sumptuous feasts. The conspicuously wealthy earn their reputation through spending, but what an empty reputation it is.

Marcus Aurelius sold off imperial furnishings to pay down war debts. José Mujica, the former president of Uruguay, gave 90 percent of his salary to charity and drove a twenty-five-year-old car. Who's more impressive? Those who spend extravagantly or those who prioritize others?
