## May 6th THE BEAUTY OF VIRTUE

***“What, then, makes a man beautiful? Is it not the presence of a man’s excellence? Very well, then, young man, do you too, if you wish to be beautiful, labour to achieve this, the excellence that characterizes a man.—And what is that?—Observe who they are whom you yourself praise, when you praise people dispassionately; is it the just, or the unjust?—The just;—is it the temperate, or the dissolute?—The temperate;—and is it the self-controlled, or the uncontrolled?—The self-controlled.—In making yourself that kind of person, therefore, rest assured that you will be making yourself beautiful; but so long as you neglect all this, you must needs be ugly, no matter if you employ every artifice to make yourself look beautiful.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.1.6-9

Epictetus said this to a young student of rhetoric who had turned up with elaborately styled hair and fancy clothes. He didn’t mock him. He asked him whom he actually admired.

Try the same question at work. Think of the engineers you respect most, the ones you’d want on the call during a bad incident. It’s rarely the one with the slickest slides or the biggest following. It’s the one who is fair when things go wrong, who stays level while everyone else is typing in capitals, who can turn down a bad idea without making it personal.

None of that comes with your genes. You build it one tense meeting at a time. A polished profile can make you look impressive for a while. It won’t make you the person people trust when the night goes badly.
