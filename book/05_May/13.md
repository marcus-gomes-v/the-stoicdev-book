## May 13th CULTIVATING EXCELLENCE THROUGH HABITS

***“Every habit and faculty is confirmed and strengthened by the corresponding actions, that of walking by walking, that of running by running... In general, therefore, if you want to do something, make a habit of it; if you want not to do something, refrain from doing it, and accustom yourself to something else instead. The same principle holds true in the affairs of the mind also; when you are angry, you may be sure, not merely that this evil has befallen you, but also that you have strengthened the habit, and have, as it were, added fuel to the flame.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.18.1-5

You don’t become someone who writes tests by agreeing that tests are good. You become that person by writing them, on the small changes too, until opening a pull request without them feels strange.

Epictetus says the same mechanism runs in the other direction. When you lose your temper over a flaky deploy, you aren’t only having a bad moment. You’re practising. The next outburst will come a little more easily.

So watch what you repeat. If there’s a habit you want gone, stopping is rarely enough. Put something in its place, like a walk around the block before you answer, or a draft you leave unsent until the morning.
