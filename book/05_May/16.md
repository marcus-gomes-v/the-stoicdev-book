## May 16th BUILDING HABITS, BREAKING CYCLES

***“If you don’t wish to be a hot-head, don’t feed your habit. Try as a first step to remain calm and count the days you haven’t been angry. I used to be angry every day, now every other day, then every third or fourth. . . if you make it as far as 30 days, thank God! For habit is first weakened and then obliterated. When you can say ‘I didn’t lose my temper today, or the next day, or for three or four months, but kept my cool under provocation,’ you will know you are in better health.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.18.11b-14

In the tech world, where projects can be complex and frustrations frequent, managing anger and maintaining professionalism are crucial. Utilize the 'chain method' for behavioral change, inspired by comedian Jerry Seinfeld's advice to a young comic. Mark each day you manage your reactions, aiming to extend your streak of calm days. This method applies to any area needing improvement, not just anger management. Each unbroken link strengthens your ability to stay composed under pressure, enhancing both your personal growth and professional interactions.
