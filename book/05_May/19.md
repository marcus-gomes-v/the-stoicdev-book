## May 19th PRACTICE BEYOND THEORY

***“That is why the philosophers admonish us not to be satisfied with merely learning, but to add thereto practice also, and then training. For in the course of years we have acquired the habit of doing the opposite of what we learn and have in use opinions which are the opposite of the correct ones.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.9.13-14

You can explain blameless postmortems perfectly. You’ve read the books and linked the articles in the team channel. Then something breaks at 2 a.m., and the first thing out of your mouth on the call is “who merged this?”

Epictetus wouldn’t be surprised. Years of habit pull the other way, and learning a principle once doesn’t undo them. The old reflex is simply faster than the new idea.

That’s why he names practice and then training as two separate steps. Practice is doing the thing on purpose when it’s easy, like asking “what made this possible?” in a calm retro. Training is doing it under pressure, again and again, until it becomes the reflex. Nobody gets there by reading one more article about it.
