## May 20th DEEPER, NOT WIDER

***“What’s the point of having countless books and libraries, whose titles could hardly be read through in a lifetime. The learner is not taught, but burdened by the sheer volume, and it’s better to plant the seeds of a few authors than to be scattered about by many.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 9.4

In the tech industry, where the latest trends and tools can dominate focus, there's a parallel in how we consume knowledge. Instead of sprawling across numerous frameworks and languages superficially, what if developers, leaders, or tech enthusiasts embraced a deeper understanding of fewer, more fundamental technologies? Seneca's advice underscores the value of depth in learning: mastering core principles that apply broadly rather than skimming the surface of many. This approach not only enhances expertise but also builds a solid foundation from which one can adapt and innovate. As in coding, where understanding the deeper logic can lead to cleaner, more efficient programs, in learning, depth can be more transformative than breadth.
