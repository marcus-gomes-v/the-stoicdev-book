## May 20th DEEPER, NOT WIDER

***“What is the use of possessing numberless books and libraries, whose titles their owner can hardly read through in a lifetime? A student is overwhelmed by such a mass, not instructed, and it is much better to devote yourself to a few writers than to skim through many.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 9.4

Look at your bookmarks folder. Or the shelf of programming books with the spines still stiff, or the courses you bought in a sale and opened once.

Seneca saw the same thing in rich Romans whose libraries were there mostly to decorate the dining room. His advice was to stay with a few writers and let them sink in, rather than skim through many.

For a developer that might mean one language you know down to its memory model instead of six you can build a to-do app in. One good book on databases, read slowly with a notebook open, instead of forty blog posts. Depth compounds. Someone who really understands how a query planner works can pick up the next database in a week. Someone who skimmed ten still can’t say why their query is slow.
