## May 23rd LIVING FULLY TODAY

***“Teach me that the good of a life does not rest on its length but on its use, and that it can happen, and indeed very often does, that someone who has lived long has lived little.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 49.10

Some engineers spend fifteen years at a company and learn a great deal. Others spend fifteen years there and live the same year fifteen times, with the same complaints in every retro and the same framework they stopped questioning long ago.

Seneca asks to be taught that a life is measured by its use and not its length. He also notices how often someone who lived long has lived very little.

Careers work the same way. A résumé lists years of experience as if they were all equal, and they aren’t. One year in which you shipped something hard and changed your mind about a design you were sure of is worth more than five on autopilot.

You don’t need a new job to fix it. You need this week to differ from last week in at least one way that matters. Learn the part of the system you always route around. Pair with someone you never work with. Do that often enough and the years start to mean something.
