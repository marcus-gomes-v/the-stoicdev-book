## May 24th CREATING YOUR OWN LUCK

***“I was once a fortunate man, but I lost it, I know not how.—But fortunate means that a man has assigned to himself a good fortune: and a good fortune is good disposition of the soul, good emotions, good actions.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 5.36

Talk to anyone who has been in this industry for a while and you’ll hear some version of it. “I used to be lucky. Good teams, good managers, projects that worked. Then it dried up.”

Sometimes that’s true. Markets turn, companies fold, a reorg puts you under a manager who doesn’t rate you. Marcus doesn’t deny any of it. He defines fortune differently. A fortunate person, he says, is one who has given himself good fortune, and the kind he means is a good disposition of the soul, good emotions and good actions.

That kind of luck doesn’t depend on the job market. It shows in how you treat the new hire and whether you do careful work on the unglamorous ticket. It shows when your project gets cancelled and you don’t turn bitter. People who have it tend to find the next good opportunity, because others remember what it was like to work with them.

Think back to the last time you felt lucky at work. Some of that luck was probably the way you showed up.
