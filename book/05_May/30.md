## May 30th MEASURING TRUE PRODUCTIVITY

***“I cannot call a man industrious, if I hear merely that he reads or writes, and even if one adds that he sits up all night, I cannot yet say that the man is industrious, until I know for what end he does so... If, however, the end for which he toils is his own governing principle, to have it be, and live continually, in accordance with nature, then and then only I call him industrious.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.4.41; 43

Some teams treat the commit graph like a scoreboard. Green squares every day, messages in the channel at midnight, a reputation for always being online. It looks like hard work, and sometimes it is.

Epictetus wants to know what it’s for. A man who loses sleep for his reputation he calls ambitious. One who does it for money he calls fond of money. He keeps the word industrious for effort aimed at your own mind, at keeping it in line with nature.

Before you open the laptop again tonight, ask what the extra hour is serving. Sometimes it’s worth it. Often the most useful thing you can do for tomorrow’s work is sleep.
