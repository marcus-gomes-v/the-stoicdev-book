## June 1st MASTERING THE ART OF ADAPTABILITY

***“The things however which are proper to the understanding no other man is used to impede, for neither fire, nor iron, nor tyrant, nor abuse, touches it in any way.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.41

The migration was supposed to take a weekend. On Saturday morning the vendor changes their API. On Sunday the staging database runs out of disk. By Monday the plan you presented with such confidence is a list of things that didn’t happen.

Most of that was never yours to protect. Plans, schedules and vendors live outside you. What lives inside is your judgment, and Marcus was blunt about it: no fire, no iron, no tyrant and no insult can touch it.

That’s where adaptability comes from. A better plan helps less than you’d think. What helps is holding every plan with a clause attached: this, unless something gets in the way. When something does, you haven’t lost anything that was really yours. You still have a mind that can look at Monday’s mess and choose the next move.
