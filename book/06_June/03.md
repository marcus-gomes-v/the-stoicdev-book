## June 3rd EMBRACING ADAPTABILITY

***“He is not able to serve in the army: then let him become a candidate for civic honours: must he live in a private station? then let him be an advocate: is he condemned to keep silence? then let him help his countrymen with silent counsel. Is it dangerous for him even to enter the forum? then let him prove himself a good comrade, a faithful friend, a sober guest in people’s houses, at public shows, and at wine-parties. Suppose that he has lost the status of a citizen; then let him exercise that of a man.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 4.3

The reorg moves your team off the product you built. The promotion goes to someone else. The new manager wants you on maintenance for a quarter. Any one of these can feel like the end of being useful.

Seneca is writing about someone whom fortune has pushed out of public life, and his answer is a chain of fallbacks. If he can’t serve in the army, he can stand for office. If he’s silenced, he can help with quiet advice. If even the forum is closed to him, he can still be a good friend and a sober guest at dinner.

There is almost always a smaller version of your work still open. On maintenance you can write the runbook everyone needed. Without the title you can still be the reviewer whose comments make other people’s code better. Do the work that’s in reach, and do it properly.
