## June 6th JUDGEMENT IN PERSISTENCE

***“Add also those who are by no means fickle, yet who must thank their dulness, not their consistency for being so, and who go on living not in the way they wish, but in the way they have begun to live.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 2.6

Every company has a project like this. It started four years ago for a reason nobody remembers. It has two maintainers, a roadmap that keeps sliding and a standing meeting on Thursdays. Nobody has decided to keep it going. Nobody has decided to stop.

Seneca had an unflattering name for that kind of staying power. He didn’t call it consistency. He called it dullness: people who go on living the way they began, instead of the way they want.

Persistence is a virtue when you chose it. When you notice you’re sticking with a framework, a job or a project only because changing course would take effort, it’s something else. The honest test is simple. If you were starting today, knowing what you know now, would you pick this again? If the answer is yes, keep going and stop second-guessing it. If it’s no, say so in the next planning meeting, even if the room goes quiet. Momentum feels like a reason. It isn’t one.
