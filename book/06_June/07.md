## June 7th CHOOSING YOUR INFLUENCES

***“We are wont to say that we are not able to choose who our parents should be, but that they were assigned to us by chance; yet we may be born just as we please: there are several families of the noblest intellects: choose which you would like to belong to.”***
—SENECA, ON THE BREVITY OF LIFE, 15.3

Nobody picks their first team. You land wherever the hiring process drops you, with whatever manager, codebase and habits happen to be there. Some people learn careful engineering that way. Others learn that tests are optional and that the loudest voice in the design review wins.

Seneca says the family you’re born into is chance, but you can choose another one: the great minds whose work you can study. That holds for us too. You can apprentice yourself to the engineer who wrote the paper you keep rereading, to the maintainer whose commit messages teach you something every time, to the old colleague whose code reviews you still remember.

Pick them on purpose. Read their work slowly, borrow their habits, ask yourself what they would do with the mess in front of you. Give it a few years and you start to look like part of the family.
