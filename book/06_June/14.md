## June 14th SHIFTING PERSPECTIVES FOR BETTER OUTCOMES

***“Everything has two handles, by one of which it ought to be carried and by the other not. If your brother wrongs you, do not lay hold of the matter by the handle of the wrong that he is doing, because this is the handle by which the matter ought not to be carried; but rather by the other handle—that he is your brother, that you were brought up together, and then you will be laying hold of the matter by the handle by which it ought to be carried.”***
—EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 43

A teammate merges a change that breaks checkout on a Friday evening, and you spend the night rolling it back. By Monday you have a story ready. In it they’re careless, they never test, they always do this.

Epictetus would say you’ve picked the situation up by the wrong handle. The wrong is real, but you can’t carry anything by it. It only gets heavier.

The other handle is still there. This is the person who covered your on-call shift when you were sick, who sat with you through your first incident, who backs you in planning meetings. Pick it up by that handle and the conversation changes. You can still talk about the missing test and the review that should have caught it. You’ll be talking to a teammate instead of a defendant, and the fix is more likely to stick.
