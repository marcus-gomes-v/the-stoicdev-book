## June 22nd BREAK THE CYCLE OF UNCHANGED HABITS

***“If you be once defeated and say that by and by you will overcome, and then a second time do the same thing, know that at last you will be in so wretched a state and so weak that by and by you will not so much as notice that you are doing wrong, but you will even begin to offer arguments in justification of your conduct.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.18.31

The retro has a familiar item. Estimates were off again. Everyone nods, someone writes “be more careful with estimates” on a sticky note, and two sprints later the same note goes up.

Epictetus describes where this leads. You lose once, promise you’ll win next time, then do exactly the same thing. Eventually you stop noticing that anything is wrong, and you start building arguments to defend it: estimates are always wrong in this industry, it’s the product team’s fault.

The way out is to change something concrete after the first defeat, not the fifth. Split the work into smaller tickets. Add a spike before the big story. Write down how far off you were and why. A small change you can test next sprint beats another promise to do better.
