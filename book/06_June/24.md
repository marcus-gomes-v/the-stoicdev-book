June 24th SEEKING PEACE, NOT VICTORY

"The beautiful and good person neither fights with anyone nor, as much as they are able, permits others to fight . . . this is the meaning of getting an education—learning what is your own affair and what is not." 
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.5.1; 7b-8a

In the realm of technology and leadership, where debates can quickly become divisive, the truly educated leaders distinguish themselves by their reluctance to engage in unproductive quarrels. They understand that true education isn't about mastering argumentation or overpowering others with one's intellect; rather, it involves recognizing which battles are worth fighting and which are distractions from more critical objectives. This approach doesn’t just apply to personal interactions but extends to managing teams and projects: emphasizing collaboration over confrontation, and guidance over domination. By fostering a culture where disputes are viewed as opportunities for dialogue rather than contests to be won, leaders can cultivate a more innovative, supportive, and productive workplace.
