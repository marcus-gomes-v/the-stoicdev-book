## June 25th SILENCE IS A SOLUTION

***“This is why we say that nothing befals the wise man which he did not expect—we do not make him exempt from the chances of human life, but from its mistakes, nor does everything happen to him as he wished it would, but as he thought it would.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 13.3

In planning meetings the most confident promise often wins. Two weeks, easy, no risk. The engineer who stays quiet and then says “probably, unless the payment provider gives us trouble” sounds less impressive in the moment.

Seneca would side with the quiet one. The wise person, he says, isn’t spared bad luck. What they’re spared is the mistake of not seeing it coming. Things don’t go the way they wished, but they go roughly the way they thought they might.

That kind of foresight usually comes from keeping quiet long enough to listen. Let the team finish describing the problem. Let the client walk you through the whole workflow before you estimate anything. The risks are usually hiding in the part you would have interrupted. Then, when the provider does give you trouble, nobody is scrambling. You said it might happen, calmly, and the plan is already there.
