## June 27th CHARACTER IN CHALLENGES

***“What boots it, husband, to make woe heavier by lamentation? This very thing, methinks, is regal—to face adversity and, the more dubious thy station and the more the greatness of empire totters to its fall, the more firm to stand, brave with unfaltering foot. ’Tis not a manly thing to turn the back to Fortune.”***
—SENECA, OEDIPUS, 81

In Seneca’s play, Oedipus is lamenting the plague in Thebes when Jocasta cuts him off. What good does it do, she asks, to make trouble heavier by complaining about it? A king faces adversity, and the shakier his position, the firmer he should stand.

You don’t need a throne to recognize the moment. The release is slipping, the client is unhappy, and the team is watching your face to see how bad it really is. If you sigh and list everything that went wrong, the trouble gets heavier for everyone in the room.

A bad week is a stress test, and people learn more about you from it than from any good one. Say plainly what’s broken. Say what you’ll do in the next hour. Stay steady while people are watching, and save the venting for a friend outside the team. Turning your back on a rough week only makes it longer.
