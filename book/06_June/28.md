## June 28th BALANCED SELF-REFLECTION

***“Philosophy asks for frugality, not self-punishment, and a frugal life can still be a tidy one.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 5.5

After a bad incident some engineers punish themselves. They stay late for a month, cancel the weekend, reread the postmortem looking for proof that they aren’t good enough. It looks like accountability. Mostly it’s just suffering.

Seneca was writing to a friend who had thrown himself into philosophy and was at risk of overdoing it: rough clothes, uncut hair, a bed on the floor to prove a point. He told him philosophy asks for a plain life, not a penalty.

Self-reflection works the same way. Look honestly at what went wrong, write down what you’ll change, and then stop. You can be hard on the work and still decent to the person who did it. A simple, tidy review that leads to one real fix is worth more than a week of quiet self-punishment that leads to nothing.
