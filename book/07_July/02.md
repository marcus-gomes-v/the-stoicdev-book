## July 2nd - Duty Above All

***“Let it make no difference to thee whether thou art cold or warm, if thou art doing thy duty; and whether thou art drowsy or satisfied with sleep; and whether ill-spoken of or praised; and whether dying or doing something else. For it is one of the acts of life, this act by which we die; it is sufficient then in this act also to do well what we have in hand.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.2

The pager goes off at 3 a.m. You’re groggy, the apartment is cold, and the first message in the channel blames your team for the outage. None of that changes the job in front of you: find the problem, stop the damage, write down what you did.

Marcus lists the conditions we use as excuses (cold or warm, sleepy or rested, insulted or praised) and sets them all aside. He even puts dying on the list, as one more act of life that can be done well or badly.

This isn’t a case for working sick or skipping sleep. Rest is part of doing the work well. The point is narrower. How well you handle the task in your hands shouldn’t depend on your mood, or on who happens to be watching.
