## July 3rd - Embrace Life’s Opportunities

***“We picture the work of the philosopher to be something like this: He should bring his own will into harmony with what happens, so that neither anything that happens happens against our will, nor anything that fails to happen fails to happen when we wish it to happen.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 2.14.7

There’s a small trick that changes a lot of days: swap “I have to” for “I get to.” I have to fix this flaky test becomes I get to find out why it fails one run in twenty. I have to sit through another planning meeting becomes I get to hear what the business is actually worried about.

Epictetus asks for something harder than a change of wording. He wants your will lined up with what actually happens, so that events stop feeling like something done to you. The release slipped. The library you depend on was abandoned. The reorg moved you to a team you didn’t pick. Fighting these in your head adds a second problem to the first.

Once you stop arguing with the facts, you can work with them. The slipped release buys you a week for the tests you kept postponing. The abandoned library is a good reason to finally read its source. Wanting what happens has less to do with cheerfulness than with where you spend your energy.
