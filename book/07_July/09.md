## July 9th - Leadership Through Wisdom

***“For my part, I think that a good king is, by that very fact and of necessity, a philosopher, and that a philosopher, by that very fact, has something kingly in him.”***
—MUSONIUS RUFUS, LECTURES, 8

Musonius said this to a king, one of the client rulers of Syria who came to visit him. The argument behind it is plain. You can’t be a good ruler without being a good person, and the practice of becoming a good person is what he called philosophy.

Swap king for tech lead and it still holds. You can learn the mechanics of leading a team from a book: one-on-ones, roadmaps, how to run a retro. What the books can’t give you is the judgment underneath. Whether to cover for the engineer who keeps missing deadlines or sit down and talk to them. Whether to tell the director that the date is fiction. How to share out credit when the launch goes well, and blame when it doesn’t.

Those are questions about justice, self-control and courage, and they come up every week. The second half of his sentence matters too. Anyone who has really worked on those questions already has something of a leader in them, title or not. Teams usually notice who that person is long before the org chart does.
