## July 10th - Mastery in the Margins

***“Love the art, poor as it may be, which thou hast learned, and be content with it; and pass through the rest of life like one who has intrusted to the gods with his whole soul all that he has, making thyself neither the tyrant nor the slave of any man.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 4.31

There’s a kind of developer you meet maybe twice in a career. They aren’t on conference stages. They write SQL the query planner seems to enjoy, and their shell scripts handle the edge cases. When they name a function, you never have to open it to know what it does. Ask them about it and they shrug: it’s just the job.

That shrug is close to what Marcus means. Love the art you learned, even if it looks small from the outside. Writing clear code, reading a stack trace, giving an honest estimate. Nobody hands out prizes for these, and they are most of what the work is.

It’s tempting to treat the basics as a stage you pass through on the way to something more impressive, like architecture or management. Then one day you notice you can’t write a decent test anymore, and you’re bluffing your way through reviews.

Marcus adds a second half that’s easy to skip: be neither tyrant nor slave to anyone. A craft you actually love gives you that footing. You don’t need to dominate the meeting to feel competent, and you don’t need the boss’s approval to know the work is good. You can go back to it, a little better each time, and be content there.
