## July 11th - Invest in Yourself

***“But what does Socrates say? ‘As one man rejoices,’ remarks he, ‘in improving his own farm, and another his own horse, so I rejoice day by day in following the course of my own improvement.’”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.5.14

Everyone has something they tend with care. The side project with the immaculate README. The homelab with better uptime than production. The dotfiles repo with four hundred commits. We polish these things for years and enjoy every minute of it.

Socrates, as Epictetus tells it, had the same pleasure, but he aimed it at himself. Day by day he watched how he was getting better, and he enjoyed it the way other men enjoyed a good farm or a fast horse.

Try pointing that care inward for a while. Leave the résumé and the GitHub graph alone and look at the person who writes the code. Notice whether you’re less defensive in reviews than you were last year. Notice whether you can give a straight answer about an estimate where you used to hedge, or sit with a hard bug for an hour without reaching for your phone.

Those are slow improvements, and nobody will star them. Keep a small log if it helps, the way you’d keep a changelog, and read it back in six months. It might turn out to be the project you’re proudest of.
