## July 17th - Balance in Progress: Embrace and Educate

***“As those who try to stand in thy way when thou art proceeding according to right reason will not be able to turn thee aside from thy proper action, so neither let them drive thee from thy benevolent feelings toward them, but be on thy guard equally in both matters, not only in the matter of steady judgment and action, but also in the matter of gentleness to those who try to hinder or otherwise trouble thee. For this also is a weakness, to be vexed at them, as well as to be diverted from thy course of action and to give way through fear; for both are equally deserters from their post,—the man who does it through fear, and the man who is alienated from him who is by nature a kinsman and a friend.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 11.9

Every team has someone who blocks things. The architect who rejects any proposal that isn’t his. The stakeholder who reopens in June a decision you thought was settled in March.

Marcus sees two ways to fail here. You can give up on the work, let the proposal die, stop pushing for the fix. Or you can keep pushing and come to hate the person in the way. He calls both desertion. One man leaves his post out of fear, the other walks away from someone who is, in his words, a kinsman and a friend.

The harder path keeps both. Hold to your judgment about what the system needs, and stay gentle with the person who disagrees. Write the clearer doc. Ask what they’re actually worried about. Escalate if you must, without contempt. Getting angry at them feels like strength, but Marcus files it under weakness.
