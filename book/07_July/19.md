## July 19th - The Power of Understanding

***“Every soul, the philosopher says, is involuntarily deprived of truth; consequently in the same way it is deprived of justice and temperance and benevolence and everything of the kind. It is most necessary to bear this constantly in mind, for thus thou wilt be more gentle towards all.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 7.63

Someone on another team pushes a change that takes your service down for an hour. In the postmortem it turns out they never knew your service depended on that field. Nobody told them, and the doc was three years out of date.

It’s an ordinary version of what Plato said and Marcus copied out: nobody chooses to be wrong. People don’t wake up wanting to be careless or unfair. When they are, it’s usually because something is missing, whether information, experience, sleep or a better example.

That doesn’t mean nothing happens. You still fix the doc and add the contract test, and sometimes you still have a hard conversation. But you go into it differently when you assume the other person would have done better if they’d known better. Your tone changes. So do the meetings, and over time so does the team.

Marcus wanted it in mind constantly, which includes the heat of the moment. It’s easiest to forget in the middle of the incident channel, and that’s exactly where it helps most.
