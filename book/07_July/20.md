## July 20th - Anchored in Justice

***“He who acts unjustly acts impiously. For since the universal nature has made rational animals for the sake of one another, to help one another according to their deserts, but in no way to injure one another, he who transgresses her will is clearly guilty of impiety towards the highest divinity.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 9.1

Injustice at work is rarely dramatic. It’s the quiet stuff. Taking the interesting ticket and leaving the migration to the new hire, every single time. Writing a vague performance review for someone you don’t like. Letting a customer believe a feature exists because the contract is almost signed.

Marcus treats this as close to sacrilege. For him people were made for each other, to help each other according to what each deserves. To harm the people you work with is to go against nature’s own intention, and he saw that as an offense against the highest divinity.

You don’t need his theology to feel the point. A team is a small version of that order: everyone is there to make the others more effective. Each unfair act, even a small one, pulls a thread loose, and people notice faster than you think. Ask of the decisions you make this week whether they give people what they’re due. It’s a simple test, and it catches a lot.
