## July 22nd - The Privilege of Choice

***“Nothing is honorable when it is done by someone unwilling, by someone forced. Every honorable act is a willing one.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 66.16

Two developers fix the same security bug on a Friday afternoon. One does it while complaining in every channel that this isn’t their job and someone else should have caught it. The other does it because users are exposed and they happen to be the person who can fix it fastest. The diff is identical.

Seneca would say only the second did something honorable. The first did something useful, under protest. What makes an act good, for him, is that the will is behind it. Drag it out of yourself with resentment, stalling and fear, and you keep the action but lose the best part of it.

This is less about mood than about ownership. Plenty of tasks land on you that you wouldn’t have picked. You can still choose them. Decide that you’re doing the on-call shift or the boring audit because it needs doing and you’re someone who does what needs doing. Then do it without the running commentary. Once you’ve picked the task up yourself, nobody is forcing your hand.
