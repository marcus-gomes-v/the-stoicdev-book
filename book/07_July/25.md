## July 25th - SUCCESS AT WHAT COST?

***“When, therefore, you see a man often wear the purple robes of office, and hear his name often repeated in the forum, do not envy him: he gains these things by losing so much of his life. ... Some lose their lives during the early part of the struggle, and never reach the height to which they aspired: some after having submitted to a thousand indignities in order to reach the crowning dignity, have the miserable reflexion that the only result of their labours will be the inscription on their tombstone.”***
—SENECA, ON THE BREVITY OF LIFE, 20

You know the name. The principal engineer whose talks everyone watches, whose posts get passed around, who seems to be in every design review that matters. It’s natural to want that.

Seneca asks what it cost. Behind a lot of visible careers are years of missed dinners, a marriage that quietly ended, a body that gave out at forty. Some people burn out halfway up. Some reach the top after years of swallowing their pride in meetings they hated, and find that what they got is a line on a résumé, the modern version of an inscription on a tombstone.

None of this makes ambition bad, or hard work a mistake. It means the price is real, and it gets paid in time, the one thing you can’t earn back. Before you envy someone’s position, look at what they traded for it and ask whether you’d make the same trade.

A few lines later Seneca asks about the people who can’t stop: “Can men find such pleasure in dying in harness?” Your work can be a large part of your life without being all of it.
