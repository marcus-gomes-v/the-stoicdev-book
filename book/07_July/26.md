## July 26th - THE DANGER OF INACTION

***“He often acts unjustly who does not do a certain thing; not only he who does a certain thing.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 9.5

In a code review you notice that a teammate’s query builds SQL straight from user input. You aren’t the assigned reviewer. You approve your own part, close the tab, and tell yourself someone else will catch it.

Marcus would call that injustice. Not the dramatic kind, just the kind that consists of not doing something you could have done. The same goes for the meeting where a colleague’s idea gets credited to someone louder and you say nothing, or the onboarding doc you know is wrong and never fix.

Silence feels neutral because nobody can point to anything you did. But the injection bug ships anyway, and the colleague learns that speaking up in that room doesn’t pay. You were there, you saw it, and you could have changed the outcome.

Speaking up rarely takes a speech. A one-line review comment. A quick “actually, that was Priya’s idea.” A ten-minute fix to the doc. Small acts, done when you notice, are most of what keeps a team fair.
