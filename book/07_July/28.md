## July 28th - PRACTICE PATIENCE AND UNDERSTANDING

***“Some people are quicker and others slower, and some were brought up in better habits, others in worse. Those of poorer character or nature will need more proofs and more work before they accept these teachings and are molded by them, just as, I think, a body in bad condition needs a great deal of care if it is ever to be in good health.”***
—MUSONIUS RUFUS, LECTURES, 1

It’s the third time this week the same colleague has asked how to rebase. You explain it again, a little more slowly than you need to, and when the call ends you catch yourself thinking: how is this still hard for them?

One of Musonius’ students in Rome was Epictetus, then still a slave. Musonius knew people don’t start from the same place. Some are quick. Some had good habits drilled into them early, and some didn’t. The ones who start behind need more explanation and more patience before an idea takes hold, the way a run-down body needs more care before it’s healthy again. He didn’t treat that as a flaw in the student. It was the condition of the patient, and you treat the patient you have.

Think about where your own head start came from. Maybe a parent who owned a computer, or a first job with a senior who reviewed your code line by line. Maybe you just had more free evenings at twenty than the person asking you about rebase. None of that was your doing, any more than their late start was theirs.

So explain it a third time, and make it a better explanation. Write it down so the fourth time is easier. A year from now they may be the one explaining it to the next new hire, and they’ll explain it the way you did.
