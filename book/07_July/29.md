## July 29th - A CURE FOR THE SELF

***“A man who has practiced philosophy to heal himself grows immense in spirit, full of confidence, impossible to defeat, and the closer you get, the bigger he looks.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 111.2

Seneca wrote this in a letter about sophisms, the clever logical puzzles some philosophers loved to trade. People who spend their time on those, he says, get good at puzzles and nothing else. They become no braver and no calmer. Philosophy only changes you if you take it as medicine, on yourself, for the things that are actually wrong with you.

Developers know the trap. You can read every post about clean architecture and still write tangled code under pressure. You can quote the research on psychological safety and still be the person nobody dares to disagree with in a review. Knowing about a thing and being changed by it are different, and the gap is easy to hide, even from yourself.

The medicine is less glamorous. Pick one thing that keeps going wrong. Maybe you get defensive when someone questions your design. Maybe you promise dates you know are fiction because saying no feels worse. Read what the Stoics say about it, then practice on the next real occasion, and the one after that.

Seneca compares the result to a mountain. From far away its height doesn’t impress you, and up close you see how steep it really is. People who have done this work are like that. They don’t show off. You notice them in the meeting where everything is going wrong, when they’re the only one still thinking clearly, and you realize how much is there.
