## August 4th - SHIFT YOUR FOCUS

***“In all that pertains to yourself directly you must change completely from your present practices, and must cease to blame God or man; you must utterly wipe out desire, and must turn your aversion toward the things which lie within the province of the moral purpose, and these only; you must feel no anger, no rage, no envy, no pity...”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.22.13

Read a few postmortems and you’ll notice how much of them is about other people. The vendor changed the API without warning. Product rushed the spec. All of that may be true, and none of it tells you what to do next.

Epictetus is describing what it takes to live as a philosopher, and the first step is the hard one: stop blaming God or anyone else. Then point your attention at the only place where you have real say, which is your own judgment and what you do with it.

He puts anger and envy on the list of things to drop. That sounds like a lot to ask until you count what they cost you in a single week: the long rant in a private channel, the evening spent comparing your title with someone else’s. That time could go into the section of the postmortem that starts with what you will change, written honestly.
