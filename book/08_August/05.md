## August 5th - THE POWER OF SILENCE

***“The art of silence is taught by life’s many ills.”***
—SENECA, THYESTES, 319

Junior engineers tend to answer every question in the room. What’s the estimate? Two days. Why did the deploy fail? Probably the cache. They’re trying to help, and they’re often wrong in front of thirty people.

The line comes from a dark play. Atreus is plotting revenge and decides not to tell his young sons, because they’re too young to keep a secret. Silence, he says, is something you learn from trouble. That holds outside the play too. The senior people who say little on incident calls usually have a reason. They’ve watched a confident guess send a whole team down the wrong path at 3 a.m.

You don’t need years of bad nights to learn it. Before you speak in the next meeting, ask yourself whether you know or whether you’re guessing. If you’re guessing, say so, or wait until you’ve checked the logs. You’ll say fewer things, and people will start trusting the ones you do say.
