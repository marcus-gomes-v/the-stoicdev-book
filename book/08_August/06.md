## August 6th - FINDING ROOM TO MANEUVER

***“Call good sense to your aid against difficulties: it is possible to soften what is harsh, to widen what is too narrow, and to make heavy burdens press less severely upon one who bears them skilfully.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 10.4

Two weeks to launch, one backend developer out sick, and a vendor API that allows a hundred requests a minute. On Monday it looks impossible, and the first instinct is to say so, loudly, in the planning meeting.

Seneca’s advice is to think before you despair. Just before this line he talks about small rooms. Badly arranged, they’re useless. Arranged with some thought, even a cramped one becomes livable. Most hard situations at work are like that. They have more room in them than the first look suggests.

So you start looking for the room. Some features can ship behind a flag and be finished next sprint. The API calls can be batched, or cached for an hour without any user noticing. Your sick colleague’s piece may not need to land in this release at all. None of this makes the problem disappear. Each change makes it a little lighter to carry.

The people who seem to pull off the impossible are mostly the ones who keep looking for that extra inch after everyone else in the room has stopped.
