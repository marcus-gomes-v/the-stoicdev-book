## August 7th - PRINCIPLES IN PRACTICE

***“Where a man can live, there he can also live well. But he must live in a palace; well then, he can also live well in a palace.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 5.16

There’s a story developers tell themselves: I’d write better code, and be a better colleague, somewhere else. Somewhere without the politics and the promo committees that reward whoever demos loudest. Here, you just have to play the game.

Marcus wrote this as an emperor. The palace was where he lived, with its flatterers and schemers, and he could have used it as an excuse. Instead he told himself that wherever a person can live, a person can live well, and that this included the palace.

Your palace might be a big company with six layers of approval, or a startup where every week brings a new top priority. You can still write the honest estimate. You can still give credit to the person who actually found the bug, review code carefully when nobody is checking, and say no to the shortcut that would hurt users. The setting makes all of that harder. It doesn’t make it impossible.
