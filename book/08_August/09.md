August 9th - STICK WITH JUST THE FACTS

“Don’t tell yourself anything more than what the initial impressions report. It’s been reported to you that someone is speaking badly about you. This is the report—the report wasn’t that you’ve been harmed. I see that my son is sick—but not that his life is at risk. So always stay within your first impressions, and don’t add to them in your head—this way nothing can happen to you.” 
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.49

In the fast-paced world of technology, it's easy to let our minds race ahead of us. A minor issue in the code becomes a catastrophic failure in our minds, or a colleague's criticism spirals into doubts about our competence. Marcus Aurelius's advice is to stick with just the facts—observe the initial impression and refrain from adding unnecessary layers of interpretation.

This approach is invaluable in tech. A bug report is just that: a report of a problem. It’s not an indictment of your skills or a harbinger of your project's doom. By keeping our assessments factual and straightforward, we can maintain clarity and objectivity. This prevents us from being overwhelmed by imagined catastrophes and keeps our focus on practical solutions.

Today, practice this straightforward pragmatism. When faced with challenges or feedback, stick to the facts. Avoid dramatizing or speculating. This will help you stay grounded, make clearer decisions, and maintain your peace of mind amidst the inevitable ups and downs of your work.
