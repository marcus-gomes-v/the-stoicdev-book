## August 9th - STICK WITH JUST THE FACTS

***“Say nothing more to thyself than what the first appearances report. Suppose that it has been reported to thee that a certain person speaks ill of thee. This has been reported; but that thou hast been injured, that has not been reported. I see that my child is sick. I do see; but that he is in danger, I do not see. Thus then always abide by the first appearances, and add nothing thyself from within, and then nothing happens to thee.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.49

A message from your manager at 4:55 p.m.: “Got a minute tomorrow morning?” By 5:10 you’ve decided it’s about last week’s incident, that your review is in trouble, and that you should probably update your CV tonight.

Look at what was actually reported. Your manager wants a minute tomorrow. That’s all the message says. Everything else came from you.

Marcus practiced this with things far heavier than a calendar invite. He had many children and lost several of them young, so “my child is sick” was not an abstract example for him. Even there he kept apart what he could see and what he was adding to it. The child is sick, yes. That the child is in danger, he does not see.

An alert works the same way. “Error rate above 2%” is the report. “We’re losing customers and it’s my deploy” is a story you wrote in two seconds. Open the dashboard first. Check what changed. Respond to the facts in front of you, and let the story wait until there’s some evidence for it.
