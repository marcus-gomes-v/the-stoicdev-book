## August 12th - OWN YOUR ACTIONS, NOT JUST YOUR WORDS

***“Everything they say, everything they parade before an audience, belongs to others. Plato said it, Zeno said it, Chrysippus and Posidonius said it, and a long column of names as many as they are great. I will show you how they can prove it is theirs: let them do what they have said.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 108.38

Some code reviews read like a bibliography. Every comment cites a famous book, a famous talk, a famous blog post. The citations are usually right. The person leaving them often owns a service with no tests and a config file full of secrets.

Seneca is talking about philosophers who recite Plato and Zeno to an audience. Those words, he says, still belong to Plato and Zeno. The only way to make them yours is to do what they say. He knew the charge from the inside. He wrote about simple living while he was one of the richest men in Rome, and his critics never let him forget it.

There’s nothing wrong with borrowing. Nearly everything we know came from someone else’s book or someone else’s code. But a principle you’ve only quoted isn’t yours yet. It becomes yours on the day you follow it when it’s inconvenient: you write the test under a deadline, or you split the function even though it already works. After that, quote whoever you like.
