## August 14th - PHILOSOPHY IS FOR LIVING, NOT FOR SHOW

***“Philosophy is not a craft for pleasing the crowd, nor something put together for show; its business is with things, not words. We do not use it to while away the day with some amusement, or to take the tedium out of our free time. It shapes the mind and builds it, puts life in order, governs our actions, points out what must be done and what must be left alone; it sits at the helm and steers the wavering through dangerous waters. Without it nobody can live fearlessly, nobody can live at ease. Every hour brings countless things that call for counsel, and that counsel has to be asked of philosophy.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 16.3

It’s easy to turn Stoicism into decoration. A quote on a slide, a book on the desk. Seneca would be unimpressed, and that includes a book like this one, if all you do is read it.

His picture is a ship’s helm. Philosophy is supposed to be in your hands when the water gets rough, and at work the water gets rough several times a day. A reviewer leaves a condescending comment on your pull request. Your manager asks you to promise a date you know is fiction. You find a bug in your own code from last month, and nobody else has noticed it yet.

Each of those is a small decision with a better and a worse answer, and none of them comes with a warning. That’s when the reading is supposed to pay off. If a page in the morning helps you answer that reviewer calmly and own the bug before someone else finds it, it has done its job. If it only made you feel wise for a minute, it was entertainment, and Seneca says philosophy is not for passing the time.
