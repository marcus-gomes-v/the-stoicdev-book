## August 15th - THE SUPREME COURT OF YOUR MIND

***“It can be handed on quickly, in a handful of words: the only good is virtue; there is certainly no good without virtue; and virtue itself is seated in the better part of us, the part that reasons. What, then, will this virtue be? Judgment that is true and unshaken. From it the impulses of the mind will come, and by it every impression that sets an impulse moving will be made clear.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 71.32

Seneca packs the whole of Stoic ethics into a few lines here, and the part worth slowing down for is the definition at the end. Virtue, he says, is judgment that is true and doesn’t move.

A developer’s day is full of impressions that push you to act. A new framework is trending, and your stack suddenly feels old. A friend’s pull request has a real problem, and the impulse is to approve it anyway because you like them. Each impression arrives with its own urgency. None of them arrives with a verdict.

The verdict is your job. Think of your judgment as a court that every impression has to pass through before it’s allowed to act. Does the framework solve a problem you actually have? Would you approve this code from a stranger? A court that rules one way on Monday and the other way on Friday is no use to anyone, so steadiness matters as much as being right.

People notice it, too. The engineer whose “yes” means the same thing every time, in a calm week or in a release crunch, is the one the team trusts with the hard calls.
