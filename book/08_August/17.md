## August 17th - TAKE RESPONSIBILITY

***“For nothing outside the sphere of the moral purpose can hamper or injure the moral purpose; it alone can hamper or injure itself. If, then, we too tend in this latter direction so that, whenever we go amiss, we blame ourselves, and bear in mind that nothing but judgement is responsible for the disturbance of our peace of mind and our inconstancy, I swear to you by all the gods that we have been making progress.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.19.2-3

Epictetus opens this discourse with a simple test. The ordinary person says “woe is me” because of a child, a brother, a father. The philosopher, if he can ever be made to say it, adds after a pause: “because of myself.”

Try it on a bad sprint. The feature shipped late because the designer changed the mockups and the backend team broke the contract. Maybe so. Now run it again with yourself in the sentence. The ticket I wrote was vague. I saw the contract change in the changelog and said nothing.

This isn’t the same as beating yourself up, and it doesn’t clash with blameless postmortems. Blameless is how you treat other people. Epictetus is talking about how you treat your own mind. The only thing that can really be damaged here is your judgment, and only you can damage it. Get in the habit of looking there first, he says, and you are making progress. He swears to it by all the gods.
