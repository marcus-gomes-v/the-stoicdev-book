## August 18th - CHOOSE YOUR BATTLES WISELY

***“The good man is invincible; naturally, for he enters no contest where he is not superior. ‘If you want my property in the country,’ says he, ‘take it; take my servants, take my office, take my paltry body. But you will not make my desire fail to get what I will, nor my aversion fall into what I would avoid.’ This is the only contest into which the good man enters, one, namely, that is concerned with the things which belong in the province of the moral purpose; how, then, can he help but be invincible?”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.6.5–7

Some fights at work can’t be won by anyone in your position. The VP has already picked the vendor. The reorg was decided in a room you weren’t invited to. You can spend weeks arguing about these, and plenty of people do.

Epictetus’s good person is invincible for a slightly sneaky reason: they only enter contests they can’t lose. Take my property, take my office, take my body, he says. You still can’t make me want what I don’t want, or walk into what I’ve decided to avoid. Those are the only matches he signs up for.

At work, that contest is your own conduct. Whether your code review is fair. Whether you tell the truth about the estimate. Whether you do good work on the vendor you didn’t choose. Nobody can take those from you, and nobody can beat you at them. Put your energy there, and the fights you used to lose start to matter less than you thought.
