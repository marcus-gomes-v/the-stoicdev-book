## August 19th - SIMPLIFY TO FOCUS

***“Occupy thyself with few things, says the philosopher, if thou wouldst be tranquil.—But consider if it would not be better to say, Do what is necessary, and whatever the reason of the animal which is naturally social requires, and as it requires. For this brings not only the tranquillity which comes from doing well, but also that which comes from doing few things. For the greatest part of what we say and do being unnecessary, if a man takes this away, he will have more leisure and less uneasiness. Accordingly, on every occasion a man should ask himself, Is this one of the unnecessary things? Now a man should take away not only unnecessary acts, but also unnecessary thoughts, for thus superfluous acts will not follow after.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 4.24

Look at yesterday’s calendar. A status meeting that could have been two lines in a channel. Forty minutes polishing a script you’ll run once. None of it was terrible, and most of it wasn’t needed.

Marcus takes an old piece of advice, do few things if you want peace, and improves it. Fewer things is fine, but he would rather you did what is actually required and did it well. Cut the rest, and you get two kinds of calm at once: the calm of a short list and the calm of good work.

His test is a question you can ask a dozen times a day: is this one of the unnecessary things? It works on meeting invites and on the refactor you started to avoid the real task. It also works on thoughts, the part people skip. The argument you keep rehearsing with an absent colleague is usually what produces the unnecessary message at 11 p.m.
