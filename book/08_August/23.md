August 23rd - APPEAL TO REASON, NOT MORALIZING

“Therefore, explain why a wise person shouldn’t get drunk—not with words, but by the facts of its ugliness and offensiveness. It’s most easy to prove that so-called pleasures, when they go beyond proper measure, are but punishments.” 
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 83.27

In the tech world, where collaboration and innovation thrive on persuasion and teamwork, moralizing rarely inspires change. Instead of lecturing or appealing to abstract principles, focus on showing the tangible benefits of a different approach. For example, instead of saying, "We should use this methodology because it's the right way," explain how it will save time, reduce errors, or improve performance—outcomes that align with everyone's self-interest.

The same principle applies to your own habits. Rather than guilt-tripping yourself into adopting better practices, focus on the practical benefits: How will this change make your life easier, your work smoother, or your health better? Recognize that unchecked indulgences, like overworking or skipping exercise, ultimately lead to burnout or diminishing returns.

Today, whether influencing others or improving yourself, focus on clear, practical reasons for change. Show, don’t moralize. Demonstrate how the change serves the greater good—and everyone’s self-interest.
