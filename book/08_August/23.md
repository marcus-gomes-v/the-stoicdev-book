## August 23rd - APPEAL TO REASON, NOT MORALIZING

***“Then say why a wise man should not get drunk, and show how ugly and obnoxious the thing is with facts, not with words. That is the easiest thing to do: prove that what people call pleasures turn into punishments once they go past their limit.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 83.27

Seneca is reacting here to a clever argument. Zeno had tried to prove, with a neat little syllogism, that a good man would never get drunk. Seneca found the logic weak and the whole approach wrong. If you want to persuade someone, show them what drunkenness actually does.

That’s good advice for anyone trying to change a team’s habits. “We should write tests because it’s best practice” convinces nobody who doesn’t already agree. “Four of our last six incidents came from the two modules with no tests” starts a very different conversation. Show the facts about the thing itself and skip the sermon.

The same goes for your own habits. The late-night coding binge feels great at the time, and so does the third coffee and the weekend spent on a side project instead of sleeping. Past a certain point they stop being pleasures and become the reason you’re short-tempered in Monday’s standup. You don’t need to scold yourself about it. Just look honestly at what it’s costing you.
