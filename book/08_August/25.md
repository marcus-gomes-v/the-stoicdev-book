## August 25th - HONOR THE OLD, INNOVATE THE NEW

***“‘Then am I not to walk in the tracks of those before me?’ I will take the old road, yes, but if I find a shorter, more level one, I will build that one. Those who opened up these questions before us are guides, not masters. Truth lies open to all; no one has claimed it yet.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 33.11

Every codebase has its founders. They chose the database, the folder structure, the strange retry logic in the payment service that nobody dares to touch. Usually they had good reasons, and you’ll save yourself a lot of pain by finding out what those reasons were before you change anything.

But Seneca draws a clear line. The people who came before are guides, and guides can be wrong. Their decisions were answers to the problems they had, with the tools that existed then. If you find a shorter, smoother road, build it.

The skill is doing both at once. Read the old commit messages and ask the person who wrote the retry logic. Then, if the better way still holds up, write the proposal. Nobody owns the truth about how this system should work, not the founders and not you either.
