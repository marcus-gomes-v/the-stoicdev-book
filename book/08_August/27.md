## August 27th - FIND HUMOR IN CHALLENGES

***“We should imitate Democritus rather than Heraclitus. The latter of these, whenever he appeared in public, used to weep, the former to laugh: the one thought all human doings to be follies, the other thought them to be miseries. We must take a higher view of all things, and bear with them more easily: it better becomes a man to scoff at life than to lament over it.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 15.2

Every team has the story. The demo that failed only on the CEO’s laptop. The monthly job someone scheduled for the 31st, which turned out to run less often than expected. The commit titled “final fix,” followed the next morning by “final fix 2.”

You can tell these stories two ways. As Heraclitus, weeping: look at this mess, nothing here works. Or as Democritus, laughing: look at us, a room full of smart people outwitted by a calendar. Seneca votes for laughing, and a few lines later he gives a good reason. The person who laughs at people still hopes they’ll improve. The one who weeps has given up on them.

That doesn’t mean joking during the outage while customers wait. It means that afterwards, in the retro, a little laughter keeps people honest. Nobody admits a mistake in a room that feels like a funeral.
