## August 28th - LUXURY WITHOUT DEPENDENCE

***“The builder of the universe, who wrote out for us the laws of living, arranged things so that we would be sound, not spoiled. Everything that keeps us healthy is prepared and within reach; everything luxury wants is scraped together with misery and worry. Let us use this kindness of nature, then, and count it among the great ones.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 119.15

Tech pays well, and the money tends to rearrange your life without asking. The bigger apartment, the vacations that need two weeks of planning. None of it is wrong. Then a manager you can’t stand arrives, and you realize you can’t leave, because the next vesting date is eight months away and the mortgage was sized for this salary.

Seneca was one of the richest men in Rome, so he isn’t preaching poverty here. His point is that what keeps you well is cheap and close at hand. What luxury needs has to be chased and then protected, and the worry is the price nobody puts on the label.

A useful exercise: work out what your life actually costs if you strip it back to what keeps you healthy and content. For many developers that number is far below their salary. Knowing it changes how you walk into a hard conversation with your boss. You can still enjoy the nice things. You just don’t have to obey them.
