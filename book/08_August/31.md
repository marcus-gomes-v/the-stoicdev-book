## August 31st - EXTEND THE GRACE YOU GIVE YOURSELF

***“Whenever you take offense at someone’s wrongdoing, immediately turn to your own similar failings, such as seeing money as good, or pleasure, or a little fame—whatever form it takes. By thinking on this, you’ll quickly forget your anger, considering also what compels them—for what else could they do? Or, if you are able, remove their compulsion.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 10.30

In tech, as in life, frustrations with others can often escalate quickly. A colleague missed a deadline, pushed buggy code, or failed to communicate. It’s easy to brand such behaviors as careless or thoughtless. But Marcus Aurelius reminds us to pause and reflect on our own shortcomings. How many times have you acted out of exhaustion, stress, or simple oversight? 

Instead of focusing on others’ mistakes, extend the same understanding you’d hope to receive. This isn’t about excusing negligence but recognizing the human conditions that drive errors. Use this perspective to foster collaboration and constructive feedback, rather than resentment or conflict.

Today, when you feel offended or frustrated, turn inward first. Acknowledge your own past missteps and approach others with empathy. This practice doesn’t just ease tensions; it builds stronger, more productive relationships.
