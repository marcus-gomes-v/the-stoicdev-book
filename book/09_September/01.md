September 1st - A STRONG SOUL OUTLASTS FORTUNE

“The rational soul is stronger than any kind of fortune—from its own share it guides its affairs here or there, and is itself the cause of a happy or miserable life.” 
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 98.2b

Cato the Younger lived a life of deliberate discipline. Though he could afford luxury, he chose simplicity—walking barefoot through Rome, eating plain food, and braving harsh weather bareheaded. These choices weren’t acts of deprivation but of training. Cato was cultivating a resilient soul, indifferent to external circumstances, so he could thrive in any situation.

In the tech world, external conditions—tight deadlines, shifting priorities, or unexpected failures—are beyond your control. But like Cato, you can train yourself to endure and adapt. Face challenges without complaint, embrace simplicity where possible, and practice calm indifference to things outside your influence. By strengthening your inner self, you prepare for whatever comes, ensuring your happiness and focus aren’t dictated by external fortune.
