## September 2nd - PHILOSOPHY AS REHAB FOR THE SOUL

***“Men, the lecture-room of the philosopher is a hospital; you ought not to walk out of it in pleasure, but in pain. For you are not well when you come; one man has a dislocated shoulder, another an abscess, another a fistula, another a headache.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.23.30

The code review that helps you most is rarely the one that feels good. It’s the one where a senior engineer points at the function you were proud of and asks why it does four things. You close the tab a little stung. Two weeks later you notice you’ve stopped writing functions like that.

Epictetus learned from Musonius Rufus, who spoke so directly that each student felt someone must have gone to Rufus and told him their faults. That’s what he means by a hospital. We all come in hurt somewhere. One of us gets defensive in retros, another can’t say no to a manager, another quietly assumes every bug belongs to someone else. A teacher who only offers clever ideas lets you leave with the same injury you walked in with.

So be wary of the talks and threads that only make you feel smart. Look for the feedback that presses on the sore spot, and stay long enough for it to work.
