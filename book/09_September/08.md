## September 8th - STAY STEADY, COME WHAT MAY

***“No man loses anything by the frowns of Fortune unless he has been deceived by her smiles... but he who has not been puffed up by success, does not collapse after failure: he possesses a mind of tried constancy, superior to the influences of either state; for even in the midst of prosperity he has experimented upon his powers of enduring adversity.”***
—SENECA, ON CONSOLATION TO HELVIA, 5.4-6

Seneca wrote these lines to his mother from exile on Corsica. A few years earlier he had been one of the most admired speakers in Rome. He knew both of the faces of fortune he describes here.

We see the same pattern on a smaller scale. The startup is growing, the stock is up, your team can do no wrong, and slowly you start to believe you are the reason. Then the market turns, the round falls through, layoffs arrive. The people who fall hardest are usually the ones who took the good years as proof of who they were.

Seneca doesn’t ask you to be gloomy when things go well. He asks you to hold success loosely, and to test yourself quietly while it’s easy. Live for a month on a smaller budget. Notice whether you still respect your own work when nobody is praising it. If you’ve done that in the good years, the bad ones have much less to take from you.
