## September 9th - FEAR MAKES EVERYTHING WORSE

***“Yet if we fear as much as it is possible to fear, there is no point in living, and no end to our misery.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 13.12

Your manager sends a message at 4:52 p.m.: “Got a minute tomorrow morning?” No context. By nine you’ve decided it’s about last week’s outage, that you’re headed for a performance plan, maybe worse. You sleep badly. In the morning she wants to ask whether you’d mentor the new hire.

Seneca describes this in the same letter: the mind inventing false pictures, twisting an ambiguous word toward its worst meaning. If you let fear grow to the full size of everything that could happen, he says, it never stops. There is always one more thing that might go wrong.

A plainer question helps. What do I actually know right now? Usually very little, and the little you know is rarely as bad as the story you’ve built around it. Deal with what’s in front of you and let tomorrow’s meeting be tomorrow’s.
