## September 10th - PRACTICE FOR TOUGHER DAYS

***“Still, I am so eager to test the firmness of your mind that I will give you a rule that great men have given before me: set aside a few days in which you are content with very little and very cheap food, and with rough, coarse clothing, and say to yourself, ‘Is this what I was afraid of?’ It is in the midst of safety that the mind should get ready for hardship, and amid fortune’s favors it should harden itself against her injuries. The soldier drills in the middle of peace; with no enemy in sight he throws up a rampart and wears himself out with needless labor, so that he will be equal to the labor that is needed.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 18.5-6

Seneca’s advice here is practical. Every so often, live for a few days on plain food and rough clothes, and ask yourself whether this is what you were so afraid of. He wanted you to meet the feared thing while life was still comfortable, the way his soldier builds a rampart with no enemy in sight.

Teams that run game days do the same. They kill a database replica on a calm Wednesday afternoon, with coffee and everyone awake, to see what breaks. It feels like wasted effort, since nothing is on fire. But on the night a real failure comes, the runbook has been tested and nobody is reading it for the first time.

Try it on your own life. Work for a week without the tool you lean on most. Look at what your budget would be if the job ended. Most of what we dread turns out to be survivable once we’ve stood in it, and peacetime is the cheapest place to learn that.
