September 12th - STAY GROUNDED

“Zeno always said that nothing was more unbecoming than putting on airs, especially with the young.” 
—DIOGENES LAERTIUS, LIVES OF THE EMINENT PHILOSOPHERS, 7.1.22

In the tech world, it’s easy to let early success or expertise inflate your ego. The young developer who believes their coding is flawless or the newly promoted manager who assumes they know better than the team—these are common archetypes, and their stories often end the same way: with a humbling fall.

Overconfidence isn’t strength—it’s a liability. By staying humble and approachable, you not only avoid unnecessary confrontations but also position yourself to learn and grow. As Isocrates advised, affability and humility make relationships easier and keep arrogance in check.

Today, reflect on your attitude toward others. Are you open to feedback, willing to admit mistakes, and eager to collaborate? Stay grounded, keep learning, and let your actions speak louder than any airs you could put on. In doing so, you’ll cultivate respect and avoid the hard lessons that pride so often brings.
