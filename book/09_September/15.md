## September 15th - CULTIVATE PHILOSOPHY QUIETLY

***“Practise first not to let men know who you are; keep your philosophy to yourself a little while. That is the way fruit is produced: the seed has to be buried and hidden for a season, and be grown by slow degrees, in order that it may come to perfection. But if it heads out before it produces the jointed stock, it never matures... That is the kind of plant you are too; you have blossomed prematurely, and the winter will blight you utterly.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.8.35-37

Three weeks into learning Rust, it’s tempting to write the thread about why everyone should rewrite everything in it. A month after your first management course, it’s tempting to correct how your peers run their one-on-ones. We want to show the fruit before the plant has roots.

Epictetus saw real danger in this. Talking about a skill too early commits you to positions you don’t yet understand. Every public claim makes it harder to admit what you don’t know, and the first real winter, a nasty production bug or a pointed question in an interview, shows how shallow the roots were.

So learn quietly for a while. Build small things and break them, read other people’s code, keep notes nobody sees. When the work gets good, people notice without being told. And you’ll have something solid underneath you when the frost comes.
