## September 15th - CULTIVATE PHILOSOPHY QUIETLY

***“First practice not letting people know who you are—keep your philosophy to yourself for a bit. In just the manner that fruit is produced—the seed buried for a season, hidden, growing gradually so it may come to full maturity. But if the grain sprouts before the stalk is fully developed, it will never ripen. . . . That is the kind of plant you are, displaying fruit too soon, and the winter will kill you.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.8.35b-37

In tech, as in life, it's tempting to flaunt what you’ve learned. You might be eager to show off your new coding skills, leadership insights, or problem-solving techniques. But Epictetus reminds us that true mastery takes time and showing off too soon risks stalling your growth.

Think of your philosophy, your skills, and your personal growth as a garden. Tend to it patiently, quietly letting the roots take hold. Focus on the work itself, not on seeking validation or applause. When the time comes, your growth will naturally bear fruit results that speak for themselves, without the need for performance or posturing.

Today, resist the urge to rush. Focus on nurturing your craft and your character. The most impactful growth happens out of sight, and the most lasting success comes from deep, unseen preparation. Let your work, over time, reveal who you truly are.
