## September 16th - GREATNESS IS EARNED, NOT GIVEN

***“Prosperity comes to the mob, and to low-minded men as well as to great ones; but it is the privilege of great men alone to send under the yoke the disasters and terrors of mortal life: whereas to be always prosperous, and to pass through life without a twinge of mental distress, is to remain ignorant of one half of nature.”***
—SENECA, ON PROVIDENCE, 4.1

Good luck isn’t picky. It lands on careful engineers and careless ones alike. Someone joins a startup the year before it’s acquired. Someone’s side project takes off because the right person happened to share it. Seneca isn’t bitter about this. He just doesn’t think it tells you much about anyone.

What tells you something is how a person handles the disasters. Romans made a defeated army march under a yoke of spears, and that’s his image: the great person makes the trouble bow, not the other way round. Think of the lead who keeps a team calm and honest through a brutal quarter, or the developer who owns a bad migration and fixes it in the open.

If your career has been smooth so far, he’d say you’ve only seen half of how things work. The other half will come. It won’t feel like a gift when it arrives, but it’s the half that teaches.
