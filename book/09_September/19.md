## September 19th - STRENGTH THROUGH ADAPTABILITY

***“Remember that to change thy opinion and to follow him who corrects thy error is as consistent with freedom as it is to persist in thy error. For it is thy own, the activity which is exerted according to thy own movement and judgment, and indeed according to thy own understanding too.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.16

You argued for the event-driven design in the architecture review. You wrote the doc, drew the diagrams, defended it for forty minutes. Then a quieter engineer asked how you’d handle ordering across partitions, and you realized you didn’t have a good answer.

There’s a pull, in that moment, to keep defending. Backing down feels like losing, as if someone else got to decide for you. Marcus saw it the other way. Changing your mind because someone showed you a better argument is still your decision. You looked at the evidence and you judged. That’s as free as anything you’ll do all week.

Stubbornness looks like strength, but often it just means the old opinion is making your decisions for you. Say “good point, I hadn’t thought of that,” update the doc, and thank the person who caught it. People remember that longer than they remember who was right.
