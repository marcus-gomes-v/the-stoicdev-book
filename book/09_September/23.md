## September 23rd - THE UNBREAKABLE MIND

***“Remember that the ruling faculty is invincible, when self-collected it is satisfied with itself, if it does nothing which it does not choose to do, even if it resist from mere obstinacy. What then will it be when it forms a judgment about anything aided by reason and deliberately? Therefore the mind which is free from passions is a citadel, for man has nothing more secure to which he can fly for refuge and for the future be inexpugnable.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.48

Marcus makes an odd concession here. Even a stubborn mind, one that refuses out of plain obstinacy, can’t be forced. Nobody can make you agree. Then he asks what that same mind could do if its refusals were careful and reasoned instead.

That’s the difference between two engineers who both say no in a design review. One says no because it isn’t their idea. The other says no because they’ve read the incident history and can explain exactly where the new approach would fail. Both are hard to move. Only the second is worth listening to, and only the second can stay calm about it.

He calls a mind free from passion a citadel, a place to retreat to when everything outside is loud. You build it the slow way. Check your reasons before you dig in, notice when anger is doing the arguing, and practice on small decisions so the habit is there for the big ones.
