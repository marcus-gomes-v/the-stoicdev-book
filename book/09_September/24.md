## September 24th - PREPARE FOR THE UNEXPECTED

***“Things we did not expect press down on us harder; novelty adds weight to a calamity, and no one has ever been hit by something that astonished him without suffering more for it. So we must never be caught off guard. The mind has to be sent on ahead to everything, and we should think not of what usually happens, but of whatever can happen. For what is there that fortune, whenever she likes, cannot drag down at the very height of its flourishing?”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 91.3–4

Seneca wrote this letter after fire destroyed the city of Lyon in a single night. His friend Liberalis came from there and was shattered by it. Seneca’s point isn’t that the loss would have been small if anyone had expected it. Surprise puts a second weight on top of the first.

Anyone who has been on call knows this. The outage you rehearsed is stressful. The outage nobody imagined, the whole region going dark, the certificate that expired on a holiday, the vendor who quietly retired the API, is the one that leaves people frozen on the call.

So send your mind on ahead, as he puts it. In design reviews, ask what happens when a dependency is gone, not just slow. Write the runbook for the failure that seems silly. This is less gloomy than it sounds. You’re only making sure that when fortune knocks something over, you’ve already pictured where the pieces will land.
