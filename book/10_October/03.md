## October 3rd - RECOGNIZING OUR INTERCONNECTEDNESS

***“Frequently consider the connection of all things in the universe and their relation to one another. For in a manner all things are implicated with one another, and all in this way are friendly to one another; for one thing comes in order after another, and this is by virtue of the active movement and mutual conspiration and the unity of the substance.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.38

Open the dependency graph of any real system and it looks like a ball of yarn. A one-line change to a date helper breaks billing, the mobile app and a report the finance team runs every Monday morning. Nothing in that codebase lives alone, even the parts that pretend to.

People are wired together the same way. Your pull request sits in someone’s review queue. Their review depends on how much sleep they got after last night’s page. The support engineer explaining your feature to an angry customer is working from the docs you did or didn’t write.

When a deadline is close, it’s easy to see only your own ticket. Marcus told himself to think often about how everything is tied to everything else, and he meant it as a habit, something to come back to on ordinary days. Before you merge, before you snap at someone in a thread, follow the line one step further and look at who is on the other end.
