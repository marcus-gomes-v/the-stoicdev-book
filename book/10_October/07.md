## October 7th - BE GOOD FOR YOUR OWN SAKE

***“He who does wrong does wrong against himself. He who acts unjustly acts unjustly to himself, because he makes himself bad.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 9.4

Nobody noticed. You marked the flaky test as skipped so the release could go out, wrote “known issue” in the ticket and moved on. Or you let your manager believe the clever fix was all yours, when half of it came from a colleague’s comment on the pull request.

Maybe nothing bad ever happens. That’s the part Marcus wants you to look at. The harm he means isn’t getting caught. It’s what the act turns you into. The next shortcut is easier, because you are now someone who takes them. The next half-truth comes out more smoothly. You start checking who might be watching before you decide what’s right.

A wrong done to someone else leaves its first mark on the person who does it. So the honest status update, the credit given where it belongs, the bug you report even though it’s embarrassing: none of these are favors to the team. They are how you stay the developer you actually want to be.
