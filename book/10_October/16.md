## October 16th - SHARE WISDOM THROUGH ACTION

***“Hence those minds so quick to seize virtue, or fertile enough to bring it forth themselves. But in the dull and the blunted, or in those held fast by bad habit, the rust of the soul has to be rubbed off over a long time. And whoever hands them the principles of philosophy will lead those already leaning toward the good more quickly to the top, and will help the weaker ones and pull them out of their bad opinions.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 95.36-37

Every team has someone who picks things up absurdly fast. Hand them a new framework on Monday and by Wednesday they’re answering questions in the channel. And every team has someone who has been stuck on the same kind of bug for months, a little more embarrassed each time.

Seneca’s point is that the second person isn’t hopeless. They need more time with the basics, and someone patient enough to hand those basics over.

That someone can be you. Not with a lecture or a link to a blog post, but by pairing for an hour, by explaining why the fix works and not just what it is, by asking what they think before you tell them what you’d do. The fast learner will be fine either way. The slow one is where your help actually changes something, and teaching it will sharpen your own understanding more than you expect.
