## October 17th - THE POWER OF KINDNESS

***“A benefit should be invested in the same manner as a treasure buried deep in the earth, which you would not dig up unless actually obliged... Nature bids me do good to mankind... Wherever there is a human being, there is an opportunity for a benefit.”***
—SENECA, ON THE HAPPY LIFE, 24.2-3

Seneca’s image is a strange one at first. A good turn should be buried like treasure, deep enough that you won’t dig it up unless you truly have to.

Most of us do the opposite. We help a colleague fix their broken environment on a Friday evening and then mention it twice in the next retro. We review someone’s code carefully and quietly expect the same care on ours. When it doesn’t come, the favor turns sour.

He also says the chances are everywhere. Wherever there is a person, there is room for a kindness: the contractor nobody invites to the team lunch, the support engineer who forwards the bug report at midnight, the intern too shy to ask where the staging credentials live. Rank doesn’t matter, and neither does whether they can ever pay you back.

So do the good turn and leave it buried. Don’t keep digging it up to show people, and don’t ask for it back. If a day comes when you really need help, you may find it was there all along.
