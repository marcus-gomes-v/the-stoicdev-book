## October 19th - REPLACE BAD HABITS WITH BETTER ONES

***“And since habit is a powerful influence, when we have accustomed ourselves to employ desire and aversion only upon these externals, we must set a contrary habit to counteract this habit, and where the very slippery nature of sense-impressions is in play, there we must set our training as a counteracting force.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.12.6

You open the laptop to start the hard ticket and, without deciding to, you’re in your email. Then the team channel. Then a thread about a framework you don’t use. Forty minutes later the hard ticket is still sitting there. Nobody chose that habit. It was trained in, one small reach for distraction at a time.

Epictetus doesn’t expect willpower to win against something that strong. He says to set an opposite habit against it and train it just as deliberately. If your reflex is to avoid the hard task, make the first move toward it so small it’s almost silly: open the file, write the failing test, read the error message out loud. If your reflex in meetings is to point out what’s wrong, practice saying what would make it work first.

Look hardest at the slippery moments, the ones where the old habit shows up before you notice it. Put the training right there. Close the chat app before you open the editor. A habit that meets another habit every day loses ground slowly, and one day you notice it’s gone.
