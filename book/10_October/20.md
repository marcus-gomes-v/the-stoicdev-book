## October 20th - LIVING THE GOOD LIFE

***“Thou hast had experience of many wanderings without having found happiness anywhere,—not in syllogisms, nor in wealth, nor in reputation, nor in enjoyment, nor anywhere. Where is it then? In doing what man’s nature requires. How then shall a man do this? If he has principles from which come his affects and his acts. What principles? Those which relate to good and bad: the belief that there is nothing good for man which does not make him just, temperate, manly, free; and that there is nothing bad which does not do the contrary to what has been mentioned.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 8.1

Look back over the last ten years and count the things that were supposed to settle it. The first senior title. The startup that was going to make everyone rich. The talk at the big conference, the side project with ten thousand stars, the salary number you once thought would be enough. Each one came, and the restlessness came back a few weeks later.

Marcus had wandered too, and he names the places he looked: clever arguments, wealth, reputation, pleasure. Happiness wasn’t in any of them. It was in doing what a human being is made to do, and that comes from having principles you actually act from.

His test for those principles is simple. Something is good only if it makes you more just, more self-controlled, braver and freer. Something is bad only if it makes you less so. Run your week through it. The launch and the praise in the all-hands barely register. The time you told a client a truth they didn’t want to hear, or held your temper when the release went sideways, counts for a lot.
