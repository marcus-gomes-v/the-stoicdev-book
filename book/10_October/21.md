## October 21st - BE A HERO IN THE PRESENT

***“How strangely men act! They will not praise those who are living at the same time and living with themselves; but to be themselves praised by posterity, by those whom they have never seen nor ever will see, this they set much value on. But this is very much the same as if thou shouldst be grieved because those who have lived before thee did not praise thee.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.18

Some developers dream of building the thing people will still talk about in twenty years. The framework with their name in the history books. The open-source project that outlives them, the conference keynote that gets quoted long after they’re gone.

Meanwhile they rarely say a good word about the people next to them. The teammate who quietly fixed the flaky test suite gets nothing. The designer who saved the launch with a late change doesn’t hear about it. The junior who asked the question everyone else was too proud to ask gets an eye roll.

Marcus found this strange, and it is. We want applause from people we will never meet, and we hold it back from people we see every day. He compares it to being upset that people born before you never praised you. You won’t be there to hear the praise of the future either.

The audience you actually have is in your standups and your review queue. Tell the person who fixed the test suite that it made your week easier. Do work your current team is glad to inherit. Whatever legacy you end up with is built from days like that, and the people in them can feel it now.
