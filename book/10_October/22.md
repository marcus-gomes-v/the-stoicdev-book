## October 22nd - IMPROVE WHO YOU ARE, NOT JUST WHAT YOU CAN DO

***“Another may be more expert in casting his opponent; but he is not more social, nor more modest, nor better disciplined to meet all that happens, nor more considerate with respect to the faults of his neighbors.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 7.52

There’s a kind of developer who wins every technical argument. They know the database internals better than its docs do, they spot the race condition in a diff at a glance, and they will make sure you know it in the review. They are very good at taking an opponent down.

Marcus would grant all of that and then ask a few more questions. Are they any better with people? Any more modest? Any steadier when the pager goes off at three in the morning and the fix doesn’t work? Any more patient with a colleague who makes the same mistake twice?

We spend years sharpening the first kind of skill. Very few of us put the same effort into the second, though it decides whether anyone wants to work with us. Winning every review while the junior developers dread your comments is a bad trade.

Keep learning the new tools. Just check now and then whether the practice is also making you calmer and kinder about other people’s bugs. If it isn’t, that’s the skill gap to work on next.
