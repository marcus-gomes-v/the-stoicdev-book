## October 25th - MASTERING THE ART OF LIVING

***“What, then, is it which makes a man free from hindrance and his own master? For wealth does not do it, nor a consulship, nor a province, nor a kingdom, but something else has to be found... So also in living, it is the knowledge of how to live.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.1.62-64

Nobody expects to write good code without learning how. You read, you practice, you get your pull requests torn apart, and over years the skill becomes yours. What makes someone free to write without fumbling is the knowledge of how to write.

Epictetus says living works the same way. We act as if a bigger title, a better salary or a manager role will finally make us free of the things that knock us around. They don’t. A staff engineer can be as anxious and as easily pushed around as an intern. The thing that frees you is knowing how to live, and that is a skill you learn and practice like any other.

Epictetus learned it as a slave, about as far from wealth and office as a person can get. So don’t wait for the right job to start. The practice happens where you are: in how you take the harsh review, how you spend the hour before the deadline, how you treat the person who broke the build. One decision at a time, the way you learned to code.
