## October 26th - THE THREE PILLARS OF LIVING WELL

***“Both the greatest and the most numerous authorities have held that philosophy has three parts: moral, natural and rational. The first brings order to the mind. The second searches into the nature of things. The third weighs the exact sense of words, how they are put together and how arguments are built, so that nothing false slips in disguised as true.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 89.9

Seneca’s three parts of philosophy fit a developer’s week better than you might expect. The moral part puts your mind in order. It decides how you behave when a dark pattern lands in your sprint, when a deadline tempts you to hide a known bug, when a colleague needs someone to back them up in a meeting.

The natural part looks at how things really are. For us that’s the habit of finding out how the system works instead of guessing. Reading the library’s source. Checking the actual query plan. Accepting that the network is unreliable and the disk will fill up, whatever the roadmap says.

The rational part cares about the exact meaning of words and whether an argument holds. Anyone who has watched a project fail because two teams meant different things by “user” or “done” knows why. So does anyone who has read a postmortem where the conclusion didn’t follow from the evidence.

You need all three. Sharp reasoning without ethics builds clever harm, and good intentions without a grip on reality ship outages. Pick the part you neglect most and give it some attention this week.
