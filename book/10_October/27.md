## October 27th - THE CONSEQUENCES OF OUR EXAMPLES

***“Often upon the teacher have his bad teachings turned.”***
—SENECA, THYESTES, 311

Seneca wrote this line for a scene in which a king plans to draw his own sons into a crime. He also lived it. He spent years as Nero’s tutor and then as his adviser, and the student he helped shape ended up ordering his death.

Teams learn from their leads the way children learn from parents: from what you do far more than from what you say. If you skip code review when you’re in a hurry, the people watching you will skip it too. If you blame another team in the incident channel, they learn that’s how incidents work here. If you pad estimates to look good, they’ll pad theirs, including the ones they give you.

Then one day it comes back. The shortcut you taught turns into the 2 a.m. outage you get paged for. The blame culture you started lands on your own mistake. The habits you hand down don’t stay pointed at other people.

So look at what you’re teaching without meaning to. Somebody is already copying it.
