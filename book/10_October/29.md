October 29th - CHARACTER OVER CREDENTIALS

“Each person acquires their own character, but their official roles are designated by chance. You should invite some to your table because they are deserving, others because they may come to deserve it.” —SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 47.15b

In tech and beyond, it’s tempting to measure people by their credentials—schools attended, previous roles held, or titles earned. But as Seneca and Heraclitus remind us, these external markers are often products of chance or privilege. True worth lies in character: integrity, perseverance, and a willingness to grow.

When hiring, collaborating, or building relationships, prioritize character over accolades. Look for the person who takes responsibility, learns from failure, and treats others with respect. Similarly, in your own career, focus on building the kind of character that earns trust and admiration over time—not just achievements on a résumé.

Today, evaluate the people you work with and your own actions through the lens of character. Credentials might open doors, but character determines what happens once you walk through them. In the long run, it’s the most valuable quality you can cultivate and recognize.
