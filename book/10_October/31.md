## October 31st - RETURN TO YOUR NATURE

***“The human soul has a natural bent toward goodness, and a seed of virtue lies in each one of us.”***
—MUSONIUS RUFUS, LECTURES, 2

There’s a cynical story told in a lot of engineering orgs. People are lazy, so you need tracking. Developers cut corners, so you need gates on everything. After enough incidents and reorgs it’s easy to believe that story about others, and quietly about yourself.

Musonius, the teacher of Epictetus, told a different one. Each of us is born with a seed of virtue, a natural pull toward being good. Ask almost anyone whether they are fair or unfair, honest or dishonest, and they will claim the good answer. We know what we’re supposed to be, even when we fall short of it.

You can see that seed on any team. The developer who stays late to help a stranger from another squad. The engineer who reports her own bug before anyone else finds it. The new hire who asks, nervously, whether a feature might hurt users. Nobody trained them to do that.

For Musonius, philosophy’s job was to grow that seed and clear away the bad habits piled on top of it. On the days the cynical story feels true, look for the seed in yourself first. Then go looking for it in other people.
