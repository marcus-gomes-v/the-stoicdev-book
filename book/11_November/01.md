November 1st - EMBRACE WHAT HAPPENS
“Don’t seek for everything to happen as you wish it would, but rather wish that everything happens as it actually will—then your life will flow well.” —EPICTETUS, ENCHIRIDION, 8
“It is easy to praise providence for anything that may happen if you have two qualities: a complete view of what has actually happened in each instance and a sense of gratitude. Without gratitude what is the point of seeing, and without seeing what is the object of gratitude?” —EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 1.6.1-2
In the world of tech, where plans are often disrupted by bugs, delays, or shifting priorities, acceptance is a powerful tool. You can’t rewrite the code of life, but you can adapt your mindset. Epictetus’s wisdom reminds us that it’s easier—and far more productive—to change your attitude about what has happened than to resist the reality of it.
But Stoicism doesn’t stop at acceptance. It pushes us to go further, to find gratitude and even joy in the unexpected twists of fate. A failed project might teach you invaluable lessons. A delayed launch might create space for reflection and improvement. Amor fati—loving what happens—frees you from disappointment and opens the door to unexpected growth.
Today, practice accepting and embracing the events of the day as they unfold. Don’t just tolerate them—appreciate them. The more you align with what is, the smoother your life will flow.
