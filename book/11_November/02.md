## November 2nd - ALIGN YOUR WILL WITH FATE

***“But I have never been hindered in the exercise of my will, nor have I ever been subjected to compulsion against my will. And how is this possible? I have submitted my freedom of choice unto God. He wills that I shall have fever; it is my will too. He wills that I should choose something; it is my will too. He wills that I should desire something; it is my will too. He wills that I should get something; it is my wish too. He does not will it; I do not wish it.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.1.89

You did the work. The design doc went through three rounds of review, the load tests passed, the rollback plan is written down. Then launch day comes and the partner API goes down for six hours, or leadership pulls the feature for reasons that have nothing to do with you.

Epictetus says he has never once been forced against his will, and his method sounds almost like cheating: he wants whatever God wants. If fever comes, he wills the fever. You don’t have to share his theology to see what he’s doing. A man who has already agreed to the outcome can’t be cornered by it.

For us that means holding two things at once. Prepare as if the result depends on you, because the preparation does. Then, when the result arrives, stop reading it as a verdict on your worth. The outage and the cancelled feature were never yours to decide.

What stays yours is how you show up the morning after.
