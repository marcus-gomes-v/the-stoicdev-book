## November 3rd - ACCEPT WHAT NATURE PRESCRIBES

***“Just as we must understand when it is said, That Aesculapius prescribed to this man horse-exercise, or bathing in cold water, or going without shoes, so we must understand it when it is said, That the nature of the universe prescribed to this man disease, or mutilation, or loss, or anything else of the kind. For in the first case Prescribed means something like this: he prescribed this for this man as a thing adapted to procure health; and in the second case it means, That which happens to [or suits] every man is fixed in a manner for him suitably to his destiny.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 5.8

When a doctor tells you to cut back on coffee and walk every day, you might grumble, but you don’t take it personally. It’s a prescription. Someone looked at your situation and decided this is what it needs.

Marcus asks you to hear your hard stretches the same way. The migration that drags on for a whole quarter. The on-call week where every alert fires. The reorg that drops you onto a team working in a language you’ve never used. Nobody sent these to punish you. They are what this part of your life called for, the way cold baths were what Aesculapius called for.

That doesn’t make them pleasant, and Marcus doesn’t pretend it does. Further on in the same passage he admits that plenty of prescriptions are disagreeable and we take them anyway, hoping for health.

So take the dose. Learn the language properly. Write the runbook you wished you’d had during that on-call week. A year from now you’ll be one of the few people who knows how that system really works.
