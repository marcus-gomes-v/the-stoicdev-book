## November 6th - LIFE SPINS ON A WHIM

***“Whom the rising sun hath seen high in pride, him the setting sun hath seen laid low. Let none be over-confident when fortune smiles; let none despair of better things when fortune fails. Clotho blends weal and woe, lets no lot stand, keeps ever fate a-turning. No one has found the gods so kind that he may promise to-morrow to himself. God keeps all mortal things in swift whirl turning.”***
—SENECA, THYESTES, 613

In January the startup closes its round and everyone gets a bigger title. By September half the floor is empty. Somewhere in between, a junior developer who was told she wasn’t ready ships the fix that keeps the biggest customer, and a year later she’s leading the team.

Seneca gave these lines to the chorus of a tragedy, and they read like the history of most companies. Whoever the morning saw on top, the evening saw brought low. Nobody gets a promise about tomorrow.

The useful part is that it cuts both ways. When things are going well, hold the success loosely. It was never as permanent as the all-hands slides made it look. When things are going badly, don’t write the ending yet. The wheel that turned against you is still turning.

Keep the same head in both.
