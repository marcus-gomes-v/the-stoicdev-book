## November 10th - THE ETERNAL CYCLE

***“Consider, for example, the times of Vespasian. Thou wilt see all these things, people marrying, bringing up children, sick, dying, warring, feasting, trafficking, cultivating the ground, flattering, obstinately arrogant, suspecting, plotting, wishing for some to die, grumbling about the present, loving, heaping up treasure, desiring consulship, kingly power. Well, then, that life of these people no longer exists at all. Again, remove to the times of Trajan. Again, all is the same.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 4.32

Read the Vespasian passage again and swap a few words. People hiring, shipping, getting sick, burning out, flattering the VP, plotting for the promotion, grumbling about the roadmap, falling in love with a new framework. Go back to the mainframe shops of the seventies and you get the same list. Go back to Marcus and it’s the same list again.

Our tools are new. The people aren’t. The engineer who hoards knowledge so he can’t be replaced, the manager who takes the credit, the team that argues for a month over a naming convention: every one of them has an ancestor in Rome.

There’s something steadying in that. The problem in front of you feels unique because it’s yours, but plenty of people have been through its shape before. They got through it, mostly, and then they were forgotten, which is also what will happen to the reorg you’re losing sleep over.

Give your attention to what deserves it. Most of the drama won’t outlive the quarter.
