## November 16th - LIVE BEYOND HOPE AND FEAR

***“I found this in our Hecato: ‘You will stop fearing if you stop hoping.’ ... The main cause of both is that we do not fit ourselves to the present, but send our thoughts on ahead to things far off.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 5.7-8

It’s the week before your performance review. Half the time you’re imagining the raise, the new title, the message you’ll send home. The other half you’re imagining the meeting where they tell you it isn’t happening. You swing between the two and get very little done.

Seneca borrows a line from the Stoic Hecato: stop hoping and you’ll stop fearing. It sounds bleak until you read on. In the same letter he compares hope and fear to a prisoner and the soldier chained to him. They walk together. Both belong to a mind hanging on the future, and both come from the habit of sending your thoughts far ahead of where you actually are.

Bring them back. The review is on Thursday, and nothing you picture before Thursday will change what gets said. What you can change is the work you ship between now and then, and how clearly you can describe the work you’ve already done.

Write that summary this afternoon. Then go back to the ticket in front of you.
