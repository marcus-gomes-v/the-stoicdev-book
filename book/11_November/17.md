## November 17th - FOCUS ON YOUR OWN IMPROVEMENT

***“Handled with insolence and defiance, philosophy has brought danger on many people. Let it take your own faults away from you, not throw other people’s faults in their faces.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 103.4-5

You’ve just finished a great book on clean architecture and suddenly every pull request on the team looks like a crime scene. You leave long comments. You quote chapter and verse. You sigh in standups. A month later people have quietly stopped asking you for reviews, and they have a point.

Seneca watched the same thing happen with philosophy. Used with insolence, he says, it has put many people in danger. It is there to take away your own faults, not to give you a sharper vocabulary for everyone else’s.

Any good idea can turn into a weapon this way, Stoicism included. The tell is where your attention goes. If you’re learning, you notice your own sloppy commits first. If you’re showing off, you only notice other people’s.

So use the book on your own code for a while. Fix what you find. People will see the difference without a lecture, and a few of them will ask what changed.
