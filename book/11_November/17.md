November 17th - FOCUS ON YOUR OWN IMPROVEMENT

“When philosophy is wielded with arrogance and stubbornly, it is the cause for the ruin of many. Let philosophy scrape off your own faults, rather than be a way to rail against the faults of others.” —SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 103.4b-5a

Philosophy’s true purpose is self-improvement, not self-righteousness. It’s tempting to point out the flaws in others—whether it’s a teammate who misses a deadline or a leader who lacks vision—but Stoicism reminds us to turn that critical eye inward. Your faults, your habits, your decisions—these are the only things within your control.

In tech, where collaboration is key, judging others harshly creates division, not progress. Instead of focusing on what someone else did wrong, ask how you can better support the team or set an example. Use philosophy not as a weapon to critique others, but as a tool to refine yourself.

Today, resist the urge to judge. When others falter, respond with understanding, not criticism. Direct your energy toward improving your own actions and character. In doing so, you’ll not only grow but also inspire those around you to do the same.
