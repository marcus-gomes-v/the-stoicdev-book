## November 21st - A MOMENT OF WISDOM LASTS FOREVER

***“The continuance of time increases not any good thing; but if a man shall be wise but a minute of an hour, he will not be any way inferior in happiness to him who has all his time practised virtue and led his life happily in it.”***
—CHRYSIPPUS QUOTED BY PLUTARCH IN MORALIA: “AGAINST THE STOICS ON COMMON CONCEPTIONS,” 1062

Plutarch quoted this Stoic idea in order to make fun of it. How could one minute of wisdom be worth as much as a lifetime of it? It does sound strange. Then think about the moments in your own work that changed how you see things.

The afternoon a senior colleague sat with you and explained, slowly, why the bug wasn’t where you were looking. The review where someone pointed out, kindly, that you were optimizing the wrong thing. The day you finally said no to a project you knew was wrong. Each took minutes. You still carry them.

The Stoics didn’t measure goodness like uptime. A clear, honest decision is complete when you make it, and keeping it up for years doesn’t make that moment any more real.

That takes some pressure off. You don’t need a long, unbroken record of good judgment. You need to act well in the situation in front of you, and then do it again the next time.
