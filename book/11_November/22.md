## November 22nd - EXPECT THE BREAKAGE

***“They have ordered me always to stand as it were on guard, and to mark the attacks and charges of Fortune long before she delivers them; she is only terrible to those whom she catches unawares; he who is always looking out for her assault, easily sustains it.”***
—SENECA, ON CONSOLATION TO HELVIA, 5.3

Seneca wrote this to his mother from exile on Corsica, after the emperor Claudius banished him from Rome. He tells her he isn’t miserable, because the philosophers he leaned on taught him to keep watch for Fortune’s attacks long before they land. She only frightens the people she catches off guard.

Engineers know this in theory. It’s why we have backups, failover and runbooks. The teams hit hardest by an outage are rarely the ones with the worst systems. They’re the ones who never expected the system to fail, so they had no plan for when it did.

The same goes for what isn’t code: the job, the team you love, the project that’s finally going well. Enjoy them fully. Just keep a quiet corner of your mind aware that they can change, and think now and then about what you’d do if they did.

When the break comes, you’ll recognize it. You’ll already know the first step.
