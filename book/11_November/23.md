## November 23rd - FREEDOM FROM ATTACHMENTS

***“In a word, then, remember this—that if you are going to honour anything at all outside the sphere of the moral purpose, you have destroyed your moral purpose.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 4.4.23

Epictetus says this in a discourse about people who crave peace and quiet, and his point is sharp. Ambition isn’t the only thing that can own you. The wish for leisure can do it too. Anything outside your own choices, once you prize it enough, starts making the choices for you.

You see it in developers attached to a tool. The framework they know best becomes the answer to every design question, and any proposal to replace it feels like an insult. You see it in the senior engineer so attached to being the expert that she can’t say “I don’t know” in a meeting. And in the person so attached to a quiet, meeting-free week that one interruption ruins the whole day.

None of these things is bad to have. The trouble starts when you can’t be all right without them.

Hold them loosely enough that you could put them down. Then you’re the one deciding what to do next, and not the thing you were holding.
