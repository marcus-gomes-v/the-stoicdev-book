## November 24th - PREPARE TO LET GO

***“Whenever you grow attached to something, do not act as though it were one of those things that cannot be taken away, but as though it were something like a jar or a crystal goblet, so that when it breaks you will remember what it was like, and not be troubled. So too in life; if you kiss your child, your brother, your friend, never allow your fancy free rein, nor your exuberant spirits to go as far as they like, but hold them back, stop them, just like those who stand behind generals when they ride in triumph, and keep reminding them that they are mortal. In such fashion do you too remind yourself that the object of your love is mortal; it is not one of your own possessions; it has been given you for the present, not inseparably nor for ever...”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.24.84-86

Epictetus picks the most ordinary moment he can find: kissing your child. Even then, he says, keep a small reminder in the back of your mind, like the man who stood behind a Roman general during his triumph and kept telling him he was mortal. What you love has been given to you for the present, not for ever.

It sounds cold, but it works the other way. People who never think about loss tend to treat what they have like furniture. People who remember it can end are the ones who pay attention.

Work has smaller versions of this. The teammate you learn the most from will take another job one day. The product you spent four years on will be shut down by someone who never used it. If you expect that, you spend more time with that teammate now. You write down what made the product good while you still remember.

Treat it like the crystal goblet. Use it, enjoy it, and know it can break.
