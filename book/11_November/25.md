## November 25th - BLESSINGS IN DISGUISE

***“For my part, I would sooner be ill than live in luxury. Illness harms only the body, while luxury corrupts both, soul and body alike, putting weakness and powerlessness into the body and indiscipline and cowardice into the soul. And luxury gives birth to injustice as well, because it gives birth to greed.”***
—MUSONIUS RUFUS, LECTURES, 20

Nero exiled Musonius to Gyaros, a bare rock of an island in the Aegean, so when he says he would rather be sick than live in luxury, he isn’t speaking from a comfortable villa. He knew hardship well, and he still thought comfort was the more dangerous of the two.

His argument is short. Illness harms the body. Luxury harms body and soul: it makes you soft, it weakens your self-control, and in the end it makes you greedy, because there is never quite enough of it.

We don’t talk about luxury at work, but it shows up. The team with an enormous budget that stopped asking whether anything was worth building. The engineer whose stock vested and who now waves pull requests through. The company that raised too much money and lost the discipline that got it there.

Hard stretches cost something too, but they rarely make you careless. A lean quarter teaches you what matters. A generous one can quietly teach you that nothing does.
