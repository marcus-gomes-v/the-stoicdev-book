## December 2nd - LIVE LIKE YOU KNOW YOU’RE DYING

***“Since it is possible that thou mayest depart from life this very moment, regulate every act and thought accordingly.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 2.11

A friend from your first job dies at forty, and you find out from a post on LinkedIn. For a few weeks everything gets clearer. You leave on time. You call your parents. The argument in the team channel about naming conventions looks like what it is.

Then it fades, and the old habits come back.

Marcus wrote this line to himself, a man who ruled through a plague and buried many of his children. He didn’t ask for drama. He asked that the knowledge stay switched on, quietly, behind every act and thought. Not as a fear. More like a setting you never turn off.

Try it on something small. The comment you’re about to leave on a junior’s pull request. The hour you’re about to spend on a refactor nobody needs. If this were the last thing you did, would you do it this way? Often the answer is yes. When it isn’t, you’ll know what to change.
