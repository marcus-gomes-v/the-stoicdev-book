## December 3rd - LIFE IS YOUR MASTERPIECE

***“Philosophy does not profess to secure for man any external possession. Otherwise it would be undertaking something that lies outside its proper subject-matter. For as wood is the material of the carpenter, bronze that of the statuary, just so each man’s own life is the subject-matter of the art of living.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 1.15.2

Philosophy, Epictetus says, won’t get you anything outside yourself. It won’t get you the promotion or fix your manager. It won’t make the team adopt the architecture you proposed. Those belong to other people’s lives.

The man in this chapter had come to ask how to stop his brother being angry with him. Epictetus told him, in effect, that the brother’s anger was the brother’s material. What he could work on was how he himself behaved.

You already know what working a material looks like. You’ve watched a messy service turn, over months of small commits, into something people trust. Nobody did it in one sprint. Somebody kept showing up and making each change a little better than the last.

A life gets shaped the same way. The way you answer a hostile code review. Whether you admit the bug was yours, or how you treat the intern when you’re tired and behind. None of it is dramatic. But it’s the wood you were given, and nobody else gets to carve it.
