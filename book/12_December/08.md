## December 8th - FACE YOUR FEELINGS, DON’T SUPPRESS THEM

***“It is better, therefore, to conquer it than to cheat it; for a grief which has been deceived and driven away either by pleasure or by business rises again, and its period of rest does but give it strength for a more terrible attack; but a grief which has been conquered by reason is appeased for ever.”***
—SENECA, ON CONSOLATION TO HELVIA, 17.1

Seneca wrote this to his mother from exile on Corsica. She was grieving for him, and he doesn’t tell her to cheer up. He tells her that grief you trick into silence comes back stronger.

Developers are very good at the trick he describes. Something hurts, so we work. The layoff that took half your team. The project you gave a year to and watched get canceled in a five-minute call. You answer it with longer hours, a new side project, one more ticket before bed. Business, Seneca calls it. It works for a week or two.

Then it shows up somewhere else. You snap at a teammate over a variable name. You can’t focus on anything that isn’t on fire.

What he recommends is slower and less comfortable. Look at the thing. Name what was lost, and talk to someone about it out loud, without turning it into a joke. Reason won’t make the grief vanish overnight, but it’s the only thing that settles it for good.
