## December 9th - PROTECT YOUR MOST PRECIOUS ASSET

***“Were all the brightest intellects of all time to employ themselves on this one subject, they never could sufficiently express their wonder at this blindness of men’s minds: men will not allow any one to establish himself upon their estates, and upon the most trifling dispute about the measuring of boundaries, they betake themselves to stones and cudgels: yet they allow others to encroach upon their lives, nay, they themselves actually lead others in to take possession of them. You cannot find any one who wants to distribute his money; yet among how many people does every one distribute his life? men covetously guard their property from waste, but when it comes to waste of time, they are most prodigal of that of which it would become them to be sparing.”***
—SENECA, ON THE BREVITY OF LIFE, 3.1-2

Ask a developer to lend you their mechanical keyboard for a month, or a few hundred dollars, and watch their face. Now look at their calendar. A recurring sync nobody remembers creating. A “quick call” that ate the whole afternoon. Four Slack threads they joined because someone tagged them and ignoring it felt rude.

That’s the blindness Seneca couldn’t stop marveling at. People will fight over a strip of land along a fence and then hand their days to whoever asks first. Worse, he says, they hold the door open.

You don’t have to become difficult to fix this. Decline the meeting that has no agenda and ask for one. Block two hours in the morning and guard them like a deploy window. When someone writes “got a minute?” at ten, it’s fine to answer at four.

Be as stingy with your hours as you are with your money. The money, at least, you can earn back.
