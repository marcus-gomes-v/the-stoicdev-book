## December 11th - TRUE BRAVERY NEEDS NO SECRETS

***“‘We dislike gladiators,’ says Cicero, ‘if they are eager to save their lives by any means whatever: but we look favourably upon them if they are openly reckless of them.’”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 11.4

The crowd in the arena, Cicero noticed, had no patience for a fighter who would do anything to stay alive. The one who fought without bargaining for his life was the one they wanted to see walk out.

Offices work the same way, minus the sand. After an outage, someone always starts saving themselves by any means. They point at the config change somebody else merged. They quietly force-push over the commit that shows what happened. They go vague in the postmortem. It works for about a day, and then everyone knows.

Everyone also knows the other kind. The engineer who writes in the incident channel, “That was my deploy, rolling back now.” The lead who tells the director the estimate was wrong, and why, before the director finds out alone.

Neither of them is reckless. They just stopped treating their own reputation as the thing to protect at any cost. Oddly enough, that’s how they end up with one worth protecting.
