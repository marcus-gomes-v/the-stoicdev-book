## December 13th - MAKE YOUR YEARS COUNT

***“Thou art not dissatisfied, I suppose, because thou weighest only so many litrae and not three hundred. Be not dissatisfied then that thou must live only so many years and not more; for as thou art satisfied with the amount of substance which has been assigned to thee, so be content with the time.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 6.49

Nobody lies awake angry that they don’t weigh three hundred pounds. Marcus picks the example because it sounds silly, and that’s the point. You accept the body you were given without a grievance. Why treat your years any differently?

Developers carry a quieter version of the complaint. You’re thirty-eight and still not staff. You started coding late, after another career, and you keep counting the years you think you lost. Someone from your class founded a company at twenty-four and you read about it on the train.

But the amount was never the part you controlled. What you control is the next year, and a year is plenty. Enough to get properly good at one hard thing, to become the person juniors come to with questions, to finish the project you keep restarting.

Someone else’s timeline will always look longer from where you stand. Measuring yours against it is the quickest way to make it feel short.
