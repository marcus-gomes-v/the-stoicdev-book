## December 16th - TRUE HEALTH IS IN YOUR CONTROL

***“I tell you, you have only to learn the life of healthy men... If this is what you want, you will have it everywhere, and will live with full confidence. Confidence in what? In the only thing in which one can have confidence—in what is faithful, free from hindrance, cannot be taken away, that is, in your own moral purpose.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.26.23-24

The chapter this comes from is about a man who is afraid of being poor. Epictetus teases him: what you really fear is losing the life of an invalid, with attendants to massage you and servants to clear the table. Learn how healthy people live, he says, and he points to Cleanthes, who studied philosophy and drew water to pay for it.

There’s a developer version of that invalid. The one who can’t picture working without the free lunches, the top-spec laptop and the salary band of a company that may announce layoffs next quarter. Losing any of it would feel like losing the floor.

Healthy, in Epictetus’s sense, means you could do good work on an old machine in a small office and still be yourself. Your confidence doesn’t rest on the perks or the logo on your badge. It rests on the one thing nobody can lay off: your own choices, how you judge and how you act.

Enjoy the perks while they last. Just don’t lean on them.
