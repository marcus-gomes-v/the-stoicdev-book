## December 20th - FEAR OF DEATH IS THE TRUE BURDEN

***“Will you, then, realize that this epitome of all the ills that befall man, of his ignoble spirit, and his cowardice, is not death, but it is rather the fear of death? Against this fear, then, I would have you discipline yourself, toward this let all your reasoning tend, your exercises, your reading; and then you will know that this is the only way in which men achieve freedom.”***
—EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.26.38-39

Just before this, Epictetus walks through a student’s worries one at a time. What if I fall ill? Who will nurse me? What if I end up on a hard bed in a poor house? He answers each one calmly until they reach the end of the line, which is death. And there he says the real trouble was never death. It was the fear of it, sitting underneath every smaller fear.

You can find the same structure at work. You don’t ask the question in the architecture review because you might look junior. You don’t ship the side project because people might ignore it. You don’t push back on the deadline because it might cost you. Follow any of those far enough down and it ends in the same place, a fear of being made smaller.

His advice is to train against the fear directly, the way you’d train any skill. Point your reading at it. Practice on small things. Ask the question. Publish the half-finished post. Each time, the feared thing either doesn’t happen or turns out to be survivable, and the grip loosens a little.

That loosening is what he means by freedom.
