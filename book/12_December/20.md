December 20th - FEAR OF DEATH IS THE TRUE BURDEN
“Do you then ponder how the supreme of human evils, the surest mark of the base and cowardly, is not death, but the fear of death? I urge you to discipline yourself against such fear, direct all your thinking, exercises, and reading this way—and you will know the only path to human freedom.” —EPICTETUS, DISCOURSES, 3.26.38-39
Death itself is not the problem—it’s the fear of it that consumes us. Fear of the unknown, fear of what might happen, fear of what we might leave undone. But as the Stoics remind us, this fear is irrational. If death is the end, there is nothing left to fear. If it is not the end, then there is still no reason to fear.
In tech, as in life, fear of failure often paralyzes us more than failure itself. We hesitate to take risks, to launch bold ideas, or to share our honest thoughts because of what might happen. But just as worrying about death is futile, so too is worrying about failure before it arrives. Both fears waste time and sap our energy for what we can control: our present actions.
Today, confront your fear—whether it’s fear of death, failure, or the unknown. Realize that fearing something doesn’t prevent it; it only robs you of peace now. Direct your energy toward living well and acting boldly. In doing so, you’ll find freedom from the hold of fear.
