## December 21st - MEASURE YOUR LIFE BY HOW YOU LIVE IT

***“Often a man who is very old in years has nothing beyond his age by which he can prove that he has lived a long time.”***
—SENECA, ON TRANQUILITY OF MIND, 3.8

There’s an old line in hiring: some people have ten years of experience, and some have one year of experience ten times. Seneca would have liked it. The version he quotes here, from the Stoic Athenodorus, is harsher: plenty of old men have nothing but their age to prove they lived long.

Years are easy to count, and our industry counts them everywhere. Job posts ask for seven years of this and five of that. Your CV adds them up for you. But you know developers with fifteen years who still reach for the patterns from their first job, and others with four who have thought hard about every system they’ve touched.

Talent explains less of the gap than you’d think. Mostly it comes down to whether the years were spent paying attention: whether you asked why the outage happened or just restarted the service, whether you read the code you depended on or only its README.

Look back over this year while it’s still December. Not at how long it was, but at what you’d have to show for it if nobody were counting.
