## December 22nd - MAKE YOUR OWN MARK

***“For it is shameful for an old man, or one who can already see old age ahead, to be wise only out of a notebook. ‘Zeno said this.’ And you? ‘Cleanthes said this.’ And you? How long will you march under someone else’s command? Give the orders yourself: say something that deserves to be handed down, and bring out something of your own.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 33.7

You hear it in every design review. “Google does it this way.” “The book says functions should be short.” “There’s a famous post about why we shouldn’t.” Quoting is fine while you’re learning. Seneca says as much: we give children maxims to memorize because a young mind can hold a short saying and not much more.

But at some point quoting turns into a hiding place. Past a certain level, nobody needs to hear what the famous engineer said. They want to know what you think, given this system, this team and this deadline.

Answering is uncomfortable. You might be wrong in public, with your name on the decision record. That risk is the price of having a view worth writing down.

So the next time you reach for someone else’s authority, try the harder sentence first: “Here’s what I’ve seen, and here’s what I’d do.” Then quote the book if it still helps.
