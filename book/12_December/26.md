## December 26th - MAKE LIFE COUNT

***“We do not have a very short time assigned to us, but we lose a great deal of it: life is long enough to carry out the most important projects: we have an ample portion, if we do but arrange the whole of it aright: but when it all runs to waste through luxury and carelessness, when it is not devoted to any good purpose, then at the last we are forced to feel that it is all over, although we never noticed how it glided away. Thus it is: we do not receive a short life, but we make it a short one, and we are not poor in days, but wasteful of them.”***
—SENECA, ON THE BREVITY OF LIFE, 1.3-4

Open the screen time report on your phone, if you’re feeling brave. Then think of the side project you haven’t touched since March because you “don’t have time.”

Seneca’s claim is that life isn’t short, we make it short. The time is there. It leaks out through small holes: an hour of feeds, an afternoon in a meeting you didn’t need to attend, a weekend lost to work nobody asked for and nobody will read. None of it feels like a choice while it’s happening. That’s why, as he says, we only notice the time once it has gone.

Optimizing every minute would miss his point, since a life spent tracking your own productivity is its own kind of waste. What he wants is direction, for your hours to go somewhere you’d choose if you were paying attention.

The last week of December is a natural moment to look at where this year went. Not to punish yourself. Just to see the holes clearly, so that next year you can patch a few of them.
