## December 29th - BEGIN AND END WITH GRATITUDE

***“We should do everything we can to be as grateful as possible. For this good is our own, in the same way that justice is not, as people commonly believe, a matter that concerns only others: a large part of it flows back to its source.”***
—SENECA, MORAL LETTERS, 81.19

Seneca makes a claim that sounds backwards at first. Gratitude, he says, is mostly good for the grateful person. We think of it as something we owe others, like a debt, the same way people assume justice is all about other people. But much of it comes back to the one who feels it.

Try it on an ordinary workday. Someone spends twenty minutes on your pull request and catches the race condition you missed. You can merge and move on, or you can write one line saying it was a good catch and it saved you a bad week. The second takes thirty seconds. It changes how they see review work, and it changes you as well, because you start noticing how much of your work quietly rests on other people’s.

That noticing is the return Seneca means. A developer who keeps track of what others have given them is much harder to make bitter.

Before the year closes, write a few of those lines. Send them to the people who earned them, even if it was months ago.
