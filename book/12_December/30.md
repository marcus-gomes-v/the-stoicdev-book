## December 30th - FIND CALM IN THE CHAOS

***“For he who bears his ills with even soul has robbed misfortune of its strength and heaviness.”***
—SENECA, HERCULES OETAEUS, 231-232

In the play, the chorus says this to Iole, a princess just taken captive after her city fell and her father was killed. Not an easy moment for advice. Their point is that the same blow lands differently on different minds. Meet it with an even soul and it loses both its strength and its weight.

You see this during incidents. Two engineers get paged for the same outage. One spends the first ten minutes typing in capital letters and refreshing dashboards. The other reads the alert twice, says what she knows and what she doesn’t, and starts with the most likely cause. The outage is the same size for both of them. It weighs a lot less on the second.

That evenness is built beforehand, in quieter hours: runbooks written, failures imagined in advance, the habit of slowing down when everything says hurry.

When your own bad night comes, the calm you practiced will be what’s there to hold on to.
