## December 31st - ACT ON WHAT YOU’VE LEARNED

***“No longer wander at hazard; for neither wilt thou read thy own memoirs, nor the acts of the ancient Romans and Hellenes, and the selections from books which thou wast reserving for thy old age. Hasten then to the end which thou hast before thee, and, throwing away idle hopes, come to thy own aid, if thou carest at all for thyself, while it is in thy power.”***
—MARCUS AURELIUS, MEDITATIONS, 3.14

Every developer has the folder. Bookmarked articles, a course bought on sale, a dozen repos starred for later, a notes app full of ideas for a talk. Marcus had his own version: his notebooks, the histories of Rome and Greece, the extracts he was saving for his old age. He tells himself plainly that he will never read them.

He had a real thirst for books, as he admits elsewhere. What worried him was the moment when collecting starts to replace living. You can spend years getting ready to be the engineer you want to be, and there will always be one more book to finish first.

If you’ve read a page of this book each morning, you’ve had a year of Stoic ideas. That’s plenty of material. What’s left is Marcus’s question to himself: what will you do with it, while it’s still in your power?

Pick one idea that stuck and live it next week. Come to your own aid. The notebooks can wait.
